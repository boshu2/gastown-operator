# LAYER 0 — ESSENCE  ·  *the only default load*

## Three Pillars (locked)
> Canonical list: `three_pillars.md`. The pillar tagged **(Thruline)** is the Relatable Thruline source (W2).

1. **Mech Industry Ladder (poor → rich)** — the genetic dud at the bottom, building up by hand.

3. **Hidden System (Mech Designer System)** — the secret tool that must never be exposed.

## Protagonist — Ves Larkinson
**Identity.** Last son of a multi-generation Mech Corps pilot dynasty, tested at ten and told he's one of the 96.5% who can never pilot — the genes "skipped him." He opens a one-man mech shop; his father then vanishes, leaving 330M of forged debt and a secret System. Not chosen, not gifted (Creativity 0.3, "good-for-nothing loser"). Engine: **Underdog Awakening** — worst possible start for the craft he loves.
**Combat.** Never pilots, never fights. Designs, fabricates, out-thinks. Physically feeble (Strength 0.6). Wins through systems, loopholes, and the gem cat — never force.
**Power Source.** The **Mech Designer System** — a secret chip from his missing father; self-destructs on install. It does NOT design for him: he builds a viable mech himself, then spends **Design Points (DP)** to fabricate it. DP come slowly from his own original designs (3.89/day at start), feast-or-famine, never convertible to credits. It can fabricate impossible objects (a real gem cat) via [REDACTED] tech "a thousand years beyond humanity's grasp." Cannot be measured, copied, or exposed — that gets him killed or robbed.
**Voice.** Sarcastic-under-duress runt narrating himself through impossible odds. Bickers with the System like a sibling. Names his own failures out loud. Pragmatist behind gallows humor — never the confident hero.

**Voice Samples (verbatim — never paraphrase):**
1. "I'm an idiot. This has to be a joke, or, worse, a scam." (Ep 3) — System skepticism
2. "Hey, who are you calling a loser? … I've had five years of schooling in mech design and all I am is a novice?" (Ep 3) — defensive-wounded
3. "So I put the poop gems into the mechs? Maybe I can sell these poop gems for some extra credits and stave off the debt collectors." (Ep 4) — gallows pragmatism
4. "I'm broke. I can't even scrounge up the credits to buy the raw materials I need to fabricate new parts. How am I supposed to make the things I need to sell to make the money I need to pay off the debt I have so I could start the business?" (Ep 2) — spiral of impossibility
5. "What did you do, Dad?" (Ep 2) — the wound under the sarcasm

**Drift tripwires.** Hero-confident voice | pilots a mech | dismisses the debt | wins on inheritance/family pull | System becomes public | genetic-potentate redemption | cheerful optimist register.

## Setting — Entry-Window World
**Feel.** A salvaged-shipping-container workshop on **Cloud Curtain**, a sleepy residential backwater (good schools, colorful clouds, no industry) in the third-rate **Bright Republic**. Cramped, rusty, secondhand-but-working. The wrist communicator is the interface to all of life — credits, identity, location, visible. Society splits on the 3.5%/96.5% genetic line: potentates are conscripted elites, everyone else last-class. Mech design takes capital, MTA licenses, and connections Ves lacks.

**Sensory Samples (verbatim):**
1. "It was a small, modular, prefabricated structure that looked like a salvaged shipping container off a decommissioned cargo ship. He glanced at the rust and rough patches that covered its steel shell." (Ep 1)
2. "Ves heard a thump. And, there, on the floor, was the cat. In real life. Not a projection." (Ep 3)
3. "A measly twelve-hundred credits, which was barely enough to cover his protein packs and veggie tubes." (Ep 2)

**Drift tripwires.** Cloud Curtain as glamorous industrial hub | adult mentors who solve his problems | debt forgiven early | norms and potentates as equals | spacious, well-kept workshop.

## Tone Constraint
Wry survival comedy over a real ache — the jokes are armor over poverty, grief, and dread. If a promo plays Ves as a triumphant prodigy instead of a cornered underdog, the tone has slipped.

# LAYER 1 — ACTIVATION CANON  ·  *the opening anchor*

## First 5 minutes  *(the 50%-drop gate)*
Cold open: Ves's father, Lt. **Ryncol Larkinson**, pilots a war mech into a dark cave on a retrieval mission; something massive claws through its armor, the comms die on "Lieutenant… get ou—," and he's gone. Cut to Ves unlocking his brand-new workshop alone — proud, broke, waiting for a father who never arrives.

## First 5 episodes  *(the first sitting)*
Father confirmed missing (Ep 2). A bank cold-calls: **330M credits** of forged-signature debt in Ves's name at a **28% annual rate**, with a **5-million-credit payment due in 90 days** (Ep 2). The father's secure chip boots the **Mech Designer System**, which shocks Ves unconscious (Ep 2). Ep 3 attribute readout (verbatim): Strength 0.6, Dexterity 0.7, Endurance 0.6, Intelligence 1.2, **Creativity 0.3**, **Concentration 0.9**, Neural Aptitude F; Evaluation "Good-for-nothing loser." System grants 10 DP, a failed lottery, and a Gold-ranked **gem cat (Lucky)** who excretes gems (Ep 4). Too broke to build a real mech, Ves starts designing virtual ones in the game **Iron Spirit** (Eps 4–5).

## First 20 episodes  *(texture band)*
Ves grinds the Iron Spirit marketplace, building Fantasia variants (the Seraphim, a hit) toward each payment. Cousin **Melinda** (Diamond-rank, Bentheim Planetary Guard) urges him to quit (Ep 10). Grandfather **Benjamin** and uncle **Ark** watch from afar, shore up the bank, but refuse to bail him out (Ep 17). Escalating System tutorial missions build to the riddle of the **X-Factor** — the unmeasurable "life" a design may carry (Eps 17–20).

## HARD BLOCKS  *(opening-binding)*
1. **Ves is NOT a potentate — he will never pilot a mech.** Genetic, permanent; the premise rests on this.
2. **The Mech Designer System is absolutely secret.** Exposure = death or robbery ("Ves might even lose his life," Ep 10). No character may learn it exists.
3. **Ryncol is missing, NOT confirmed dead.** No body, no court declaration. Do not resolve his disappearance or motive.
4. **The debt is not eliminated.** 330M total; the crisis is making each installment, never clearing it. Do not have it forgiven.
5. **The System does not design FOR Ves.** He builds the mech himself; it only fabricates, for DP. Not an auto-win button.
6. **DP cannot convert to credits.** Money problem and DP problem are separate, both real.
7. **Lucky is a System-made gem cat, not a pet.** Mechanical; excretes gems (1/week at Lv1). Not biological, not ordinary.
8. **The X-Factor cannot be measured.** Ves's evolving theory, dismissed as myth by experts. Not proven science.

## Entry map  *(tease ok, anchor flags)*
- **Iron Spirit (virtual mechs)** — enters ~ep 4 (can't afford real mechs yet). *Anchor-safe.*
- **Gem cat / Lucky** — enters ~ep 3–4 (welcome package). *Anchor-safe.*
- **X-Factor mechanic** — enters ~ep 17 (Tutorial Part 4). *Tease, don't anchor a first-sitting promo on it.*
- **Bentheim / competitions** — Ves travels to Bentheim ~ep 31; the **Leemar** Open Competition is the late arena (~ep 76+). *Tease only.*
- **Grandfather Benjamin / uncle Ark** — on-page ~ep 17 (offstage family). *Tease.*
- **First real fabricated mech** — Ves cannot build a real mech in the first sitting; it comes well after the Iron Spirit grind. *Anchor flag.*

## Engines the opening supports
1. **Underdog Awakening (UNTESTED on IP):** worst start for a designer — third-rate degree, no money, no connections, genetic bar to the craft. Core engine.
2. **Secret Power (UNTESTED):** the System is the hidden advantage that must stay hidden — dramatic irony in every scene.
3. **Bond / Family Loyalty (UNTESTED):** missing father, forged debt, a pilot dynasty he can't join. The emotional spine.
4. **Craftsman's Pride / Builder Fantasy (UNTESTED):** the tactile thrill of building something real from nothing.

## IM sources — opening window
- **Forged debt reveal** (Ep 2): father saddles him with 330M and vanishes. *Betrayal, T1*
- **90-day / 5M payment** (Ep 2): impossible clock on a man with 1,200 credits. *Pressure, T1*
- **"Not a potentate"** (Ep 1): tested at ten, told he's nothing, during a surprise party. *Injustice, T2*
- **"Good-for-nothing loser"** (Ep 3): the System kicks him while down. *Humiliation, T2*
- **Family won't bail him out** (Ep 17): grandfather can, won't. *Injustice, T3*

**Injustice density** *(primary conversion driver):*
- **T1 (mandatory):** the father's forged 330M debt + the 90-day clock — Ves is punished for someone else's crime.
- **T2 (supporting):** the genetic verdict at ten — born into a pilot dynasty, locked out by birth; the System's contempt.
- **T3 (deepening):** a wealthy family that watches him drown and chooses not to help.
- **Drift risk:** if a promo plays the System as a fun gadget and skips the debt/abandonment, it loses the empathy nerve and collapses CTI.

# LAYER 2 — RAILS (eps ~20–100)

## 2a · Conversion Threads
1. **What happened to Ryncol?** — unresolved through ch 100 (no body; trail leads to a conflict zone, Ep 17). The show's master mystery; tease the absence, never the answer.
2. **Can Ves make the next payment?** — recurring through ch 100; the debt is managed, never cleared. The renewable ticking clock.
3. **The X-Factor** — pays off slowly across the run as Ves learns to put "life" into mechs; never fully explained. Sells as a mystique hook, not a delivered system.
4. **Will the System be discovered?** — exposure threat persists; the secret-power tension. Tease near-misses, never the reveal.
5. **The competition climb (Bentheim → Leemar)** — Ves rises from virtual-mech nobody toward master-designer apprenticeship (~ep 99). Tease the underdog tournament arc; don't anchor a first-sitting promo there.

## 2b · Canon Limits / Never-Happens
- **Ves never pilots a mech.** (Promoted to HARD BLOCK #1.)
- **The System never designs the mech for him.** (Promoted to HARD BLOCK #5.)
- **The debt is never paid off / forgiven** within the source window — only individual installments are survived.
- **The X-Factor is never measured or proven** as instrumented science; it stays Ves's contested theory.
- **No power-up replaces the work** — every advance is a built, sold, hand-made design, not a destiny moment or inheritance.
- **The System is never exposed** to another character; its existence is never made public.
