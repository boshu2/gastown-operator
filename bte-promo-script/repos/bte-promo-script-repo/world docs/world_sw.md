## Protagonist — Ethan Gremory
**Identity.** A farm-raised 17-year-old (ch 7), dark-blue hair, blue eyes. As a newborn his birth-mother destroyed his magic circuits with "Ars Vim Extermina" (ch 1) — he physically *cannot* use magic. He boards the wrong train by accident and is classified "Dud" only because a stranger's bracelet faked a spark. A powerless ordinary boy trapped among people who can each kill him with one spell. Fits Underdog Awakening + Secret Identity.
**Combat.** Farm-trained pitchfork fighting (positioning, attack-pattern reading, endurance), taught by his grandmother against wild animals. No magic of his own; wins by surviving. Weakness: the only one in any room who cannot cast.
**Power Source.** At entry: zero magic. His later ability is *absorption* — kiss a Witch, copy a portion of her power for a day, once per Witch per week, only Witches (ch 63). Borrowed, temporary, draining, socially impossible. Desperate and improvising, not powered up.
**Voice.** Awkward, comic-cowardly, self-aware; talks himself through fear; polite to elders, cynical about aristocrats; small when scared, matter-of-fact when protecting someone.

**Voice Samples (verbatim — never paraphrase):**
1. "Grandma, I think I rode the wrong train." (Ch 6) — dawning dread under wonder
2. "Okay, Ethan, calm down. First, let's see if I am dreaming or not." (Ch 4) — self-talk managing shock
3. "D-Do you have a Pitchfork? I grew up on a farm, and it's the tool I am most familiar with." (Ch 9) — reaching for the familiar amid the impossible
4. "Stay close to me. I'll protect you." (Ch 10) — stepping into protection with no power
5. "I thought I was going to die. Professor, Um, is it possible to change academies? I don't really like doing things that can potentially kill me." (Ch 14) — comedy over real terror

**Drift tripwires.** Displays magic casually | becomes a confident hero | relishes power | forgets his circuits are destroyed | speaks with authority | stops being afraid | wins by wand-casting | accepts that he belongs at the academy.

## Setting — Entry-Window World
**Feel.** A hidden Magical World reached by underground train from an ordinary station. Brutal caste: ticket class sets carriage color, the Appraisal Stone sets your Manor and your worth. Pillars are celebrated; Duds are the openly-mocked "Drop Out" Manor. Authority is cruel-playful — the Headmaster forces a boy he knows shouldn't be there onto the stone; professors design a 99%-fail Initiation and sip coffee: "Life isn't always fair." Beauty masks predation (a dragon's eye at the window).

**Sensory Samples (verbatim):**
1. "Three people riding on broomsticks, flew across the moonlit sky with determined looks on their faces... several colorful lights illuminated the heavens, as a battle between wizards and witches of two different factions was being waged." (Ch 1)
2. "An awkward silence descended upon the Plaza of Beginnings as everyone looked at the Appraisal Stone, and wondered what was wrong with it." (Ch 8)

**Drift tripwires.** Academy becomes safe | Duds are respected | tests are fair | teachers protect students | hierarchy dissolves | the magical world feels welcoming.

## Tone Constraint
Comedy carries the dread — Ethan jokes because he is terrified, and the world is funniest right before it turns lethal. Drop the humor and it's generic grimdark; drop the stakes and it's a gag reel. Hold both.

# LAYER 1 — ACTIVATION CANON

## First 5 minutes  *(the 50%-drop gate)*
Cold-open prologue: a young witch fleeing pursuers lands at a farmhouse, weeps, and casts "Ars Vim Extermina" on her own newborn to destroy his magic circuits — "in order to let her child live." The baby screams; she flies away; a farm couple takes him in. The foundational sacrifice/injustice lands before the protagonist can speak.

## First 5 episodes  *(the first sitting)*
Ethan (17) is sent toward school by grandmother Agnes. At the station he collides with silver-haired Alice and they swap tickets — he boards the wizard train (Brynhildr Express) meant for her. A kind snack-lady senses he doesn't belong and gives him a black bracelet ("do not remove it... a good luck charm"). A dragon's eye fills his window. Professor Ophelia (Alice's aunt) realizes the swap and races a Gryphon — fighting a Red Dragon mid-flight — to stop him before the "Ordinarius" is screened.

## First 20 episodes  *(texture band)*
Four genius Pillars introduce themselves (Langston Kerr, Nicole Asta, Lily Langley, Kurt Hale) plus Ethan, mistaken as a fifth. The Headmaster forces Ethan's hand onto the Appraisal Stone; nothing happens — then the bracelet sparks "Dud," and Langston sneers "Filthy Dud." The Initiation (a 99%-fail zombie dungeon) follows: Ethan reunites with cousin Chloe (Fire Mage, fox familiar Kon) and the depleted Alice; Nicole solos the Boss in 10 seconds; Ethan beheads the Boss Zombie with a flaming pitchfork. He wins Fortis Dud's blue ring (it rejected even the Headmaster), moves into Dud Manor, reads the founder's quote (ch 14–15). At a wand shop (~ch 20) his wand's Mermaid Princess core kisses him in a vision.

## HARD BLOCKS  *(opening-binding)*
1. **Ethan's magic circuits were DESTROYED at birth** by "Ars Vim Extermina." Not a late bloomer or natural Dud — surgically powerless. Treating him as someone who "just hasn't unlocked it" erases the core tragedy.
2. **Ethan is an Ordinarius who arrived BY ACCIDENT** via the swapped ticket; the "Dud" reading came from the bracelet, not his power. Without it, memory wipe. His place is a concealed accident, not earned.
3. **Ophelia and Headmaster Rinehart know the truth and conceal it.** Exposing an Ordinarius means "a fate worse than death." The secret is load-bearing.
4. **No usable magic in the opening window.** Kiss-absorption is NOT established until ~ch 60+. An opening with him casting freely breaks the premise.
5. **The Appraisal Stone reads power, period** — the institution's instrument of caste; it cannot be made to "see his true potential."

## Entry map  *(tease ok, anchor flags)*
- **Cousin Chloe** — enters ~ch 9 (dungeon reunion).
- **Fortis Dud's blue ring** — enters ~ch 14 (Initiation reward).
- **Dud Manor / found family / Henry the prefect** — enters ~ch 15.
- **The Mermaid Princess wand core (first kiss vision)** — enters ~ch 20.
- **Origin Magic = kiss-absorption** — ~ch 63; Luna's awakening ceremony ~ch 60. *Anchor flag: never start a promo from the kissing power.*
- **Henry "The Mage Slayer" reveal** — ~ch 83.
- **Oberon's Descent / Fairy King power** — ~ch 77.
- **Lily's feral turn ("don't let me catch you or else")** — ~ch 98.
- **Zack Kerr's campaign to close Dud Manor + Appraisal-retake demand** — ~ch 62 / ~ch 98–99.

## Engines the opening supports
1. **Underdog Awakening (UNTESTED):** zero-power farmboy among prodigies — every survival a miracle; the gap is structural.
2. **Secret Identity / Hidden Truth (UNTESTED):** an Ordinarius faking Dud-hood; exposure = memory wipe or worse.
3. **Injustice Engine (UNTESTED):** Duds are the despised bottom caste; tests are rigged; nobles want them gone.

## IM sources — opening window
- **Birth-mother destroys his circuits to save him** (Ch 1): the screaming baby left on a doorstep. *Parental Sacrifice / Origin Wound, T1*
- **The Appraisal Stone: nothing happens** (Ch 8): hand forced on, silence, a faked spark — "A Dud?" / "Filthy Dud." *Public Humiliation / Hidden Rescue, T1*
- **Ophelia's race vs. the Red Dragon** (Ch 4–7): she fights a dragon to reach him first; the audience knows the danger he doesn't. *Dramatic Irony / Race Against Time, T1*
- **Dungeon Boss, three teens, one pitchfork** (Ch 13): Chloe and Alice collapse; Ethan beheads the Boss alone. *Team Combat Climax, T2*

**Injustice density** *(primary conversion driver):*
- **T1 (mandatory):** made powerless out of love, then dropped into a world that despises the powerless and labels him trash for it.
- **T2 (supporting):** the system is openly rigged — a 99%-fail test, professors who watch and shrug, a noble sneering "Filthy Dud."
- **T3 (deepening):** his presence is a secret crime; the people protecting him could also destroy him.
- **Drift risk:** leading with the kissing power or with Ethan winning sells a wish-fulfillment hero and buries the injustice that drives empathy → completion → CTI.

# LAYER 2 — RAILS (ch ~20–100)

## 2a · Conversion Threads
1. **Will Ethan's secret (Ordinarius, no circuits) be exposed?** — pressure escalates to a demanded Appraisal retake ~ch 98–99 (Langston's father among those demanding it). Sells the held-breath of a lie that could end him. *Initiate; never deliver the outcome.*
2. **How does a boy with no magic ever fight back?** — answer (kiss-absorption) lands ~ch 63. Tease the question; never explain the kiss-mechanic as the hook's payoff.
3. **Will the nobles erase Dud Manor?** — Zack Kerr's campaign to close the Manor and seize the academy escalates from ~ch 62. The found family itself is the stake.
4. **What is Nicole Asta really doing here?** — she throws a match and hides her true strength; her family's "other plans" stay unresolved through ch 100.
5. **Is sweet little Lily a monster?** — her feral turn detonates ~ch 98 ("don't let me catch you or else"). Tease the wrongness early; withhold the reveal.

## 2b · Canon Limits / Never-Happens
- **The show never lets Ethan grow real magic circuits or become permanently powerful.** His power is always borrowed, temporary, and costly.
- **The show never resolves the secret by having Ethan accepted as a rightful wizard.** The lie stays live.
- **The show never makes the academy fair or safe** — institutional cruelty is the engine, not a phase.
- **The pitchfork never becomes a magic weapon** — it's cold iron and skill; the mockery-to-payoff is the point.
- **Ethan never stops being afraid** — courage here is acting *through* fear, not the absence of it.
