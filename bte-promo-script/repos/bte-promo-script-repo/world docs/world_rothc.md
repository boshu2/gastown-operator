# LAYER 0 — ESSENCE  ·  *the only default load*

## Three Pillars (locked)
1. **The Daughter Who Has to Be a Son**  *(Thruline — secret identity + stolen inheritance)*
2. **A Militarized Star Commonwealth Where Rank Is Destiny**  *(setting — Planet Dominion, the Central Scout Academy, mecha)*
3. **The Mindscape and Little Four**  *(engine — the hidden training system nobody else knows exists)*

## Protagonist — Alaina Valerius
**Identity.** In her first life she was a chronically ill woman on Valora who spent twenty-four years in a bed, in daily agony, watching her family nearly go bankrupt keeping her alive — and who watched her own brother quietly exhale with relief when she died. She wakes as a fetus in an interstellar Commonwealth ten thousand years later, the unborn child of Major General Caius Valerius, killed at the front two months after the wedding. Commonwealth law lets only male relatives inherit military honours, so her mother stands in front of the government, the military, and her husband's circling relatives and announces she is carrying Caius's **son**. Alaina is born a girl, named a young lord, and has never been allowed to be anything else since. The engine is the gap between what she is legally required to be and what she actually is — and the fact that the only person who could ever undo it is her own mother, who did it out of love. Secret Identity on Institutional Injustice.
**Combat.** Fights on precision, patience, and deception — never mass. She is a small child among children twice her weight; she wins by seeing the opening first, by ambush, by playing the harmless kid until the last instant. Every technique she owns is a killing technique learned in the Mindscape, which is exactly why she cannot use most of them in a sanctioned match without exposing herself.
**Power Source.** Two hidden systems, both invisible to this era's technology. (1) The **Aether channelling exercises** an old Valoran doctor taught her in her first life — useless for a decade, then live from the moment she reincarnates: they null pain, repair a day's training damage overnight at no cost, and multiply everything she absorbs. (2) The **Mindscape** — the learning space of a Mandora-system device that calls itself **Little Four**, staffed by numbered instructors, run on missions, deadlines, honour points, and punishments. Limits: her real psionic power is sealed by Little Four (tier four at birth, reported as tier two); she has never touched a mecha; she is six years old through almost the entire window.
**Voice.** Dry, wry, self-deprecating, allergic to attention. An adult's patience inside a child's mouth. She banters with Little Four, is helplessly soft with her mother, meets adults with a flat politeness that reads as unnerving poise, and states brutal things in a bored register. Not a hero — a woman who has already lost one life and intends to spend this one free.

**Voice Samples (verbatim — never paraphrase):**

2. "Bring this Young Lord an extra large steak!" (Ep 18) — the wry adult voice inside the four-year-old
3. "I was experiencing death. In all kinds of ways. Wanna try?" (Ep 58) — asked what she did over the holiday
4. "I don't punish the innocent." (Ep 54) — to the traitor she has just cornered
5. "Saving you was my choice. Dying is yours." (Ep 93) — refusing to be anyone's master
6. "Freedom. A life without chains." (Ep 94) — naming what she actually wants

**Drift tripwires.** Wins by raw power or scale | is publicly outed as a girl | pilots a mecha | claims first place and keeps it | reveals Little Four or the Mindscape to anyone | is played as a swaggering prodigy rather than a woman hiding in a child | learns her father was a god-class pilot | resolves the inheritance, the disguise, or Caius's murder.

## Setting — Entry-Window World (the Valerius estate, Planet Dominion)
**Feel.** A capital planet you have to earn the right to live on. The Commonwealth preaches equality and runs a rigid class ladder in the shadows — which planet you may call home is decided by rank, and Dominion is the top rung, reserved for high officials, decorated commanders, and old families. Every child is enrolled at six and groomed for the military; mecha operators are the aristocracy of that world, and there are only twelve god-class ones alive. The Valerius estate is enormous, hushed, and under siege from the inside: a widow, an old chamberlain, a private hospital full of hand-picked loyalists, and a baby whose body is a state secret. Outside it: transparent landing pads hundreds of stories up, hover traffic stacked four deep, and relatives who would rather have the dead man's benefits than his child.

**Sensory Samples (verbatim):**
1. "The entire landing pad was transparent. A sheer drop of hundreds of stories yawned beneath them." (Ep 14)
2. "A large, red bird with outstretched wings was emblazoned on its chest, looking like it was about to take flight, its body wreathed in flames that shone brilliantly in the morning sun." (Ep 57 — the Valerius phoenix)
3. "Assessment Data: Physical Fitness, S rank. Spiritual Power, Tier Two. Potential, S rank. Overall Assessment: Excellent. Recommended for focused cultivation." (Ep 3 — the newborn pod, seconds after it screamed an alarm)

**Drift tripwires.** Dominion as a meritocracy | the estate as a safe haven rather than a fortress under siege | the disguise as a costume rather than a legal necessity | the military as a protector | mecha as common hardware.

## Tone Constraint
Institutional-injustice underdog with a hidden-identity spine, not a flex. Alaina's edge is invisible and her constraint is total: the more brilliantly she performs, the more dangerous her life gets — perfect scores get buried, a record-breaking rank gets hacked down, a flawless bath result gets read as broken pain receptors and repaid with harsher training. If a beat lets her simply dominate, or skips the mother who did this to her out of love, empathy collapses and it reads as a power fantasy. Little Four's comedy is the release valve, never the lead.

# LAYER 1 — ACTIVATION CANON  ·  *load at W1 + canon gate*

## First 5 minutes  *(the 50%-drop gate)*
A woman floats above her own body in a military hospital and watches her family cry — and catches her younger brother quietly exhale, as if a weight had lifted. She is twenty-four and has been a burden for all of them. A child's voice screams at her that she is about to dissolve, and yanks her backwards. Cut: Star Calendar 4731, a spaceport packed with soldiers boarding for the front. Seraphina puts her husband's hand on her stomach and tells him he's going to be a father; Caius names the baby Alaina, after her — "That way, a part of you will always be with them" — and boards the flagship. A month later the fleet enters a death tunnel and is consumed in a dead-star meteorite field. No survivors, no bodies. Before Seraphina can grieve, a distant Valerius relative arrives to claim the honours Caius died for — and offers to keep her, as a favour. She throws him out, and then, in front of a Commonwealth government representative, makes the choice the whole show is built on: she announces she is carrying Caius's **son**, because only a son may inherit.

## First 5 episodes  *(the first sitting)*
Alaina spends the pregnancy practising Aether exercises that never worked once in twenty-four years — and feels Aether for the first time. She is born in the family's private hospital, delivered by hand-picked loyalists. The newborn assessment pod screams an alarm and then reports **S / Tier Two / S** — better than her father's own birth results — and Seraphina immediately orders the results sealed, because a girl who tests that high gets taken. Quentin, the chamberlain, is quietly heartbroken that she is a girl: no woman has ever piloted an Imperator. Alaina meets the voice in her head — "mecha learning device number four-four-four-four-four-four-four-four-four, from the Mechanical Kingdom of the Mandora star system" — and learns it was the device's awakening that killed her in the first place. Then she hears her mother say the words: "From now on, you are the young lord of the Valerius family. You can only be the young lord of the Valerius family." She wriggles a hand down to check, and finds she is still a girl.

## First 8 episodes  *(the anchor band)*
At the handover of the inheritance documents, Soren Valerius — the branch family's grand elder — proposes that the "most outstanding" Valerius child receive Caius's benefits instead, and produces a one-year-old candidate. The government representative smiles and says nothing. The military representative says the military does not allow the child of a hero to be bullied, and means the opposite. Seraphina, ice-cold, wagers everything: whoever's child tests higher wins, and the loser leaves Dominion forever — and if Alaina loses, Alaina is barred from every military system in the Commonwealth for life. The branch child tests S / Tier One / A-Plus. Alaina tests S / Tier Two / S. The Valerius family is exiled from Dominion within the month, and Seraphina severs her daughter from the name entirely.

## First 20 episodes  *(texture band)*
The military begins delivering **premium-grade gene stimulating agent** — nearly ninety percent purity, worth more than cities, escorted by top mechas. The rarest SS-rank infants absorb seven vials; Alaina absorbs ten, and a general quietly classifies her file as an S-class secret to keep the military from taking her. The Mindscape opens: a circular hall of sealed doors, a pitch-black psychological-resilience test she passes at **Triple S**, and the numbered instructors — **Number One**, who tests without announcing it, and **Number Nine**, who demonstrates three times and never explains. Honour points, deadlines, punishments. At eighteen months the Valerius **tempering bath** — inky green, ninety-six percent plant, four percent snake, scorpion, centipede and spider venom — improves her metrics by 30–80% against her father's all-time record of 10–50%. She endures it in silence, so the household concludes she has faulty pain receptors and resolves to train her harder until she feels "real" pain.

## HARD BLOCKS  *(opening-binding)*
1. **Alaina is a girl living publicly as "Young Lord Alain Valerius."** Known only to Seraphina, Quentin, Elara, the family's hand-picked medical staff, and Little Four. **No one outside the household is ever shown discovering it, and the show never dramatises a reveal.** Her peers, teachers, rivals, and the enemy Empire all say "he." *Translation caveat: the source is inconsistent — the Superintendent's own file (ep 26) reads "the daughter of Major General Caius Valerius," and the Dean and Lt. Gen. Scott say "his daughter" / "the girl" (eps 85–86), with no in-story reaction from anyone. Treat as MTL pronoun drift, not canon. Never build a beat on either reading.*
2. **The disguise exists because Commonwealth law lets only male relatives inherit military honours and benefits.** It is legal and economic, never a whim. Load-bearing — it drives every constraint she lives under.
3. **Caius Valerius is DEAD** — his fleet was consumed in a death tunnel in a dead-star meteorite field before Alaina was born. No body. Never shown alive. *(Ep 55 foreshadows an impossible future choice between Caius and Alaina, and ep 100 raises the contradiction that legacies require Imperator class or higher — tease, never resolve.)*
4. **Little Four and the Mindscape are a total secret.** No character in the world knows either exists. Every intervention Little Four makes must be deniable — a "malfunctioning" hover car, a mecha that "froze."
5. **Alaina's real psionic power was tier four at birth**, sealed by Little Four to tier two; the data given to Seraphina, Quentin, and the military is **fabricated**. She is at the peak of tier four by six. Nobody knows, including her mother.
6. **Alaina has never piloted a mecha through ep 100.** Mecha appear as transport, as escort, as shop models. She rides in a cockpit once and blacks out from her fear of heights.
7. **She never keeps a public first place.** Second place gets hacked down to seventeenth; two later tournaments end at fourth by forfeit. Deliberate mediocrity is her survival strategy, and the show enforces it.
8. **She does not know her father was the youngest and fastest-rising god-class pilot in Commonwealth history.** She thinks he was an unremarkable major general. The Dean, a lieutenant general, and Little Four all know and none of them tell her.
9. **Her reincarnation is known to no one but Little Four.**
10. **The Aether exercises are from her first life, are undetectable by this era's technology, and are never explained in-world.** The household credits everything to Caius's genetics.
11. **The branch family lost the ep-8 wager and were exiled from Dominion.** The departure itself is never shown, only confirmed in passing at ep 14. Soren never reappears — the confrontation is spent; do not re-run it as a live threat.
12. **She is six years old for almost the entire window** (seven by ep 98). Do not age her up.

**Injustice density** *(primary conversion driver):*
- **T1 (mandatory):** a woman who spent one whole life as a burden is reborn and immediately has her identity taken — not by an enemy, but by a mother who loves her, because a law says daughters don't inherit. Her father is dead before she exists, and his own relatives arrive to strip his child in front of the government he died for.
- **T2 (supporting):** every excellence is punished. Perfect absorption gets her file buried. A record-breaking bath result gets her diagnosed as defective and trained harder. A rank earned honestly is hacked away. A smile that wins a stranger over earns her a warning to stop smiling.
- **T3 (deepening):** the system is rigged all the way down — intelligence scoring belongs to the great houses, class placement is bought, mecha are restricted, retainers' children are barred from academies, and a hero's orphan is worth more to the high command as bait than as a person.
- **Drift risk:** lead with the Mindscape / the cheat-code system and skip the disguise and the mother, and it reads as a system-flex power fantasy — the exact wrong show.

# LAYER 2 — RAILS (eps ~20–100)

## 2a · Conversion Threads
1. **The disguise with no exit** — Seraphina's plan is to raise a second girl as a loyal guard who will publicly "marry" Alaina at adulthood; by ep 72 the practical horizon is hormone blockers at puberty. Alaina's stated life goal (ep 73) is a child of her own, which the disguise makes structurally impossible. Sell the trap; never open the door.
2. **Who killed Caius** — moles buried in the Commonwealth's own high command, still unidentified through ep 100. The enemy Empire has a foothold inside the military; the pilots who attacked Alaina were active-duty Commonwealth soldiers. Sell the betrayal, never deliver the name.
3. **Caius's sealed legacy** — a relic withheld from his daughter for six years by a lieutenant general trying to decrypt it for the military, then hidden in the academy's Nexus as an open public challenge under permanent surveillance. Only his heir can open it. Initiate the reach; never deliver the inheritance.
4. **The father she doesn't know** — Alaina believes Caius was an unremarkable major general. He was the youngest god-class pilot in Commonwealth history, one of twelve alive, and everyone around her knows except her. Pure dramatic-irony fuel.
5. **"Belief"** — the name of Caius's Imperator mecha *and* the place Seraphina always said she wanted to go. Alaina answers it correctly at ep 100 without knowing what it is. Tease, never explain.

## 2b · Canon Limits / Never-Happens
- **The show never lets her win by power.** The narration forecloses it explicitly (ep 50): she is not a destined hero who flexes and wipes out hordes, not a beloved main character, not a damsel waiting on a prince. Every win is precision, patience, deception, or delegation.
- **The show never outs her.** No rival, doctor, teacher, or enemy ever suspects, and no reveal is ever dramatised. The closest anyone comes is Julian in ep 74, speculating about where her strength comes from — "An elite family? Some secret, powerful organization? Or maybe." — and deliberately stopping. The source leaves what he almost thought open; **do not play it as a near-outing.**
- **The show never lets her take credit or a following.** She forfeits, she is hacked down the rankings, and when a village she has saved kneels to her she refuses them and is called heartless for it.
- **The show never puts her in a mecha.** Not through ep 100.
- **The show never resolves the inheritance law, the disguise, her father's murder, or her father's legacy** — all four stay open wounds.
- **The show never lets an adult explain the world to her.** Every major reveal — the bait plan, the protection detail, the relic, the surveillance order in her own name — happens in scenes she is not in.
