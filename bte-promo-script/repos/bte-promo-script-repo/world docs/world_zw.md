## Protagonist — Tristan Walden
**Identity.** A 16-year-old raised as livestock in a Lycan enclosure, marked at 12 to become a werewolf the day his parents were taken as monster tribute. Escaping to join the resistance, he's bitten by a zombie, saved by an unknown figure — system error, no such class. Days later the Mistress's werewolf bite stacks a third gene set; it resolves into a **Grade F Zompirewolf — the first of its kind.** A nobody slave is now the most unclassifiable thing alive, hiding it to survive long enough for revenge. Engine fit: **Secret Identity / Hidden Strength + Injustice Engine.**
**Combat.** Reads opponents before he strikes (Perception/Reflex), slows their attacks (Battle Tactics), wins by outsmarting — surrenders a duel on purpose, turns rules against rule-makers. Physically outmatched through the opening; his edge is cold tactics and a body no one can read.
**Power Source.** Three gene sets (Zombie/Vampire/Werewolf) run at once, each leveled by a feeding act: drink blood, eat flesh, kill. A status plate only he sees; Julian hacked it so others see a plain werewolf. Limits: all three within 5 levels or the genes war for control (death); all three off at once = Deceased Human, real death; sun nerfs all stats.
**Voice.** Cold and flat, dry contempt underneath. Short declaratives when threatened; longer when genuinely surprised. He runs an internal kill counter — revenge is debt accounting, not rage. When people expect fear he gives indifference; that's the tell. His one warmth is his father's line, quoted before something reckless.

**Voice Samples (verbatim — never paraphrase):**

**Drift tripwires.** becomes hot-tempered | enjoys the hurting | softens toward the Mistress | turns the Julian/system bond into buddy-comedy | resolves his parents' fate | openly deploys vampire/undead genes in Lycan space | his interior adopts the oppressor's term for himself (livestock/cattle/mutt) — he rejects it, never owns it | his retorts get narrated instead of voiced (he speaks in confrontations).

## Setting — Entry-Window World
**Feel.** A century after a three-point virus turned ~70% of humanity into Undead, Vampires, or Lycans by region, the survivors who couldn't flee Earth live as livestock — kept, fed, gifted, hunted under treaty. Not a rubble apocalypse: the rulers rebuilt cities, courts, economies, academies. The injustice is orderly. Tristan has a shower and a heater and is still a slave, watching the others celebrate the walls.

**Sensory Samples (verbatim):**
1. "Location. Earth. Year. Thirty-one hundred. Designation. Lost Cause." (Ep 1)
2. "The lycans were kind enough to give them shelter, food, and basic supplies, so they were much better off than people in the nearby Territories ruled by the undead and the cold ones." (Ep 1)
3. "In the distance, Tristan watched the others laughing and smiling... as if they couldn't see the walls surrounding them, or the guards standing on top of those thick walls, watching their every move." (Ep 1)

**Drift tripwires.** generic zombie apocalypse (humanity is organized and subjugated, not scattered) | night creatures as the main threat (the politics are the prison) | erase the treaty (the king lets vampires hunt a few werewolves every six months — the load-bearing absurdity).

## Tone Constraint
Cold dark comedy over institutional horror — the laugh is Tristan's flat contempt against polite, rule-bound cruelty. Failure mode: tip into fun power fantasy or gory splatter and the injustice stops landing — the kills read as action, not justice.

## First 20 episodes  *(texture band)*

Introduced but not yet load-bearing: the Director, the underground fight circuit, the noble bounty.

## HARD BLOCKS  *(opening-binding)*
1. **Tristan is NOT a werewolf — he is a Zompirewolf (tribrid).** The species doesn't exist; no character may confirm it. The Mistress believes him a gifted werewolf. Treating him as a normal werewolf breaks the premise.
2. **The blessing is black; he always says "yellow."** Black = immediate extraction/experimentation. Reveal the real color only as a controlled "secret cracks" tease.
3. **All three gene levels must stay within 5 of each other.** Exceeding the gap = gene warfare (agony, death) — this forces the covert night feeding. Power is never free.
4. **Deactivating all three traits at once = permanent death** (Deceased Human). Absolute limit, set by Julian in Ep 5.
5. **The Wind Daggers are stolen and traceable** to the Elara/Felix killings; secret night hunts only, never public.
6. **Julian cannot be summoned** — he only initiates, in life-threat. Part of "the Syndicate," nature withheld in the opening. Don't invent his backstory as anchor.
7. **Seraphina cannot be overpowered in the entry window.** Her aura alone incapacitates her own elite guards ("Extremely Dangerous"). Don't write Tristan close to beating her.
8. **Tristan's class is "Unassigned."** His tribrid nature breaks classification — no predictable ceiling. Don't assign him a class.

# LAYER 2 — RAILS (eps ~20–100)

## 2a · Conversion Threads
1. **Are his parents alive?** — initiates Ep 1, reframed ~ep 65 (the king says they may be alive, location withheld). The emotional engine. Never deliver.
2. **The Director knows his real surname — and betrayed his parents.** — seeded ~ep 18, named ~ep 65. Who is she, and what does she want with him?
3. **The secret cracking.** — runs the whole arc: the Mistress's all-seeing eye, the Director's scanner anomaly. The day they learn what he is, everything changes.
4. **What is the Syndicate?** — teased Ep 5, paid ~ep 96 (The Voidborn; Silas Kent living inside him). Tease the question only; the answer lives deep.
5. **Seraphina's plan to use him against the king** — seeded Ep 4 ("my new sword, to tear the king's family apart"). He is playing her while she thinks she owns him.

## 2b · Canon Limits / Never-Happens
- **The show never lets Tristan use his power freely** — every vampire/undead level costs a risky secret feeding; balance rule is permanent.
- **The show never resolves the parents on-screen** — they are reported, never shown; status stays a question through ch 100.
- **The show never writes Seraphina as romance** — the dynamic is possession and predation; he keeps a kill counter with her name on it.
- **The show never makes Julian a reliable ally** — he initiates only, has his own revenge, and withholds clearance-gated truth.
