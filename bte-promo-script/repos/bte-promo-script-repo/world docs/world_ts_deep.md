## World Mechanics

### The Deity-Bloodline System
- **Seven deity families total** (Sterling + six others). Each deity formed an ancient covenant with one bloodline. The Sterlings, bonded to the Golden Deity, are historically the strongest and most influential.
- Rival families' powers are mostly undetailed — Sylvia names possible domains: "mastery over elements, illusions, shadows, physical force, even certain aspects of fate or knowledge" (Ch 14). Do not invent specific named-family abilities for the entry window.
- **Perception tiers:** only the Light Bearer (one per generation, direct line) perceives Aurion in full manifestation and receives direct instruction. Collateral Sterlings (e.g. Sylvia) perceive only warmth, pressure, or a flash of golden light. Non-Sterlings perceiving the Deity directly are **annihilated**.
- The rival families pooled their powers to assassinate Jed's father — an unprecedented coalition. They believe they ended the direct Sterling line.
- **The Eighth Deity (Ch 74):** a hidden eighth power whose hosts were "hunted, exterminated" centuries ago during the "last great Shadow War"; described as the only power that is "a true mirror" to Aurion. Deep late reveal — not entry canon.
- **Rival families named in the mid/late arc:** Brown, Stone, Harbour (operatives begin probing Stonevale ~ch 66–91). The Browns are flagged as the most immediate territorial rival.

### The Light Bearer Powers
- **Physical:** speed (world goes slow-motion, outruns vehicles), strength (kills with bare hands, crushes pipe/metal), instant healing (a near-fatal beating's injuries vanished, Ch 5).
- **Senses:** night vision, hearing across blocks (heard Ella crying blocks away, Ch 6), threat perception.
- **Instinctive knowledge:** medical/herbal (diagnosed and brewed a remedy untrained, Ch 7), combat instinct (techniques never learned), cognitive enhancement.
- **The locket:** opens only to Sterling blood; post-activation contains a golden gem; a communication link to Aurion and the Sterling legacy.

### The Sacrifice System
- Each Light Bearer faces a unique sacrifice "tailored to their life, their circumstances, their deepest connections" (Ch 13).
- **Lord Arthur's sacrifice:** could never set eyes on his own children — forcing Jed and his siblings into orphanages under false identities. Cosmic decree, not choice.
- **Jed's sacrifice:** UNKNOWN; due by the next full moon (~4 weeks from start); still "impending" / UNRESOLVED through ch 100.

### The Sterling Empire
- **Wealth:** Jed's black-card "allowance" holds ~$1 trillion — "a tiny fraction" of overall Sterling wealth (Ch 15).
- **Holdings:** "approximately seventy percent of all significant business and real estate holdings within the city" — including Stonevale General (Sterling Medical Holdings owns the whole hospital).
- **Sterling Applied Technologies:** R&D far beyond commercial market — the one-of-one obsidian phone (no seams/buttons/logos, eagle sigil, bespoke encryption; "That's a myth," Ch 17).
- **Operatives:** elite bodyguards, intelligence, institutional contacts; Sylvia commands them; they defer to Jed.
- **King's Landing:** a ~$100M penthouse Jed acquires ~ch 33–34; where he shelters Ella and Mrs. Diaz.
- **Lord Arthur Sterling:** the previous Light Bearer; remembered by operatives (e.g. Mitchell White) for "quiet strength, unwavering integrity, profound kindness"; Jed bears his exact birthmark and is connected via a paternal ring/mark thread (Ch 81–82).

### The Social Hierarchy (Stonevale)
- A "kingdom of heirs and golden names" (Ch 1). Wealth is visible in clothes, cars, family names; scholarship kids are invisible or mocked.
- Authority figures (receptionists, hospital staff, restaurant managers, security) treat people by apparent wealth; the same people who sneer at Jed grovel when the Sterling name surfaces.
- Tiers: Chris Jones = new money (local power, weaponized through bodyguards/bribery, insignificant vs. Sterling); the Blacks = old money (reflexive institutional contempt).

- **Jed ↔ his siblings:** known to exist, scattered in orphanages, unfindable "for now."

## Flavor Notes
- The wealth-contrast IS the injustice engine — every scene where Jed is sneered at is a scene where the audience knows he's the richest person in the room. The dramatic irony is the central pleasure.
- The black card is the recurring IM vehicle: contempt reverses on sight — but each use risks exposure.
- Jed's humor under duress is the tonal counterweight; without it the show is just suffering.
- The Jenifer/Chris betrayal is structurally complete by Ch 3 — use it for setup, don't dwell; the engine is the hidden power, secret wealth, and the people Jed protects.
- Two layers of antagonism: mortal (Chris, Ruben — wealth and violence) and cosmic (rival deity families — existential). Entry-window promos operate in the mortal layer; the cosmic layer is destination.
