## Protagonist — Nathan Hayfield
**Identity.** A nameless, memory-less warrior soul wakes inside the corpse of Nathan Hayfield — a scrawny twelve-year-old beaten to death in a school duel — resurrecting him as he dies. He keeps the dead boy's face, name, and family and vows to fight for them; nobody knows the original Nathan is gone. An ancient master's instinct in a child's broken body, carrying a borrowed life he's terrified of "stealing." Fits **Hidden Identity / Reborn-Underdog** engines.
**Combat.** Wins by technique, deception, and reading opponents — never brute size. His soul knows every sword form; his child's body can barely execute them. Weakness: the body breaks before the soul does, and his strength must stay hidden.
**Power Source.** The **Power of Asura** — rare spiritual magic channeled by speaking the god's name; named techniques (Asura Fist, Asura Eye for lies/intent, Razor Finger, Speed Slice, Weakening Aura), plus **alchemy** (Dexterity Elixir, healing potions). Limits: power runs on a fixed ladder (Beginner→Apprentice→Master); leveling slows hard at Master; early spells are too weak to harm and need meditation + practice to land.
**Voice.** Archaic and theatrical ("thee/thou," "I cry your mercy") in public; dry, modern, sardonic in private thought. Tender and guilt-ridden toward the family he deceives; cold toward cruelty. A weary veteran narrating his own strategy through a boy's grin — never a swaggering chosen-one.

**Voice Samples (verbatim — never paraphrase):**
1. "Nathan Hayfield, wherever you are – I dedicate my life to thee." (Ep 2) — vow / solemn
2. "If you are a puppet of some greater force, you relinquish your own will. If ever I relinquish my will to a greater force, I would rather someone killed me." (Ep 9) — creed / defiant
3. "Overconfidence has slain more warriors than battle, famine, and illness all put together." (Ep 5) — veteran tactician
4. "I see no real men here. Unless you refer to the ambulatory chamberpots standing in front of me." (Ep 5) — sardonic taunt
5. "I'm a stranger here. I stole this from him. And brought his family further into conflict." (Ep 8) — private guilt

**Drift tripwires.** Becomes a swaggering chosen-one | wins by raw size/strength | reveals the soul-swap to family | loses the guilt over the borrowed life | maxes power instantly with no ladder cost.

## Setting — Entry-Window World
**Feel.** A medieval feudal kingdom (Calandria) of honor-bound nobility and class corruption. House Hayfield is a fallen noble line — a holed thatch roof under a proud banner. Powerful houses (Raven, Clay) covet their land and want them stricken from nobility. Strength = rank; duels run "until satisfaction," sanctioned only by a noble witness; warrior academies forge nobles' children into "war heroes" through sanctioned brutality.

**Sensory Samples (verbatim):**
1. "Once simply elegant, it had fallen into disrepair. With holes in the roof, paint flaking off the walls... The only thing that seemed new was the Hayfield family banner, flying proudly." (Ep 1)
2. "The life of a spiritual warrior is pain and survival. So training to be one should be no less rigorous." (Ep 1)
3. "...a white sword tilled a golden field." (Ep 3, Hayfield crest)

**Drift tripwires.** Becomes modern/urban | guns or gunpowder | a benevolent fair justice system | duels without the "until satisfaction" code | power untethered from social rank.

## Tone Constraint
Grim feudal cruelty lightened by genuine family warmth and Nathan's dry wit. Failure mode: tipping into pure grimdark (loses the hearth heart) or quippy power-fantasy (loses the injustice that drives empathy).

# LAYER 1 — ACTIVATION CANON  ·  *the opening anchor*

## First 5 minutes  *(the 50%-drop gate)*
A scrawny 12-year-old, Nathan Hayfield, is goaded into a duel by the bigger, noble-backed bully Jason Clay, who insults his father and sister. The crowd jeers; the headmaster refuses to stop it ("until satisfaction"). Jason methodically breaks Nathan's bones, ignores his yield, and crushes his skull. Nathan is carried home to Dunshire and **dies** in his sister Amelia's arms. Then a blue light pours from his mouth, his corpse levitates, a shockwave is felt across the world by every powerful mage — and the dead boy's eyes open.

## First 5 episodes  *(the first sitting)*
A memory-less warrior soul now inhabits Nathan. He recovers his "host's" memories piecemeal, overhears the Ravens pressuring his father Sir Ulrich to renounce nobility, and vows to defend the family. He discovers the **Power of Asura** (Fist, Eye, Razor Finger). He returns to the Academy of the Mystic Blades, faces the headmaster, reunites with loyal friend Baelor, and — when Jason kidnaps Baelor as bait — duels and defeats Jason easily, then kills him to save Baelor's throat (Ep 5).

## First 20 episodes  *(texture band)*
Cedric Raven lies about the duel; only Stewart Lightstone (star pupil, High Lord's son) intervenes to save Nathan (Ep 7), seeding the Lightstone alliance. Nathan returns home a folk hero, trains in secret (weighted hikes, Mount Dun, alchemy), and over a **two-year time skip** (Ep 12) rises from Beginner to Master rank while hiding his true strength. He gifts Baelor a sword technique; the Clays send the assassin Mordrid after Amelia (Ep 16); a spiritual-warrior **examination** looms where Nathan ranks 8th (Ep 19–24), drawing the attention of the Doom Faction.

## HARD BLOCKS  *(opening-binding)*
1. **Nathan is a foreign warrior soul in the original Nathan's dead body.** The whole premise; the original boy is gone.
2. **The family must NOT know about the soul swap.** Load-bearing secret driving Nathan's guilt and every deception.
3. **Power runs on a fixed ladder (Beginner→Apprentice→Master, numbered tiers); leveling slows at Master.** No instant omnipotence.
4. **Nathan starts physically weak — a child's broken body.** His edge is skill/Asura, not size; early spells can't even harm.
5. **Asura is channeled by name and learned through meditation/practice.** It is not free or limitless.
6. **The world is medieval-feudal; rank = power = survival.** No modern tech, no fair impartial justice.
7. **Priscilla (the mother) is dead before ep 1.** Her death is an open mystery, not a living character.

## Entry map  *(tease ok, anchor flags)*

- **Asura Eye (lie/intent detection)** — enters ~ep 3.

## Engines the opening supports
1. **Reborn / Second-Chance Underdog (UNTESTED):** dead-weakling-reborn-as-master opens an instant transformation loop from the cold open's death.
2. **Hidden Identity (UNTESTED):** the soul-swap secret the family can never learn is a permanent dramatic-irony engine.
3. **Injustice-Revenge (UNTESTED):** noble bullies murder a child with impunity; the system protects them — primes the empathy-to-payback loop.

## IM sources — opening window
- **The duel-murder of a yielding child** (Ep 1): Jason ignores "I yield" and crushes Nathan's skull as the headmaster watches. *Injustice, T1*
- **The system protects the killer** (Ep 6): Cedric lies, the headmaster calls Baelor's throat-wound "counterfeit." *Injustice, T1*
- **Ravens pressure a grieving father to renounce his nobility** (Ep 2): "withdraw your nobility... or who knows?" *Injustice, T2*
- **Assassin sent to murder the sister in her sleep** (Ep 16): "I want her to suffer." *Injustice, T2*

**Injustice density** *(primary conversion driver):*
- **T1 (mandatory):** a powerless, honorable child is beaten to death for sport by a noble-backed bully while authority refuses to intervene — and the system then shields the killer.
- **T2 (supporting):** the corrupt powerful houses (Raven/Clay) hunt the whole broke, honorable family — father, sister — to seize their land and erase their name.
- **T3 (deepening):** Nathan must hide his power and his true identity, swallowing every taunt and grief alone, unable to claim even the family he's fighting for.
- **Drift risk:** if a promo leads with Nathan's strength/wins instead of the helpless injustice, it kills the empathy nerve and the completion/CTI collapse.

# LAYER 2 — RAILS (eps ~20–100)

## 2a · Conversion Threads
1. **Who killed Nathan's mother, Priscilla?** — pays off deep (past ep 65; drives the Doom Faction arc). An open murder mystery; Nathan joins the trial partly to hunt her killer.
2. **Will the family ever learn Nathan isn't really Nathan?** — open through ep 100+. The dramatic-irony loop seeded from ep 2; Ulrich nearly suspects ("reborn that day," Ep 9).
3. **Can a near-commoner boy win the Doom Faction Trial and a Dukedom?** — pays off ~ep 24→trial arc (ep 65+). Prize: title of Duke + a fiefdom, restoring House Hayfield.
4. **The fall of House Raven** — pays off ~ep 61 (Nathan slays Remus Raven, becomes "the Demon Knight"). Can be *teased* as the looming siege of Dunshire; never deliver the kill.
5. **What was Nathan's original life / where did the warrior soul come from?** — open indefinitely; the star-ocean of lost souls (Ep 9). The deepest identity hook.

## 2b · Canon Limits / Never-Happens
- **The show never lets Nathan win by brute strength** — his edge is always technique, Asura, deception, and reading the enemy.
- **The show never resolves the soul-swap to the family within the opening** — the secret stays a secret; reveal would break the core engine (promote to HARD BLOCK #2).
- **The show never grants instant power** — every level is earned through training/combat on the ladder; gains slow at Master.
- **The show never makes the justice system fair** — appeals to honor codes only work via leverage/allies, never neutral fairness.
- **Asura never becomes a generic "anything" power** — it manifests as specific named techniques with practice cost.
