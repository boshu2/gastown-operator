## Protagonist — Jed (Jedidiah) Sterling
**Identity.** Broke scholarship kid at Stonevale — pizza boy, cleaner, laundry guy, tutor, errand boy at once (Ch 2). Orphan, told his parents died in a "car accident"; refuses his rich best friend Damian's money every time. His girlfriend Jenifer betrays him with Chris Jones, the bully who has him beaten and dumped in a trash bin to die (Ch 3–4). A locket he could never open snaps open as he dies; the Golden Deity Aurion enters him and names him the Light Bearer, heir to the Sterling Dynasty (Ch 4–5). Engine: **hidden-heir-underdog** — wealth is a trillion, *status stays zero*, hiding it is the game.
**Combat.** Bare hands only. Supernatural speed (the world "syrups," afterimages, outruns leaping dogs), instinctive strikes, two-finger knockouts, kills with throat-slash and spear-hand. Powers arrived fully formed through the bond — not trained. Wins by speed, not technique.
**Power Source.** The Sterling locket — direct-bloodline heirloom, opens only to Sterling blood, never budged his whole life. Activated when his blood touched it as he died (Ch 4): a golden eagle (Aurion) emerged, dissolved into sparks, and entered him; injuries vanished. Grants strength, speed, instant healing, enhanced senses (night vision, hearing across blocks), instinctive medical/herbal knowledge, cognitive enhancement. **Not** a leveling system, mentor-taught, gradual, or an external weapon post-activation. Only the Light Bearer — one per generation, direct line — can perceive Aurion fully without dying.
**Voice.** Self-deprecating under pressure; names his own absurdity while covered in garbage. Protective to a fault. Quiet defiance, not loud revenge. Moral friction about the killing power ("What am I becoming?"). Names his poverty without shame; white-lies to friends.

**Voice Samples (verbatim):**
1. "I'd rather work than feel like a burden." (Ch 1) — pride-as-identity
2. "Crazy locket. You wait till I'm about to die before you do anything." (Ch 4) — dark humor under duress
3. "All good! Just performing an unscheduled structural analysis of municipal waste receptacles. Grade A performance." (Ch 6) — humor-as-deflection
4. "Good question. Still working on the answer." (Ch 6) — wry, to "What are you?!"
5. "I'm just a nobody," he muttered. "A delivery boy with no parents, no future, no—" (Ch 5) — identity wound, raw

**Drift tripwires.** Cocky-rich-guy voice | fights with trained technique or weapons | power awakens gradually through study | publicly operates as a Sterling | relishes violence | treats wealth as normal | treats friends as subordinates.

## Setting — Entry-Window World
**Feel.** Jed orbits the pizza shop (neon sign, grease, mildew backroom), the scholarship dorm, and the wealth spaces he doesn't belong in — the Blueprint Hotel, the Crystal Hollow, Stonevale General. The texture is contrast: cracked shoes on marble, a pizza bag in a VIP suite, a trillion-dollar card in faded jeans. Wealth is the visible hierarchy — clothes, name, car decide how staff and classmates treat you. The Sterling name flips it instantly (sneers turn to bows), but Jed can't use it without exposing himself to the families that murdered his father.

**Sensory Samples (verbatim):**
1. "Rain-slicked streets glistened under the streetlights, and the scent of old grease drifted from the half-cracked kitchen window." (Ch 1)
2. "The backroom always smelled faintly of garlic and mildew. A beat-up radio crackled above the lockers, playing '80s rock hits no one liked but everyone tolerated." (Ch 1)
3. "Water cascaded down textured crystal panels embedded in the walls, catching the ambient light and scattering miniature rainbows across the manicured entrance." (Ch 10 — Crystal Hollow)

**Drift tripwires.** Fantasy/medieval setting | magic academy or cultivation sect | Jed comfortable in wealth spaces | poverty played for comedy over dignity | no class-contrast tension in the frame.

## Tone Constraint
A power fantasy with heart: the wealth-contrast injustice carries the empathy, Jed's humor under duress is the counterweight. Slip the humor and it's just suffering; drop the injustice and the trillion is a hollow flex.

# LAYER 1 — ACTIVATION CANON  ·  *the opening anchor*

## First 5 minutes  *(the 50%-drop gate)*
Jed, drained from a delivery shift, learns from Damian that Jenifer — who claimed she was sick — is at the party. The lie lands. Establishes the scholarship-kid-in-a-kingdom-of-heirs world, the cracked-shoes-on-marble wound, and the central injustice before any supernatural beat.

## First 5 episodes (ch 1–5)  *(the first sitting)*
The inciting spine: Jed delivers pizza to the very room where Jenifer is with Chris (Ch 3); Chris reveals the betrayal was always a trap; Jed is beaten and dumped in trash to die (Ch 4); the locket activates and Aurion enters, heals, and names him heir of the Sterling bloodline (Ch 4–5). Powers, locket, and deity all established by the first sitting's end.

## First 20 episodes (ch 1–20)  *(texture band)*
Jed saves Ella from dogs/thugs (Ch 6) and heals her mother Mrs. Diaz with instinctive herbal knowledge (Ch 7). Sylvia Sterling (custodian) phones him (Ch 8), then bows publicly at the Crystal Hollow (Ch 11–12). She reveals his father was the last Light Bearer whose sacrifice was never to see his children (Ch 13), was **murdered** by rival deity families, and that Jed has scattered unknown siblings (Ch 13–14). The trillion-dollar card (Ch 15); the one-of-one obsidian phone (Ch 17). Jed kills Martin Garcia rescuing Ella/Mrs. Diaz (Ch 18); Mrs. Diaz falls into a coma (Ch 19–20). Dr. Armitage spots his father's birthmark; Sandra reveals Jenifer used Jed as her "grade-booster" (Ch 20).

## HARD BLOCKS  *(opening-binding)*
1. **Jed's Sterling identity is SECRET** — his father's murderers think the line is extinct (his only protection). Never operating publicly as a Sterling.
2. **Jed's powers are SECRET from the people he loves** — he later knocks his friends out to keep it. No casual displays before witnesses he cares about.
3. **The trillion-dollar card is "a tiny fraction" of Sterling wealth (Ch 15), but spending openly risks exposure.** Don't buy through problems in the open.
4. **Lord Arthur Sterling could NEVER see his children — deity-imposed sacrifice, not abandonment (Ch 13).** Never portray him as choosing to leave.
5. **Jed's father was MURDERED by a coalition of rival deity families who believe the line is dead (Ch 13–14).** Don't resolve or expose it.
6. **Aurion took eagle form and SPOKE — unprecedented; only Alexander the First had direct communication (Ch 13).** Don't diminish this.
7. **A SACRIFICE is owed by the next full moon (~4 weeks); its nature is UNRESOLVED through ch 100.** Don't invent or resolve it.
8. **Martin Garcia is DEAD (Ch 18); Mrs. Diaz is in a COMA, not dead (Ch 19–20).** Don't reverse either.
9. **Chris Jones is a MORTAL bully — not deity-connected.** Don't elevate him to a supernatural threat.

# LAYER 2 — RAILS (ch ~20–100)

## 2a · Conversion Threads
1. **Jed's unknown sacrifice, due by the next full moon** — unresolved through ch 100. The clock over the whole story; the cost of his power, deliberately withheld.
2. **The scattered Sterling siblings** — confirmed to exist (Ch 13–14), forbidden to find "for now." A reunion the audience aches for; never delivered in-window.
3. **Who murdered his father, and the coming reckoning** — six rival deity families; their direct operatives (Brown/Stone/Harbour) close in from ~ch 66+. Pays off as the macro-war, seeded as cold-traffic intrigue.
4. **Mrs. Diaz's coma** — the emotional cost of Jed's intervention; an open wound that keeps the trillion grounded.
5. **Will Chris/Ruben ever learn what Jed is** — Chris saw Sylvia bow and can't explain it; Ruben hunts a man he thinks is "a fucking delivery boy." Dramatic-irony loop.

## 2b · Canon Limits / Never-Happens
- **The show never lets Jed solve a problem by openly spending or flaunting Sterling money.** Each use of the card risks exposure; the constraint is the point.
- **The show never gives Jed a leveling/stat system or trained progression.** Power arrived whole through the bond; he grows in control and knowledge, not in unlocked tiers.
- **The show never resolves the sacrifice or reunites the siblings within ch 1–100.** Promising either as a payoff breaks faith with the source.
- **The show never makes Jed a public, named Sterling power.** Secrecy is the survival premise, not a temporary phase.
