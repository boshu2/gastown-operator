# ANCHOR 1: WEAPONS (MVS — Quinn)

**Why this is a reference:** The highest-performing promo tested. Non-origin, event-based, mid-series entry. Proves you can drop a cold audience into a world mid-stream with zero exposition. Peak IM density (7 IMs, 7 flavors, 30% CTI). Secret Identity engine at full power.

**Promo type: FRESH TAKE.** This is not a compression of early episodes. Quinn does get gauntlets and goes to Weapon Training in the show, but this promo is an invention — it sells the world in a fundamentally different way than the source material presents it. The events are engineered for CPI, not adapted from a sequence of episodes. **Reference this when:** building an original angle that reimagines the world for cold traffic.

## Promo Brief
- **Throughline:** Quinn must survive an academy of ability users while hiding that he's the thing they've spent centuries hunting — a vampire.
- **Dramatic Container:** Event-based — Weapon Selection ceremony → Arena Duel
- **Quest:** Survive weapon selection and the duel without exposing his vampire nature.
- **Antagonist:** Rex — Level Four bully with a crew, escalates from mockery to physical violence.
- **Stakes Character:** Erin — strongest student, protects Quinn before knowing him, forms bond through action.
- **Cliffhanger:** Dread-forward binary — Quinn activates full vampiric powers to save Erin. Secret exposed. Rex and Loop are watching. No clean exit.

## Key Execution Moments

**The try-fail that teaches the world:** "Quinn picks up a sword — it shatters in his grip. Grabs a flail — it goes weightless, floats out of his hand." The audience discovers "weapons choose their wielders" without anyone stating the rule. This is the template for Institutional Depth IMs.

**Antagonist dialogue that accumulates resentment:** Rex spits on Quinn while he's held in Loop's psychic grip. The audience doesn't just dislike Rex — they REMEMBER the specific moment. Surgical, not generic.

**Bond through action, not warmth:** Erin's ice spike between Rex's legs. She doesn't comfort Quinn. She tells him to move. The bond is competence and protection, not sentiment.

**The dual-layer engine:** Every beat after Beat 1 carries BOTH physical danger and detection danger simultaneously. When Quinn catches the spear, the physical win IS the detection risk. This is what makes the Secret Identity engine prevent pacing plateaus.

## What This Script Does That Others Should Copy
- Zero exposition before crisis. The audience is in a fight by sentence two.
- Every IM uses a different flavor. Ice-powers, sentient objects, ancient artifacts, size contrast, impossible catch, surgical precision, vampiric reveal. Seven moments, seven vocabularies.
- The central question ("why would someone this powerful take this?") is planted in the first 10 seconds and never resolves.
- Context delivered as cargo: "Vampires are hated, considered monsters" lands inside a beat where Quinn is choosing not to fight back. The world-building IS the dramatic tension.
- The final beat IS a Tier 1 IM — Quinn stepping in front of Erin and absorbing both spells shifts the world from "academy with a secret" to "what happens when a vampire stops hiding." The audience's understanding of what's possible changes AND Quinn's trajectory is permanently altered. This is earned by every beaten-down beat before it.

# ANCHOR 2: SPORE CLOUD (WBT — Ren)

> ⚠️ **OFF-ESSENCE ANCHOR — REFERENCE THE STRUCTURE, NOT THE "CLOUD" CHARACTER (WBT HARD BLOCK 8).** This anchor's metrics and its journey-compression mechanism are valid. But its entire brief and beat map are built on a fabrication: a sentient companion beast named **"Cloud"** that nuzzles, shields, leads Ren to the flower, and serves as "bond object / stakes character / IM delivery vehicle." **WBT canon has no character named Cloud** — Ren's beast is a fused, near-inert spore (cosmetic hair-mushrooms), and the empathy bond is **Ren ↔ his parents**, not Ren ↔ a pet. When transferring from this anchor: take the *opening-compression* and *journey-as-world-expansion* craft only. Do **not** import the Cloud-as-bond-object structure — that is the exact spore-personification this system now hard-blocks. (Compare the W5 finding in `working files/documentation/WBT_SummitClimb_v1.md`, which flags spore-as-character/combat-power as HARD BLOCK breaks.)

**Why this is a reference:** Strongest validation of journey-based promos. Opening compression done right. Third-best CTI (21.31%). Best Q4 (97.8%). Self-Sacrifice engine with the bond object as IM delivery vehicle.

**Promo type: OPENING COMPRESSION.** This is the first ~6 episodes of WBT compressed into a single-day story. The IMs and vital beats from the source material are strung together to build a promo that feels true to the show's opening without much invention. Similar approach to Live Action 2.0 (MVS). **Reference this when:** compressing early episodes into a single promo, preserving the show's natural IM moments while engineering the connective tissue for CPI.

## Promo Brief
- **Throughline:** Ren hatches the worst beast on record and refuses to give up on it — every choice to stay loyal costs him more, until the beast reveals it was never what anyone thought.
- **Dramatic Container:** Journey-based — hatching ceremony → home → forest → mantis → underground pit → ancient ruins → bloom
- **Quest:** Find the Half-Flower of the Heavens in the deadly forest to gain a second beast and save his family from poverty.

## Key Execution Moments

**The tenderness beat that earns everything:** Cloud lands on Ren's shoulder and nestles against him. Mushrooms sprout. Ren laughs for the first time all day: "I'm not alone." This is the scroll-stopper in the Self-Sacrifice engine. Without it, the audience doesn't invest in the bond.

**IM through contrast, not magnitude:** The mushroom hypnosis works because the "useless" ability does the ONE thing no powerful beast could do — freeze the deadliest predator using its own mechanism. The contrast between Cloud's perceived worthlessness and the actual result is what makes the friend say "wait, what?"

**Journey structure earning IMs naturally:** Each new environment introduces a new world system — the forest's fungal network, the underground ruins, the ancient spore colony, the Half-Flower. The journey itself IS the world-expansion.

**Show-don't-tell institutional depth:** The hatching ceremony doesn't explain how beast-bonding works — the audience watches students transform. Wings, claws, golden scales. Then Ren gets mushrooms. The rules are discovered through the spectacle of watching them play out.

## What This Script Does That Others Should Copy
- Bond object as IM vehicle. Every Tier 1 moment is Cloud doing something impossible. The stakes character IS the world-expansion engine.
- Journey compression works when every location introduces a new world system. No task beats. No travel filler. Each transition is caused by the previous beat's outcome.
- The "useless thing is secretly the key" structure creates natural Tier 1 moments — each revelation about Cloud changes what the audience thinks is possible.
- Emotional escalation parallels physical escalation. Ren goes from "why did I hatch you" to "I'm putting my trust in you now." The bond grows INSIDE the crisis.

# STRUCTURAL COMPARISON: ROTTING BOY vs SPORE CLOUD

Same show, same engine, same protagonist, same beast — different containers and different results.

| Dimension | Rotting Boy | Spore Cloud |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Shape** | Event-based (Academy → Bloom Race) | Journey-based (Ceremony → Forest → Ruins → Bloom) |
| **Opening** | Mid-crisis ("My beast was pinned and I was bleeding") | Ceremony ("Whatever hatched from my egg would determine my family's fate") |
| **IM Count** | 5 (3 T1, 2 T2) | 6 (4 T1, 2 T2) |
| **CTI** | 17.99% | 21.31% |
| **Q4** | 103% (skip-ahead) | 97.8% |
| **Antagonist** | Kai — present throughout, surgical dialogue | Klein — present early, then the forest carries threat |
| **Key difference** | Event structure concentrates IMs in institutional beats + climactic sequence | Journey structure spreads IMs across environments — each location is a new world system |

**What this comparison teaches:** The journey-based version produces more natural IM opportunities because each new environment is a potential world-expansion moment. The event-based version has a stronger antagonist presence. Both work — the choice depends on whether the material's strongest escalation comes from phases within a single event or from movement through increasingly dangerous territory.

Self-Sacrifice engine at full power — every refusal costs more, every sacrifice compounds, the anticipation beat is earned entirely by the throughline. ## Key Execution Moments

**The tenderness beat:** Hawk and Rae send Karl a telepathic message after the opening humiliation: "You're special! That's why we chose you!" This is the Self-Sacrifice engine's scroll-stopper. The bond is reciprocal — the beasts love him back. Without this moment, the refusals that follow are principled but cold. With it, they're heartbreaking.

**The escalating cost principle in action:** Karl turns down a WilderBear (material cost — stays weak). Gets banned from the event (social cost — loses his chance). Runs back for Rae instead of escaping (physical cost — almost killed). Breaks the protective field to save both beasts (life cost — will die). Each refusal costs MORE. The audience watches the price climb from "stays weak" to "will die." That's the engine at full power.

**Dragon-as-hairstylist:** The single most effective fun-gate IM in any tested promo. A mythic creature — a legendary dragon that could destroy armies — is being used to blow-dry a prince's hair and warm his feet. The CONTRAST between what the dragon IS and what it's being USED for is what makes the audience say "wait, what?" and then laugh. This is magnitude through contrast, not magnitude through power.

**The anticipation beat is pure Self-Sacrifice payoff:** The dragon doesn't choose Karl because he's powerful. It chooses him because he chose his beasts first. Every single refusal the audience watched — every time Karl stayed weak instead of trading up — is the cause of this moment. The throughline produces the reward. Nothing external. Nothing lucky. The audience can trace the payoff back through every beat.

**Hunger-forward cliffhanger:** "A legendary dragon only bonds with royal blood. So what makes you so special?" This is a genuinely NEW question — not a replay of "will he survive" but "who IS he?" The world expands from beast academy to royal lineage mystery. Q4 at 95.34% — the audience accelerates through the end.

## What This Script Does That Others Should Copy
- The beast IS the stakes character AND the IM vehicle. Hawk and Rae are both — their loyalty drives the throughline, and their surprising capabilities (feather draws blood, spider flinches dragon) deliver the world-expansion.
- Every IM passes the fun gate. Dragon-as-hairstylist, tiny spider disrupting a legendary beast, feather drawing blood from scales. The world feels exciting and wondrous, never just dangerous.
- The antagonist benefits visibly from every sacrifice Karl makes. Kruger gets the WilderBear. Kruger rigs the draw. The gap between antagonist and protagonist widens with every principled choice — that's what makes the resentment compound.
- The cliffhanger introduces a question the promo never hinted at. Royal blood, hidden lineage — this comes from NOWHERE and reframes everything. The audience's model of Karl changes in one sentence.

### Key Takeaways
- **Proves FLBM works without violating the beast HARD BLOCK.** Karl is the only person with a beast. Other students have warrior/mage powers. This is the real world — and it performs ($3.72 CPI, 21.43% CTI).
- **The baby-bird-that-talks is a perfect Tier 1.** The world shifts from "Karl has a useless pet" to "beasts are conscious, intelligent, and choose their moment to reveal themselves." AND Karl's trajectory permanently changes — he now has an evolving partner, not a liability.
- **Dana as power amplifier creates a natural IM.** The Rend scene works because Karl is weak alone — Dana's boost turns his pathetic attack into something devastating. The contrast between "four arcs that bounce off" and "twenty arcs that shred a monster" is what makes it stick.
- **Journey structure with each location introducing a new threat.** Injection → train (beast discovery) → forest (plant monster) → frost giant. Each environment escalates and each produces a different IM flavor.

## Alien Expedition (MVS — Quinn)

**Why this is a reference:** $3.87 CPI — strong acquisition. BUT weak bottom-funnel (13.69% CTI). This is a cautionary reference — it scaled on ad metrics but underperformed on conversion. Demonstrates what happens when a promo leaves the primary setting (academy) and sells a world the listener won't encounter immediately.

**Promo type: FRESH TAKE — with setting risk.** Set entirely outside the academy on an alien planet. This violates the MVS world rule (promos should default to academy setting). The disconnect between "space vampire combat" and the listener's actual entry point (school story) likely explains the weak bottom-funnel despite strong top-funnel.

### Promo Brief
- **Throughline:** Quinn is sent on a military expedition to an alien planet, discovers ancient vampire technology, and must choose between claiming power and surviving his own squad's hostility.
- **Dramatic Container:** Journey-based — briefing → transport → alien planet → temple → extraction crisis
- **Quest:** Find and claim the ancient vampire dagger while surviving both aliens and hostile squadmates.
- **Antagonist:** Captain Reeves / Jackson / Sarah — the squad itself. They bully Quinn throughout and ultimately turn weapons on him.
- **Stakes Character:** The squad (collective) — Quinn must save the people trying to kill him.
- **Cliffhanger:** Dread-forward binary — Quinn surrounded by his own squad's weapons AND an alien armada. Both want him dead. No clean exit.

### IM Map

| Beat | Impact Moment | Type | Tier | Flavor |
|------|--------------|------|------|--------|
| Blood Swipe | Crimson energy erupts from Quinn's fingertips, forming a blade of pure vampire power. Slices through Dalki wrist. | Status Inversion | 2 | blood-blade |
| Ancient temple | Alien engineering mixed with something Quinn's vampire blood recognizes. Dagger on a pedestal, pulsing with inner light — alive, aware, hungry. | System/Artifact Discovery | 1 | vampire-temple |
| Dagger visions | Vampires traveling between stars in ships of living shadow. Ancient wars across galaxies. This dagger forged in the heart of a dying sun. | World Border | 1 | cosmic-vampire-heritage |

- **Strong top-funnel ($3.87 CPI, 43.18% 3s play, 28.08% thruplay) — weak bottom-funnel (13.69% CTI).** The ad hooks hard but the conversion doesn't follow. Likely cause: the promo sells space combat, but the listener enters the show at an academy. Setting disconnect.

- **Cautionary lesson on setting risk.** This is the data behind the MVS world rule. The academy is the listener's entry point. Promos set outside it can acquire cheaply but convert poorly.

## Police Station (Shadow Slave — Sunny)

**Why this is a reference:** First reference outside MVS/FLBM/WBT. Strongest engagement metrics tested (43.64% 3s play, 23.97% thruplay, 3.49% true completion). Proves the system works on horror/supernatural IP. But $8.17 CPI and 10.80% CTI — above CPI target, below CTI floor. A script that HOOKS brilliantly but doesn't convert at target levels.

**Promo type: OPENING COMPRESSION.** Compresses Sunny's nightmare infection, police station surrender, Master Jet encounter, and first nightmare manifestation into a single continuous sequence.

### Promo Brief
- **Throughline:** Sunny has been infected with the Nightmare Spell. When he falls asleep, he'll be pulled into the Nightmare Realm — survive and return with powers, or die and become a monster. He's been fighting sleep for a week. He turns himself in at a police station, but the nightmare starts breaking through before he's ready.
- **Dramatic Container:** Event-based — police station (single location, escalating crisis)
- **Quest:** Stay awake long enough to prepare, then survive the transition into the Nightmare Realm.
- **Antagonist:** Marcus — Awakened bully in the holding cell, fire powers, persistent physical threat who escalates from theft to attempted murder.
- **Stakes Character:** Sunny's mother (dead — her voicemail is the emotional anchor, her promise is the throughline).
- **Cliffhanger:** Hunger-forward — Sunny manifests shadow powers while still human, locks himself in the cell, and surrenders to sleep. "My trial in the nightmare realm was about to begin."

### IM Map

| Beat | Impact Moment | Type | Tier | Flavor | Gate 2 (Fun)? |
|------|--------------|------|------|--------|---------------|
| Reality warp | Police station walls peel back. Mountains erupt. Skeletons rise. Sky fractures. | — | — | — | ❌ Dread, not wonder. Vivid throughline beat. |
| Jet's precedent | "One other person warped reality like this. We still haven't closed the hole." | — | — | — | ❌ Scary lore. Throughline. |
| Nightmare creature | Sleeping man's arms morph into swords. Vision becomes reality. | — | — | — | ❌ Body horror. Sticky but grim. Throughline. |
| Cherry blossom vision | Floating sakura grove. Shadow army bows before a scarred, powerful version of Sunny. "You made her a promise. Keep it." | World Border | 1 | future-self-vision | ✅ Aspirational — the audience sees what Sunny COULD become. |
| Shadow manifestation | Shadow figures flow from vision into reality. Sunny manifests power while still human. Impossible. | Status Inversion | 1 | shadow-power-while-human | ✅ Exhilarating — the "useless" kid does the impossible. |

**Count:** 2 functioning IMs (2 T1, 0 T2), 2 flavors. The other three moments are vivid, sticky, memorable throughline beats that fail Gate 2 — they make the world feel intense and dangerous, not exciting and wondrous.

### Key Takeaways
- **Best engagement metrics tested.** 43.64% 3s play (highest), 23.97% thruplay (highest), 3.49% true completion (highest). The opening hook ("I'm turning myself in!") and the escalating crisis hold the audience better than any other promo. The STRUCTURE works.
- **But CPI ($8.17) and CTI (10.80%) underperform targets.** This is the FTP pattern — strong throughline, weak world-expansion. Most of the vivid moments fail Gate 2 (Fun). The world feels like suffering, not adventure. The audience says "that was intense" not "I want to spend time there."
- **Only 2 functioning IMs despite 5 vivid moments.** This is the clearest proof that the three-gate test matters. Sticky + Distinct is not enough. Without Fun, vivid moments do throughline work but don't drive installs. Shadow Slave has nearly identical IM-to-CTI performance as FTP (2 IMs → 10.80% CTI vs 1-2 IMs → 3.70% CTI).
- **The mother's voicemail is the strongest emotional anchor tested.** Dead mother's last words as a recurring physical object (the phone) that the antagonist eventually destroys. This is why the promo HOLDS despite the horror tone — the throughline is "keep the promise to my mother," not "survive the nightmare."
- **Cautionary lesson: horror/dread tone may have a CTI ceiling.** Even with strong structural execution, if the world feels like a place of suffering rather than adventure, the install impulse weakens. The audience watches to the end (best completion tested) but doesn't cross the threshold to "I want to spend time in this world." More data needed, but this pattern aligns with FTP.
