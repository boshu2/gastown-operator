# ============================================================
# POWERSTART — 3-Second Scroll-Stop Generation
# STATUS: SKILL — Generates 5 candidate opening lines from a locked beat sheet / promo draft
# LOAD: When Workflow 4 reaches Step 1 (Scene 1 opening), or when Workflow 5 is auditing a promo's hook.
# ============================================================

## Purpose

A powerstart is the first thing a cold listener hears on paid social. Its one job is to stop a thumb mid-scroll in under 3 seconds — and plant a question violent enough to pull the listener past second 3 into the promo.

This skill generates 5 candidate powerstart lines aimed at one metric: **3-second play rate.**

---

## Input

- The locked beat sheet or finished promo draft
- The **Primary Driver Thread** — one sentence describing the core tension
- Any known HARD BLOCKS from the series world file

## Output

Five candidate lines, numbered, no commentary. 4 of 5 must be first-person narration voice. The 5th may use any voice. The operator picks one and returns to the calling workflow.

---

## Core Objective

3 seconds. The RIGHT cold thumb stops.

The powerstart has two jobs:

1. **Stop the scroll** — earn 3 seconds of attention on paid social.
2. **Select for the audience** — the listeners who stop should be the listeners who will convert for THIS show. A 66% play rate with perfect audience selection beats a 74% with broad/wrong selection on CPI.

3-second play rate is necessary but not sufficient. A line that maximizes raw play rate by rugpulling — stopping scrolls for an audience that will never install — is a CPI disaster. The powerstart must set up the primary conflict and signal the emotional territory of the show, not just shock.

**The promo shifts to third-person narration after the powerstart.** The powerstart is a first-person vocal burst. Once the scroll is stopped, narration shifts to third person for the remainder. Proven strategy. The powerstart's voice rules apply ONLY to the powerstart.

---

## The 3-Second Gate

Primary framework. Every candidate must pass all three checks before any other evaluation.

### Check 1: The 5-Word Parse

Can a brain process words 1–5 and have an emotional reaction — outrage, disgust, shock, curiosity, disbelief?

**If words 1–5 don't trigger a gut reaction, nothing downstream saves the line.**

### Check 2: Vocal Proximity

Does the line sound like someone grabbed your arm at a bar and started talking? Or like the opening sentence of a novel?

First-person spoken voice outperforms narrated voice by 10–15 points. 4 of 5 candidates must be first-person.

### Check 3: The Tense Gate

Has the worst thing already happened, or is it about to happen?

Post-rupture (past tense, damage done) outperforms pre-rupture (future/conditional) by 8–12 points. Delivered violence stops scrolls. Promised violence gets evaluated.

The tense gate is a ranking tool, not a hard ban. Some pre-rupture lines work. But when choosing between two candidates of similar strength, post-rupture wins.

## The 6 Scroll-Stop Patterns

Lenses on what gut reaction the line is buying in 3 seconds. Not categories to assign mechanically.

**1. Identity Inversion** — who someone WAS vs. what they've BECOME. The gap is the hook. The brain processes status hierarchies instantly.

**2. Accomplished Violence** — something extreme, specific, and irreversible has already been done. The brain doesn't evaluate — it reacts. The more specific and physical, the faster the reaction.

**3. Behavioral Contradiction** — a person does something that makes no sense. "Why would anyone do that?" Pure involuntary curiosity. No injustice, violence, or betrayal needed.

**4. Intimate Betrayal** — someone who should protect the protagonist has already destroyed them. The closer the relationship, the stronger the hook. The WHO matters more than the WHAT.

**5. Institutional Horror** — a system or place is casually monstrous. The gap between institutional normalcy and actual horror. Primary wild-card pattern — often works without first-person voice. Use for the 5th slot.

**6. Forbidden Revelation / Power Rupture** — a secret surfaces, a body changes, a truth detonates. Most vulnerable to jargon failure. If the reveal requires system knowledge to be shocking, it won't stop a scroll.

Craft note on reveals: they should SNAP, not SUMMARIZE. "He hadn't been drunk in twenty years" → snap. "He had been pretending to be a drunk for twenty years" → summary. Same fact, different gravity.

Craft note on secrets: the rupture verb for a secret is NOT the hypothetical exposure ("if they find out" = conditional, no rupture). It's either the moment the secret nearly breaks (near-exposure event) or the moment the secret's cost becomes visible (what hiding it has already cost). "If they find out I have a dragon, they'll burn me alive" (48%) is conditional math. The secret hasn't ruptured. Find the moment it almost did, or find what keeping it has already destroyed — start there.

## Selection Rule

Pick the earliest moment in the promo that combines: **5-word gut punch + clear stakes + genre signal + forward pull.**

Scene 1 > Scene 2 > earlier beats > later. But Scene 1 is not mandatory — if the real viral image lives deeper, pull it forward. Across a batch of 5, at least two candidates should be grounded in Scene 1.

---

## Hard Bans

- **Third-person voice in slots 1–4**
- **"Was about to" / conditional future in words 1–7**
- **Retrospective timestamps** ("when I was 14," "that day")
- **Soft state-openers in words 1–3** (I was / I had / I held / I watched / They say)
- **Condition/state openers** ("I was born with," "I grew up") — states get filed, events stop scrolls
- **Observer protagonist** ("my classmate," not "me")
- **Adjective-loading** ("cruel stepmother," "brutally smacked") — if you need adjectives to make the violence register, replace the image
- **Exposition, jargon, cliché, mush**
- **Protagonist boasting** — vulnerability > competence for 3-second play rate
- **Setup-punchline delay** — if the scroll-stop requires holding a setup in memory, the line is broken for social
- **"But I made one mistake" and variants** — overused, empty forward-pull with no image

---

## Temperature Bars

1. *"That's interesting."* — scroll-neutral. **Draft.** Not a candidate.
2. *"This is already out of control."* — scroll-stop. **Checkpoint.** Not a candidate yet.
3. *"Something has already gone too far."* — install-grade. **Ship bar.**

Bar 2 is where the generator is most tempted to stop. Every bar-2 line gets one more push.

---

## Generation Process

### The Core Problem This Process Solves

The natural instinct is to APPROACH the moment — set up the sacrifice, frame the significance, build to the betrayal. This produces sacrifice-first lines, framing openers, and mid-event onsets.

**The fix: find the moment, then delete everything before it. Start in the wreckage, not on the road to the wreckage.**

---

**Step 1 — Find the 3–5 strongest RUPTURE POINTS.**

A rupture point is the specific instant where something becomes irreversible — the swallow, the punch, the departure, the reveal. For each, write the AFTERMATH in 3–5 words. Not what happened — what the world looks like one second after.

**Step 2 — Write each line starting INSIDE the aftermath.**

1. Take the aftermath fragment.
2. First person, spoken voice.
3. The first 5 words must be INSIDE the aftermath.

**The rupture verb test:** find the first verb in your line. Is it the rupture itself?

- ✗ *"I crawled through frozen hills…"* — "crawled" is sacrifice. Rupture is 15 words away.
- ✗ *"I gave her the only thing that could save my life…"* — "gave" is setup. Rupture is a clause away.
- ✓ *"She swallowed my herb"* — "swallowed" IS the rupture. Line starts in the wreckage.
- ✓ *"They turned me into their towel boy"* — "turned" IS the rupture.
- ✓ *"Mercenaries burned my unit alive"* — "burned" IS the rupture.

**If the first verb is not the rupture itself, delete everything before the rupture verb and begin there.**

**Step 2b — Add a worsening beat (if earned).** The beat must make the first clause MORE painful, unjust, irreversible, or dangerous. Not extend, explain, or atmosphere. One beat is usually enough. Two max. Three stacks information instead of escalating.

**Step 2c — Run the 3-Second Gate.** 5-word parse → vocal proximity → tense gate.

**Step 3 — Batch review.** 4+ first-person? Pattern variety? Shape variety? Every line starts on a rupture verb?

**Step 4 — Escalation pass.** Push each line one step past where it first feels shippable. Start later, add consequence, snap reveals, increase specificity, make outcomes irreversible. If it doesn't feel worse after the pass, discard.

**The 10-show test:** Could this line belong to 10 other shows? If yes → push specificity.

**Second-sentence check:** Beat 2 must WORSEN beat 1. Not extend, explain, or atmosphere.

**Step 5 — Rank** via the priority hierarchy: 3-Second Gate > scroll-stop > genre signal > forward pull > cleanliness.

**Step 6 — Output.** Five lines. Numbered. No commentary.

## When In Doubt

Read the line aloud. Ask: *"Would you stop scrolling for this?"*

Not "is this interesting?" Not "is this well-written?" Would you stop scrolling?

---

## Handoff

Output the 5 candidates. Operator selects one. The promo shifts to third-person narration after the opening. Skill ends at selection.
