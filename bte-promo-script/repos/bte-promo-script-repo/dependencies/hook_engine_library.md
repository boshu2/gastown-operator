## ENGINE 1: SECRET IDENTITY (Threat of Exposure)

### Core Tension
"They almost found out."

### Beat Logic
- Protagonist has a hidden power/identity they cannot reveal. The power gap is extreme — secretly the strongest person in the room, treated as the weakest.
- Each beat is a **near-exposure event.** The secret almost surfaces — then the protagonist pulls back, absorbs punishment to stay hidden.
- Escalation on two axes: **(a) how close the secret gets to breaking** and **(b) who is watching when it almost breaks.**
- **Two simultaneous threat layers in EVERY beat: PHYSICAL DANGER + DETECTION DANGER.** If a beat only has one, it is underperforming.

### The Central Question Contract
**"Why would someone this powerful take this?"**

Planted in the first 30 seconds. NEVER resolves inside the promo. Each near-exposure raises the cost of discovery. The audience is pulled by inevitability — they know the secret will break, they need to see HOW.

### Beat 1 Minimum Payload
Must deliver ALL in a single unbroken sequence:
- The secret established (what they're hiding)
- The cost of exposure established (what happens if caught)
- Physical danger present
- Detection danger present or implied
- Antagonist introduced or felt

### Escalation Table

| Position | Physical Danger | Detection Danger | Who's Watching |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | Bullied, attacked, humiliated | Power almost surfaces reflexively | Low-stakes witnesses |
| Early | Forced into test where hiding requires damage | Performs suspiciously well | Antagonist paying attention |
| Mid | Ally threatened — must act or let them suffer | Ally discovers secret (blast radius grows) | Detection-capable character enters |
| Late | Ambush — physically cornered | Multiple near-exposures rapid succession | Detection character in the room |
| Breaking Point | Will die if they don't use power | Using power means guaranteed exposure | The one person who must NEVER know is watching |

### Dual-Layer Beat Test
- ✅ Both physical + detection present → engine running
- 🔶 Only one layer → add the missing layer or cut
- ❌ Neither → beat doesn't belong in this engine

### Cliffhanger Shape
**Forced binary: survive OR stay hidden. Both catastrophic. No clean exit.**

### ⚠️ Engine-Specific Risk: The Inversion Trap
If the protagonist hides too passively for too long, the audience loses respect. Fix: near-reveal every 2–3 beats, skill demonstrations that don't expose the secret, active concealment beats.

### Why It Converts
- Instant hookability — no backstory needed
- Non-resolving question — protects Q2 retention
- Dual-layer tension prevents pacing plateaus
- Audience superiority position (dramatic irony)

### Performance Data
- **MVS Weapons (Quinn):** $4.22 CPI, 30% CTI, Q2: 86.49%, Q4: 84.88%. STATUS: PROVEN.
- **Retention note:** Flattest retention curve tested.

### Best Used When
- Protagonist starts with power already present
- World has a detection system
- Specific person who must never discover the secret
- Reason to hide beyond self-preservation
- Secret has a physical tell that gets harder to suppress

### Best Combined With
- **Self-Sacrifice** — hides power AND sacrifices to protect someone
- **Temptation of Power** — secret power has corrupting influence

### Impact Moment Affinity

Naturally IM-dense when the central event is institutional (ceremony, trial, selection ritual). The "forced performance" structure means the protagonist must interact with world systems under pressure — each interaction is a potential IM.

**IM risk:** If the central event is private (back-alley fight, prison cell), IM density drops sharply. The engine needs a PUBLIC, SYSTEM-RICH event container.

**Natural IM types:** Status Inversion (near-reveals), Institutional Depth (event rules constraining protagonist), Power-Ceiling Reveal (other characters demonstrating scale).

### NOT the Same As: Underdog Awakening
Different emotional contracts. Awakening asks "how strong can they get?" Secret Identity asks "how long can they keep hiding?" Secret Identity's central question is inherently non-resolving, giving it a structural retention advantage.

## ENGINE 2: SELF-SACRIFICE / LOYALTY

### Core Tension
"He chose the hard thing again."

### Beat Logic
- Protagonist bonded to something the world says to abandon. The bond is visible, public, considered a weakness.
- Each beat offers an **easier exit.** The protagonist refuses every time — each refusal costs more.
- **The world REWARDS pragmatism.** Characters who take the easy path get ahead in real-time.
- Emotional anchor (WHY they sacrifice) planted in **first 60 seconds** through action.

### The Central Question Contract
**"How much will they give up for this bond?"**

Compounding structure — never resolves because costs keep escalating.

### Beats 1–2 Minimum Payload
Across the first two beats, establish:
- Bond demonstration through action — the protagonist sacrifices for the bond object
- The tenderness moment — between punishment and sacrifice, a beat of care. This is the scroll-stopper. Do not skip it.
- Antagonist introduced or felt

The offer/refusal, rival contrast, and quest launch can arrive in successive beats. The opening just needs to show: this person will sacrifice for this bond.

### The Rival as Contrast, Not Villain
- Direct peer, not already elite
- Succeeds by taking the easier path
- If turns hostile, from rejection not malice
- Trajectory visible in every stage

### Stakes Character as Character, Not Effect
- Physical behavior mandatory (moves, reacts, gestures)
- Reciprocal loyalty VISIBLE
- Emotional reactions to protagonist's state
- Test: audience wants to protect this creature within 10 seconds?

### The Escalating Cost Principle

The protagonist's first refusal should cost less than their final sacrifice. The stakes ladder applies to the offers themselves — each one demands more, and the protagonist pays more to refuse.

**Dragon Battle example:** Karl turns down a WilderBear (material cost — stays weak). By the end, he shields his beasts from dragonfire with his own body (life cost — may die). The refusals escalate from "stays weak" to "may die." That's the engine working.

### Anticipation Beat Shape
Recognition of worthiness — not a power demonstration. The specific thing sacrificed for produces the reward. Must be a PHYSICAL ACTION.

### Cliffhanger Shape
Sacrifice pays off — but resolution WITHHELD. Validate the sacrifice + open a new world + withhold the full answer. Dominant emotion: HUNGER layered on JUSTICE.

### ⚠️ Engine-Specific Risk: The Repetition Trap
Vary sacrifice TYPE (social → material → physical → existential). Vary WHO demands it. Insert action beats that TEST the bond.

### Why It Converts
- Universal loyalty empathy
- Non-resolving question
- Clean moral forks
- Compounding investment
- Earned payoff

### Performance Data
- **FLBM Dragon Battle (Karl):** $3.64 CPI, 23.86% CTI, Q2: 81.69%, Q3: 98.56%, Q4: 95.34%. STATUS: PROVEN.
- **WBT Rotting Boy (Ren):** Script 1: 19% CTI. Script 2: 12% CTI. Same beat sheet — execution difference (IM space). STATUS: PROVEN (execution-sensitive).
- **Retention note:** Strongest Q2→Q3→Q4 curve tested.

### Best Used When
- Visible bond others consider a weakness
- Bond object chose the protagonist
- Environment punishes loyalty, rewards self-interest
- Public arena or competition
- Bond object has hidden potential

### Best Combined With
- **Underdog Awakening** — loyalty unlocks progression
- **Secret Identity** — sacrifices to protect + must hide why capable
- **Countdown / Ticking Clock** — refusals burn time

### Impact Moment Affinity

Generates IMs through the bond object and the world's system of value. The beast/creature/companion IS the IM delivery vehicle — every new behavior, ability, or display expands what's possible.

**IM risk:** If the bond object is passive or behaviorally flat, IM density suffers. The bond object must DO surprising things.

**Natural IM types:** System/Artifact Discovery (bond object's hidden potential), Institutional Depth (academy ranking/ceremonies), Status Inversion (the "weak" bond object does the impossible), Power-Ceiling Reveal (rival demonstrates scale).

**Critical lesson (WBT Rotting Boy):** Two scripts from the same beat sheet produced a 7-point CTI gap (19% vs 12%). Same IM events. Difference was execution — Script 1 let moments unfold through discovery (silence before, space after). Script 2 narrated moments from above (announced significance, explained mechanics, described emotions). IM quality depends on SPACE around the moment.

### NOT the Same As: Revenge / Justice Denied
Opposite emotional contracts. Revenge: "will they get even?" Self-Sacrifice: "will their loyalty be rewarded?"

## ENGINE 3: UNDERDOG AWAKENING / PROGRESSION

### Core Tension
"He was nothing — now he's becoming something. But what is it turning him into?"

### Beat Logic
- Protagonist starts at absolute zero. Humiliation is public, specific, physical.
- A hidden system activates. Demands tasks. Each reward reveals darker implications.
- **The progression loop IS the engine.** Each cycle: mini-cliffhanger → payoff. Dopamine every 2–3 minutes. Macro tension keeps climbing.
- Antagonist is a personal bully with persistent physical presence.
- Each power-up must cost something visible.
- Progression loop must never go dormant for more than 60–90 seconds.

### The Central Question Contract
**"What is this system turning him into?"**

### Beat 1 Minimum Payload
Must deliver ALL:
- Protagonist at absolute zero (public, specific humiliation)
- Antagonist present and personal
- The system hinted or activated
- A cost established (what the protagonist risks by engaging)

### Escalation Logic

| Track | What Escalates | Audience Feeling |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|
| **Power Track** | Rewards bigger, flashier, more dangerous | Exhilaration |
| **Cost Track** | Tasks darker, more morally ambiguous | Unease |

Both must climb together. The sweet spot: cheering and nervous at the same time.

### Cliffhanger Shape
System crosses a threshold — transformation begins but is NOT completed. The audience sees the BEGINNING of what the protagonist becomes, never the full picture.

### ⚠️ Engine-Specific Risk: Tension Resolution at Activation
The moment power activates can feel like an ANSWER. If the audience feels satisfied, Q2 collapses.

DATA: MVS Awakening Q2: 60.3% (question resolves). SW Smack Q2: 85.0% (activation delayed, made paradoxical). Dragon Battle Q2: 81.69% (never resolves). Delta between resolved and unresolved: 21–25 points.

**PROVEN FIX (validated by SW Smack — $4.79 CPI, 17.61% CTI, 85% Q2):**

Delay activation as long as structurally possible. When activation arrives, make it **confusing, taboo, or paradoxical — not triumphant.** The audience should feel "wait, WHAT?" not "hell yes." Triumph reads as resolution. Confusion reads as a new question.

Specifically:
- **Delay:** Push first real power manifestation past the 40% mark. Fill pre-activation beats with injustice density and world-building IMs so the audience is invested before the system arrives.
- **Defamiliarize:** When power activates, make the mechanism strange enough that the audience's first reaction is curiosity, not satisfaction. SW Smack: kissing transfers power — the audience doesn't cheer, they lean in confused.
- **Complicate:** Activation should immediately create a social, moral, or physical problem the power doesn't solve. The power answers "can he fight back?" while opening "but at what cost?" or "but now what?"

The earlier hypothetical fixes remain valid as additional options:
- Lead with a non-resolving hook question that survives activation
- Make activation catastrophic — the power itself creates a bigger problem
- Fragment the activation across multiple beats
- Layer a ticking clock or external pressure that the activation doesn't solve

Any combination works. The test: after the protagonist gains power, does the audience have an URGENT reason to keep watching — or do they feel the contract has been fulfilled?

### Performance Data
- **MVS Awakening (Quinn):** $3.41 CPI, 19.96% CTI, Q2: 60.3% ⚠️. STATUS: PROVEN (with Q2 risk).
- **Retention note:** Q2 bleed is structural — the opening question resolves. Q3/Q4 recover once the quest loop re-engages.
- **SW Smack (Ethan):** $4.79 CPI, 17.61% CTI, Q2: 85.0%, True Completion: 14.34%. STATUS: PROVEN. Delayed, paradoxical activation fixes Q2. Universal-emotion opening (romantic betrayal + powerless identity) drives 57.8% 3-sec play rate. Question-multiplier cliffhanger holds Q4 at 98.69%.
- **TSS Phoenix (Serena):** $3.40 CPI, 22.0% CTI, Q2: 82.0%, True Completion: 17.41%. STATUS: PROVEN. Domain-varied progression (5 distinct power domains) generates highest IM flavor variety in catalog. Question-multiplier cliffhanger holds Q4 at 99.12%. Combines Underdog Awakening with Revenge/Justice Denied elements.

### Best Used When
- Protagonist at literal zero in a power-ranked world
- Progression system with visible milestones
- Power source has dark side or cost
- Persistent personal bully present before power arrives
- Larger forces would hunt protagonist if they knew

### Best Combined With
- **Secret Identity** — awakens power but must hide it
- **Temptation of Power** — progression darkens past moral threshold
- **Self-Sacrifice** — gains power but uses it to protect someone weaker

### Impact Moment Affinity

Inherent IM potential through the quest-reward cycle. Each new ability is a potential System/Artifact IM. Risk: progression beats become MECHANICAL (stat increase) rather than WONDROUS (shadow blades growing from fingertips).

**IM risk:** Numerical rewards (+5 strength, level up) don't pass the sticky test. Rewards must be VISIBLE, PHYSICAL, and SURPRISING. Vulnerable to IM deserts in the mid-section where quest-completion beats are mechanically interesting but don't expand the world.

**Natural IM types:** System/Artifact Discovery (each ability as system reveal — but only if physically surprising), Status Inversion (powerless kid manifests something impossible), Power-Ceiling Reveal (system hints at endgame), Institutional Depth (if awakening happens inside a formal event).

### NOT the Same As: Temptation of Power
Opposite starting points. Awakening starts from zero (exhilaration that darkens). Temptation starts with power (moral dread from Beat 1).

## MERGE FORMAT

A merge splices two proven promo structures into one script. The opening section uses one proven structure's shape; the main body uses another. This is distinct from adaptation (same structure, different show) and BSV (same structure, same show, different angle).

**Proof case:** MVS Level Vampire + Weapons — $3.03 CPI, 26.13% Thruplay, 20.8% CTI. Opens with Level Zero's book-opening compression (protagonist at absolute zero, system activation, early quest loop), then transitions into the Weapons framework (tournament container, escalating combat, bond-driven sacrifice, secret identity reveal).

### When to Use
- A show has two proven promos with different structural strengths, and neither alone is optimal for a new audience segment.
- The opening of one structure solves a known weakness in the other (e.g., Level Zero's immediate system hook compensates for Weapons' slower build).
- The show's world is deep enough that a longer promo (merge formats tend to run long) doesn't exhaust source material.

### Structural Risks
1. **Dual resolution points.** Each original structure has its own resolution shape. The splice point often creates a mid-promo resolution that bleeds Q2. Level Vampire Q2: 75.0% — lower than either parent structure's best. Mitigation: treat the splice point as a BECAUSE link, not a scene break. The opening structure's cliffhanger must CAUSE the main structure's inciting event.
2. **Pacing valley at splice.** The transition from one structure to another can feel like a restart. The audience signed up for one story and gets handed a different one. Mitigation: carry at least one unresolved thread from the opening structure across the splice — a character, a question, a threat.
3. **Q4 resolution leakage.** If the merge delivers both structures' payoffs, the cliffhanger resolves too much. Level Vampire Q4: 82.95% — worst tested Q4. The secret breaking and full power display delivered satisfaction, killing install urgency. Mitigation: the merge's cliffhanger must follow §5.13 (Resolution Withholding) using ONLY the main structure's payoff shape. The opening structure's threads remain fully unresolved.

### Performance Data
- **MVS Level Vampire + Weapons:** $3.03 CPI, 20.8% CTI, 26.13% Thruplay. Q2: 75.0%, Q4: 82.95%. Opening structure: Level Zero. Main structure: Weapons. STATUS: PROVEN (with Q2 and Q4 risks from dual resolution).

### Not the Same As
- **BSV** — same structure, same show, new angle. A BSV doesn't change the structural skeleton.
- **Adaptation** — same structure, different show. An adaptation transplants one skeleton whole.
- **Merge** — two different skeletons joined. The structural DNA changes at the splice point.

## HYPOTHETICAL ENGINES (UNTESTED)

Move to main section only after testing with performance data.

### Countdown / Ticking Clock
- Core tension: "Time is running out and every move costs something."
- Beat logic: Hard deadline with cascading obstacles.
- Cliffhanger: Clock hits zero mid-action.
- Risk: Can feel mechanical without emotional anchor.
- IM affinity: Low inherent density. Clock is a pacing device. Moments must come from obstacles and environments.

### Revenge / Justice Denied
- Core tension: "They took everything and no one will help."
- Beat logic: Protagonist wronged. Every attempt at justice blocked.
- Cliffhanger: In position to take revenge — but cost is becoming the thing they hate.
- Risk: Repetitive if every beat is "blocked again."
- IM affinity: Low-to-medium inherent density. Revenge tends to narrow the world rather than expand it. Forged Through Pain (~4% CTI) had severe IM desert, but the low CTI may be an IM execution problem rather than an engine problem. A revenge promo with strong IM density and world-expansion is untested — could perform. The engine itself is not disqualified.

### Temptation of Power / Moral Corruption
- Core tension: "The power works, but it's changing them."
- Beat logic: Each use solves a problem and costs humanity.
- Cliffhanger: Using the power is the only option — but it completes the transformation.
- Risk: Needs precise calibration. Too dark = disengagement.
- IM affinity: Medium. Power manifestations can be IMs if each use looks and feels physically different.

# PROPOSING A NEW ENGINE

When source material doesn't fit a proven engine, a new engine can be proposed — but must be defined with the same structural clarity:

1. **Core tension** — one sentence the audience feels
2. **Beat logic** — what each beat does to pressure that tension
3. **Central question contract** — what the audience is waiting for, and why it doesn't resolve
4. **Beat 1 minimum payload** — what must be established in the opening
5. **Escalation logic** — how stakes compound
6. **Cliffhanger shape** — the specific unresolvable fork
7. **IM affinity** — what types of Impact Moments the engine naturally produces, and where IM deserts are likely

Default to proven engines. New engines carry untested risk. Only propose when the material genuinely demands a structure none of the proven engines can serve.
