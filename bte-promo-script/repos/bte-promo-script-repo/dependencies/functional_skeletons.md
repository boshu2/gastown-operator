# FUNCTIONAL SKELETONS — LIBRARY
# Proven, documented beat-orders extracted from winning promos. Each skeleton is a STRUCTURE to inherit, not flavor to consult.

---

## WHAT THIS FILE IS

A functional skeleton is the **abstracted beat-order** of a promo that has already converted — stripped to its structural functions and held as a reusable template. Each beat is recorded as a **function** (the bolded label + abstract description of the structural job that beat does) plus a **source example** (the original show's literal execution, kept only as a shape reference).

**The function transfers. The example does not.** When a skeleton is pinned to a new show, you transplant each beat's *structural job* (injustice / reversal / accidental-contact / moral-choice / inversion / reveal) and pour the target show's own cast, container, combat, power, and IM flavor into it. A reskinned Dragon Battle that keeps the dragon is a fidelity failure; one that keeps "accidental contact with the force that later inverts status" is a correct transplant.

## WHO READS THIS FILE

- **W1 (Adaptation / Pinning / Extraction)** — the proper home for choosing a skeleton, inheriting its engine + structure, and locking show fidelity. New skeletons extracted from winning promos are documented HERE.
- **W2 (Skeleton) — Functional Skeleton Inheritance Mode** — loads the pinned/named skeleton and builds beats 1:1 against its order and functions. The skeleton owns ORDER; the Spine owns CONTENT.
- **W3 (Beat Sheet)** — `Functional Skeleton:` header records which skeleton was inherited (honored / drifted).
- **W5 (Analysis / Deep Dive)** — verifies skeleton inheritance: every source function represented, in order, function preserved.

## BEAT 0 CONVENTION

Several skeletons carry an **optional Beat 0 — Powerstart / Cold open / In medias res**. It loads secret + stakes + emotional texture (or the endpoint) before the story begins. It can be cut without breaking structure, but when it lands it is the 3-second-play-rate driver. Inheritance preserves Beat 0 as optional unless the operator drops it.

# WEAPONS / DRAFT SELECTION

## *Core hook: Secret identity*

**Beat 1 — Protagonist at zero.** Draft board calls his name, room laughs, Erin corrects from the front — "He's a Level One." The world's hierarchy is established through what it does to him publicly. Three hundred people recalculate his worth in real time and land on zero.

**Beat 2 — First Reversal (public, contested) → Punished into being worse.** The sidestep. The clap-back. The room shifts — laughing at Rex instead of Quinn for half a second. Then Loop locks him in the telekinetic hold and Rex spits on his face. The reversal doesn't just fail to stick — it provokes a punishment worse than the original humiliation. The protagonist's position is now lower than where Beat 1 started. Trying to push back made it worse.

**Beat 3 — Love interest steps in, redirects antagonist's rage to them.** Erin's ice spike between Rex's legs. She didn't defend Quinn out of affection — she defended squad property. But the effect is the same: Rex's rage transfers from Quinn to her. "You're going to regret that, Erin." The antagonist now has a target who can actually be hurt, and the protagonist knows it's his fault. The love interest is introduced through an act that puts her in danger on his behalf.

**Beat 4 — Intro institutional stakes.** Leo steps out. One word silences three hundred people. "Ten students die a year." Phase One, Phase Two, squads dissolved. The stakes shift from social (embarrassment, status) to existential (death, elimination). The arena the protagonist is trapped in just became lethal.

**Beat 5 — First potential reveal of the secret.** The scanner. It climbs past every known level and crashes. The proctor shrugs it off — but Leo is watching from the observation deck. The secret almost surfaces through a system the protagonist can't control. The audience holds their breath. The near-miss tells them the secret is too big to stay hidden, and the infrastructure of this world will keep trying to expose it.

**Beat 6 — Ally bond on false foundation.** Erin sends Quinn first in Phase One. "Level playing field. You can do this." Then — "I know what you are." Blood goes cold. "A fighter." She's wrong about why, but not wrong that he is one. She shows the scar, reveals she's been hunted as an Original, pledges loyalty. "I have your back. No matter what." The bond is real. The premise is a misidentification. The audience knows this will detonate.

**Beat 7 — Reversal earned, then stolen.** Quinn fights Loop clean. No abilities, no vampire reflexes. Lands shots, earns the crowd's silence. Then Loop cheats — telekinesis mid-duel, same technique from Beat 2. Quinn reads it this time, times the counter to the focus split, drops Loop with one clean shot. The arena goes quiet. Three hundred people recalculating. Then Rex stands up: "He cheated." The actual cheater's squad leader demands justice. The crowd buys it. Quinn can't defend himself without answering questions that kill him. The win is narratively stolen by the person who broke the rules.

**Beat 8 — The person who chose you is about to die because they chose you.** Phase Two. Abilities live. Rex's squad doesn't try to win — they isolate Erin. Loop freezes her in the same hold from Beat 2. They beat her with intent to kill. Joints, head, bone-breaking strikes. This is Rex collecting on the debt from Beat 3 — the ice spike, the pecker line, the laughter aimed at him. Leo sees it from the deck but can't reach her in time. The protagonist has to choose: stay hidden and watch her die, or move and lose everything.

**Beat 9 — Containment failure.** Quinn moves. Shatters the combat barrier the Academy built to contain Level Five power. Dismantles Rex's squad. Breaks Loop's concentration, frees Erin. Then the System fires without permission — evolution, threshold crossed, vampire nature fully awake. Vision shifts, arena cracks, air bends. The reversal is total and public and irreversible. Every student saw it. Leo recognizes something ancient. The secret isn't leaking anymore. It detonated. The resolution creates a crisis worse than everything before it — the protagonist got what the audience wanted and it might destroy him.

**Beat 10 — CTA.** Questions mapped to the new threats, not the old mysteries. The school is hunting his secret. Leo saw something he recognizes. Erin will find out the bond was built on a lie. The convert question isn't "what powers does he get" — it's "what happens now that the thing he was hiding is the thing everyone saw."

# IMF5

## *Core hook: Weak → Strong (or) Poor → Rich*

**Beat 0 (optional) — Powerstart.** Pre-scene hook that loads the secret, the stakes, and the emotional texture before the story begins. Functions as a cold open — can be cut without breaking structure, but when it works, it's the 3-second play rate.

**Beat 1 — Protagonist at zero.** The world's hierarchy is established through what it does to the protagonist, not through explanation. Empathy is built through injustice — the audience feels the system before they understand it. The protagonist's lack is visible to everyone. e.g. Quinn has no power in a world where power is everything, and the person closest to him leaves because of it.

**Beat 2 — The Summons.** Something arrives that promises to change the protagonist's position — but the audience doesn't know in which direction. Could be salvation, could be worse. Curiosity replaces pure empathy as the retention driver. e.g. A military message pulls Quinn somewhere he doesn't belong, with no explanation.

**Beat 3 — First Reversal (Private).** The protagonist enters a space that should reject them and instead gets recognized. But the reversal is seen only by gatekeepers, not by the people who matter. The audience gets a taste of vindication — enough to believe the turn is real, not enough to feel satisfied. e.g. Commander Miller goes from dismissive to deferential, but nobody from Quinn's life sees it.

**Beat 4 — Reversal Sealed.** A secondary character tries to undo or challenge the reversal, and gets overruled. This confirms the reversal wasn't a fluke — but also re-locks it. The protagonist is told to keep it secret. The audience now holds information the world doesn't. e.g. Bridger accuses Quinn of theft, Miller forces her to apologize, then warns Quinn to tell no one.

**Beat 5 — Injustice Reload.** The protagonist returns to the world that doesn't know what's changed. The same people who degraded them in Beat 1 degrade them again — but now the audience knows something they don't. Every insult lands differently because of the dramatic irony. A potential ally surfaces but the protagonist can't accept help yet. e.g. Jennifer and Troy destroy the phone, humiliate Quinn publicly. Layla defends him but he can't even afford dinner.

**Beat 6 — Social Trap + Dramatic Irony Engine.** The protagonist is pulled into a social situation where their low status will be exposed again. Meanwhile, characters who don't know the protagonist trash-talk them in a parallel scene. The audience is watching two trains approach the same intersection. e.g. Suzan tries to cancel dinner because Quinn is powerless. Scarlet mocks the "loser from the armory" not knowing he's walking toward her table.

**Beat 7 — Emotional Bottom + Activation Trigger.** The protagonist breaks. Not publicly — privately. The lowest moment is internal. Something physical happens in the breakdown that unknowingly triggers the mechanism of change. The audience watches the transformation begin without the protagonist realizing it. e.g. Quinn punches a mirror, bleeds on the book. The book reacts. He doesn't notice.

**Beat 8 — Detonation / Cliffhanger.** External crisis and internal transformation collide simultaneously. The world breaks open (threat) and the protagonist's hidden potential activates (promise). Resolution is initiated but never delivered. The audience knows something massive just happened but not what it means. e.g. Dalki attack, the book pulses, "Activating the Vampire System." No explanation of what the system does.

# PAWN SHOP

## *Core hook: Hidden/Secret Identity (or) Weak → Strong (or) Poor → Rich*

**Beat 0 (optional) — Cold open / Powerstart.** Narrator voice establishes the protagonist at rock bottom and promises a massive status reversal. Sets the emotional contract: you're about to watch a nobody become the most powerful person in the room. Functions as a trailer for the promo itself.

**Beat 1 — The world prices the protagonist at zero.** Aiden is broke, about to be evicted, can't afford rent or a birthday gift. The lack is economic. The world doesn't hate him — it just doesn't register him. He exists below the threshold of mattering.

**Beat 2 — A mystery object signals the protagonist is worth more than the world thinks.** The pawn shop scene. Aiden tries to sell the ring and gets a gun pulled on him. The shopkeeper's terror is the first evidence that the protagonist's perceived worthlessness is a lie. The audience knows something is hidden. The protagonist doesn't.

**Beat 3 — Flashback anchors the mystery to a personal loss and plants a revenge target.** Father's murder. The ring's origin. The promise to remember the killer's face. This beat gives the mystery emotional stakes — the object isn't just valuable, it's connected to a trauma the protagonist needs to resolve. The scar is planted as a visual the audience will track.

**Beat 4 — A personal antagonist humiliates the protagonist publicly, and the protagonist absorbs it.** Derek at the strip club. The humiliation is witnessed. The protagonist has the ability to fight back but can't afford the cost. The swallowed response builds audience pressure that needs release later.

**Beat 5 — The protagonist's last source of stability betrays them.** Vanessa's birthday party. The girlfriend dumps him publicly for the antagonist. The one good thing in his life is taken, and the taking is performed as entertainment for an audience. Rock bottom. This is the lowest the protagonist will go before the reversal begins.

**Beat 6 — A forced entanglement embeds the protagonist in a world above his station.** The Ava/Harvey marriage ultimatum. The protagonist is shoved into proximity with wealth and power he doesn't understand, for reasons that have nothing to do with who he actually is. This plants the love interest and a secondary antagonist (Shane) while positioning the protagonist inside the world he's about to inherit — before he knows it's his.

**Beat 7 — The secret is revealed to the protagonist. The world inverts.** Cyrus recognizes the ring. Legacy reveal — billions, a mafia empire, all of it belongs to Aiden by birthright. In the same beat, the protagonist recognizes the scar in the photograph. The man who killed his father was his father's partner. The reversal and the revenge target arrive simultaneously. The protagonist chooses to hide what he knows.

**Beat 8 — The protagonist earns validation through action, not inheritance.** Ben Jameson fight. The legacy was told to him in Beat 7 — this beat makes him prove it physically. Converts birthright into credibility. The audience now believes the protagonist can operate in this world, not just that he's entitled to.

**Beat 9 — The protagonist's status rises while an external threat mobilizes against them.** New car, bank account, allies, a job. Intercut with Dominic Grayson learning the Tiger is back and ordering a kill. The rise and the danger are simultaneous — the audience gets satisfaction from the reversal while knowing it's already compromised.

**Beat 10 — The protagonist discovers the people protecting him may be the actual threat.** Club Cain. Rachel with Dominic. Raymond alive. Everything the protagonist was told is now in question. The trust structure detonates. The promo ends with every alliance unreliable and a gun at the protagonist's head. The audience is pulled into the app not by action but by the need to know who was lying.

# SUPERMARKET

## *Core hook: Poor → Rich (or) Weak → Strong (or) Hidden/Secret Identity*

**Beat 0 (optional) — Cold open / In medias res.** The protagonist is mid-crisis — shot, bleeding, glowing, holding a stolen gun while the love interest has a rifle to her head. Establishes the endpoint so the audience spends the entire promo anticipating the arrival. Everything that follows is "how did we get here."

**Beat 1 — The world prices the protagonist at zero despite what they've earned.** Car showroom. The protagonist is a veteran — he's already paid a price the world should respect. It doesn't. The lack is economic, but the injustice is deeper: the gap between what he's given and what he gets back. The pendant pulses for the first time — a background signal nobody understands yet, including the protagonist.

**Beat 2 — An external crisis creates a deadline the protagonist can't meet alone.** Mr. Lin is dying. A million dollars. Forty-seven dollars in his pocket. Then the anonymous payment — someone covered it, no name. The protagonist's world splits in two: the world he understood (where nobody helps people like him) and the world that just intervened (where someone powerful is watching). The mystery of who and why becomes the second engine alongside the injustice.

**Beat 3 — A powerful stranger offers a deal the protagonist can't refuse because someone else's life depends on it.** Robert Hart. The benefactor who paid. The offer: work for me, follow orders, become a millionaire. No job description. The protagonist's freedom is exchanged for his mentor's survival. From this point forward, every humiliation the protagonist endures is load-bearing — he's not just absorbing it, he's buying someone else's life with his dignity.

**Beat 4 — The mentor warns the protagonist and deepens the supernatural mystery.** Mr. Lin in the hospital. Trained Brian, warns him about the rich, tells him to trust the pendant. The pendant glows visibly for the first time. The mentor knows more than he's revealing. This beat establishes that the protagonist has been prepared for something specific — and the preparation predates the job offer.

**Beat 5 — The assignment is revealed to be beneath the protagonist, and the cost of failure is restated.** Hart estate. The million-dollar job is babysitting a college student undercover. Three predecessors failed. The gap between what the protagonist is (a combat veteran with supernatural potential) and what he's being asked to do (carry bags, attend classes) creates comic tension — but underneath it, the stakes are lethal. If the girl rejects him, the deal is off, and Mr. Lin dies.

**Beat 6 — The love interest rejects the protagonist on sight, and the protagonist cannot retaliate.** Stella's entrance. She treats him like trash. One day. The structural trap: every previous humiliation (salesman, showroom) the protagonist could have walked away from. This one he can't. His pride is the currency and someone else's life is the price. The audience feels the compression — the protagonist is squeezed between contempt from above and mortality from below.

**Beat 7 — The protagonist demonstrates lethal competence in a social setting, and the love interest witnesses it.** Party. Peter Kingston. Three bodies in three seconds, then a knife disarm. This beat releases the accumulated pressure from Beats 1–6. The protagonist's real capability becomes visible for the first time — not to the world, but to the specific person whose opinion determines whether he keeps the job. The love interest's involuntary physical response (grabbing the rope, elevated pulse) signals the beginning of a shift she hasn't consciously acknowledged.

**Beat 8 — The protagonist earns trust through vulnerability, not force.** The noodles. The fight proved competence. This beat proves humanity. The love interest's defenses crack not because the protagonist overpowered them but because he revealed something gentle. She grants access she'd been withholding — permission to accompany her to the bank. The audience understands this is the beat that puts everyone inside the building from the cold open.

**Beat 9 — The supernatural system issues a warning the protagonist can't act on because the love interest overrides him.** Pendant convulses. Brian tries to stop them. Stella refuses. The pendant goes cold. The protagonist knows something is wrong and is powerless to prevent it because the power structure of the relationship (she gives orders, he follows) hasn't caught up to the reality (he's the one who can sense danger). The audience watches them walk into the trap knowing it's a trap.

**Beat 10 — The threat reveals itself as targeted, not random.** Bank robbery. Military formation. But the leader bypasses every other hostage and goes straight for the love interest. This isn't a robbery — it's a kidnapping. The audience's question shifts from survival to conspiracy: who sent them and why.

**Beat 11 — The protagonist sacrifices his body to protect the love interest, and the sacrifice activates the supernatural system.** Brian calculates the trajectory, shifts into the bullet, takes the round. The pendant detonates. Full activation — he can see heartbeats, weapons through fabric, exit paths, structural weaknesses. He steals the leader's backup pistol without being detected. The sacrifice is the trigger. The system doesn't activate because of danger — it activates because the protagonist chose to absorb harm for someone else. The audience now understands the pendant's rule: it responds to selflessness under duress.

**Beat 12 — Resolution is withheld at the moment of maximum tension.** Brian rises. Fires. Hits one gunman. The leader has a rifle to Stella's head. Brian doesn't drop the gun. Freeze. The promo answers what Brian is but never answers whether he can save her, who sent the men, or what the pendant actually is. Three open questions, all requiring the app.

# FITP

## *Core hook: Underdog Awakening + Secret Identity*

**Beat 0 (optional) — Powerstart.** Voice-driven self-introduction. The protagonist narrates her own confinement with dark humor — she describes horror as normal, which tells the audience more about the world than any exposition could. The tone IS the hook. No event, no action — just a voice that treats a dungeon cell like a dorm room.

**Beat 1 — The Ritual.** The authority figure enacts violence on the protagonist. Not a one-off — a routine. The protagonist already knows the steps. She's on her feet waiting before he arrives. The violence escalates through stages, each worse than the last, and the protagonist's internal monologue stays calm through most of it because she's done this before. Empathy is built through normalization — the audience is horrified by what the protagonist treats as ordinary.

**Beat 2 — Care Contrast + Origin Void.** The protagonist wakes in institutional care and immediately tries to escape — kindness is so foreign she interprets help as threat. The caretaker catalogs her injuries clinically, asks who did this, and she can't answer because disclosure is more dangerous than the injuries. Then a simple question she can't answer — her own birthday — opens the backstory: no family, no history, no identity beyond what strangers assigned her. Empathy deepens through absence, not event.

**Beat 3 — Transformation Discovered.** The caretaker warns the protagonist she "looks different" but won't explain. A staff member stares and looks away when caught. The protagonist finally sees herself — one eye has changed to an impossible color. The change accelerates (both eyes, hair). The mystery is physical, undeniable, and unexplained. Curiosity activates alongside empathy: "what is she becoming?"

**Beat 4 — False Reprieve.** The protagonist returns to her space to find it completely transformed — someone upgraded everything while she was gone. She's overwhelmed by things the audience takes for granted. A caretaker gives her items to hide the transformation. Functions as a hope beat — the protagonist experiences basic human decency for the first time. Structurally, its job is to give her something to lose.

**Beat 5 — Hope Destroyed.** The authority figure finds the upgrades and weaponizes them as debt. Strips the protagonist's last freedom. Declares her a permanent servant with no path out and no way to repay. The kindness someone showed her has been turned into a chain. The protagonist narrates the flame of hope extinguishing.

**Beat 6 — Cruelty Delegated + First Manifestation.** The authority figure orders his son to punish the protagonist, then leaves. The abuse is becoming institutional — passed down generationally. The protagonist breaks down completely, begs a higher power. At the moment of maximum despair, the impossible happens — time stops, her hands glow, she burns the attacker without understanding how. He's terrified. He runs. The manifestation is confusing, not triumphant. The audience's reaction is "wait, what?" not satisfaction.

**Beat 7 — The Secret Named.** That night, an inner voice awakens and identifies itself. It names what the protagonist is: not a slave, not a servant, but something far greater. The protagonist doesn't believe it. Functions as the central promise planted in a way the protagonist's skepticism protects from resolving. The audience wants to believe it. The protagonist can't afford to.

**Beat 8 — Injustice Compression.** Weeks pass in summary. Conditions worsen — more work, more abuse, more isolation. The inner voice is the protagonist's only refuge. They bond over the one task the protagonist is allowed to do. Functions as an injustice accumulator — the audience's need for reversal compounds across every compressed detail. Also establishes that the power hasn't changed her daily reality. She's still trapped.

**Beat 9 — Witness to a Secret.** The protagonist accidentally sees something a powerful figure was hiding — a transgression that could destroy reputations. She wasn't supposed to be there. Functions as a new threat vector that has nothing to do with her own secret. The threat map expands — now another person has reason to silence her.

**Beat 10 — Attacked for Knowing + Second Manifestation.** The figure whose secret she witnessed attacks her to ensure silence. The inner voice takes over the protagonist's body — voice changes, hands glow, burns the attacker. More controlled than the first manifestation, includes a verbal threat. Functions as the second awakening fragment while raising the secret identity stakes — another person now knows she's not what she appears.

**Beat 11 — New Axis of Horror.** The authority figure's son corners the protagonist and reveals his interest isn't just cruelty — it's sexual. He's been aroused by watching the abuse. He describes what he intends. Then follows through. The protagonist dissociates. The narration goes flat. The inner voice can't intervene because the attacker outranks them in the hierarchy. Functions as the absolute floor of the story — there is nowhere lower to go.

**Beat 12 — Survival Tether.** The inner voice orders the protagonist to get up. The protagonist says she wants to die — and means it. She goes to the edge of the building repeatedly. Can't do it — not for herself, but because ending her life would end the inner voice's life too. The bond with the voice is the only thing holding. Functions as an empathy ceiling — the audience understands this is not drama, it's a genuine reckoning.

**Beat 13 — Defiance Flicker.** Another powerful figure threatens the protagonist. For the first time, the protagonist pushes back — not with power, but with truth. She removes her disguise, shows her changed eyes, and says she'd welcome whatever they do. Functions as a character turn: she's stopped hiding because she's stopped caring whether she survives. The secret identity cracks from exhaustion, not strength.

**Beat 14 — World Expansion.** The authority figure assigns the protagonist to serve a visiting delegation from the most powerful neighboring pack. Political stakes enter for the first time. The protagonist's world, which has been a closed system of abuse, suddenly opens. Functions as pre-positioning — the person who will change everything is being brought to her by the very system that's destroying her.

**Beat 15 — New Eyes on the System.** The delegation arrives. They're physically intimidating but polite — the contrast with the protagonist's own people is immediate. A senior outsider notices the protagonist's disguise, asks what she's hiding. Sees the hair, the eyes, the bruises. Warns her to cover the injuries before their Alpha sees them. Functions as the first time an outside observer registers that something is deeply wrong here.

**Beat 16 — Punished During Performance.** The authority figure punishes the protagonist for minor failures in serving the guests. Her healing can't keep up. She's serving the delegation while bleeding through her clothes. Functions to keep the injustice thread alive during the world-expansion sequence — the audience can't forget what she's enduring even as the story widens.

**Beat 17 — Pride in Creation.** The protagonist prepares an elaborate formal meal for the delegation and feels pride for the first time. Her inner voice helped her find joy in the one task she's allowed to do, and now that investment pays off emotionally. An outsider acknowledges her work. Functions as an earned emotional beat — the protagonist experiences a feeling she's never had, built from the only freedom the system accidentally left her.

**Beat 18 — Calm Before Storm.** Late at night, the protagonist restocks guest rooms alone. She leaves personal touches — things she remembered individual guests requesting. She's humming. She's calm. Functions as the last breath before the climax. The audience feels the stillness.

**Beat 19 — Mate Reveal.** The protagonist approaches the last door. A guard challenges her. She looks up. The most powerful person in the visiting delegation is standing in front of her. They recognize each other at the same instant — the bond activates simultaneously. He crosses the distance and pins her to the door. The inner voice screams confirmation. The structural answer to every beat of accumulated injustice is standing right in front of her. But it's INITIATED, not delivered. They meet. That's all.

**Beat 20 — Collision / Cliffhanger.** His force reopens every unhealed wound. Blood runs down. Her abusers arrive in the hallway — every power structure in the story collides in one corridor. The visiting Alpha attacks anyone who reaches for her. The affair secret detonates. Everyone is screaming. The protagonist is fading, can't hear anything. She grips his arm. Whispers "help." Slides down. Cut. Resolution initiated. Resolution withheld. The audience knows what the bond means — rescue, exposure, reversal. They get none of it.

# DRAGON BATTLE

## *Core hook: Loyalty / Sacrifice*

**Beat 1 — Protagonist at zero.** World hierarchy established through what it does to them. The thing the world dismisses as weakness is quietly framed as the thing to watch. Rita flicks his beasts over with one finger. Kruger's lion kicks dirt on him. Rich kids get rare beasts at birth — Karl's are a baby bird and a house spider. But the beasts speak to him telepathically: "You're special! That's why we chose you!" The audience holds two frames at once: the world says he's nothing, his beasts say he's chosen.

**Beat 2 — Excluded from the arena.** The protagonist isn't just low status — they're barred from the one event that could change their position. The door closes before the story walks through it. The Beast Master Battle is tonight. Win and level up, lose and go home. Rita bans Karl from attending. The first in his family to leave the mines, and he can't even get in the room.

**Beat 3 — External power tests the protagonist's values.** An authority offers a shortcut to power. The price is sacrificing something the protagonist is loyal to. They refuse. Punished for it. The authority silently registers the refusal as meaningful. Nadia offers her WilderBear to the weakest student — Karl could leap in status instantly, but he'd have to trade away one of his beasts. He refuses. Rita hits him. Kruger takes the bear, widening the gap. The twinkle in Nadia's eye isn't attraction. It's assessment.

**Beat 4 — Emotional bottom as resignation.** Not rage, not breakdown — surrender. The protagonist stops believing they belong. The lowest point isn't external punishment — it's internal capitulation. Karl walks to the cup, writes his name on a card, hovers his hand over it — and crumples the paper. He agrees with the world's verdict on him.

**Beat 5 — Accidental contact with the force that will eventually invert their status.** The protagonist stumbles into proximity with something far beyond their level. Survives through instinct and the bond with their allies. A mechanic gets planted that looks incidental. Neither the protagonist nor the force recognizes what just happened between them. Karl sneaks into the stables and walks into Scalix's cave. Nearly dies. Rae crawls across the dragon's face — Scalix flinches. They escape. The spider-on-the-dragon moment reads as comic relief. It's a loaded gun.

**Beat 6 — Forced into the arena by antagonists.** The protagonist doesn't choose to compete — the antagonists rig it as humiliation. The summons is weaponized contempt. But the audience holds information the antagonists don't. Karl's name comes out of the cup — Kruger rigged it. The magical binding forces him to fight the Prince and his legendary dragon. But the audience knows Karl already survived face-to-face contact with Scalix. The antagonists think they're sending a lamb to slaughter. The audience isn't sure.

**Beat 7 — Battle + the moral choice.** The protagonist is losing, finds an opening, but taking it means sacrificing the thing they refused to sacrifice in Beat 3. Same test, lethal stakes. They choose to lose. They shield their allies with their own body. Karl fights Scalix and the Prince. Gets crushed on points. Hawk draws blood — first crack. The spider trick works again, Karl has an opening. Hawk can attack while Rae is exposed on the dragon. He could win. But Rae might die. He runs into the circle, breaks the protective field, covers his beasts with his body. He chooses them over the win.

**Beat 8 — The force from Beat 5 inverts their status because of the choice in Beat 7.** The reversal is total, public, and originates from outside the protagonist. The thing they were punished for in every previous beat is the thing that earns the highest reward in the world. Power comes to them because of who they are, not what they were hiding. Scalix refuses to attack. "NO." She breaks her bond with the Prince on live television and chooses Karl. "He chooses his beasts first." The dragon — the force Karl accidentally contacted in Beat 5 — recognizes the moral choice and inverts his position in front of everyone.

**Beat 9 — The authority from Beat 3 returns with a question the inversion can't answer.** The status inversion shouldn't be possible given what everyone knows about the protagonist. The institutional power names the contradiction. Resolution initiated, identity mystery opened, payoff withheld. Karl is grabbed off the street. Bag over his head. It's Nadia. "A legendary dragon will only bond with royal blood. I know every member of my grandfather's family." She looks at him. "So what makes you so special?" The beat that tested his values now holds the key to his identity.

**Beat 10 — CTA.** Questions mapped to the threads the inversion opened, not the ones it closed.

# TRAGIC CHILDHOOD

## *Core hook: Underdog / Weak → Strong (or) Poor → Rich*

**Beat 0 (optional) — Powerstart.** A single, specific public humiliation. Not narrated — shown. The protagonist's most personal possession is displayed as a joke for the entire school. Functions as instant status placement: the audience knows exactly where this person sits in the hierarchy before a word of context is given.

**Beat 1 — Protagonist at Zero.** The world's power hierarchy is established through a montage of cruelties — each from a different person, each in a different setting, all delivering the same message: the protagonist is nothing here. The world's currency is an innate trait he doesn't have. The insults are specific and physical, not abstract. Crucially, the one thing the protagonist IS exceptional at — academics — earns him public recognition that the room laughs at. Functions to close the "can't he just work harder?" escape valve immediately. He already did. The system doesn't care. Empathy is built through volume: this isn't one bully, it's the entire world.

**Beat 2 — The Promise / The Mystery.** A bully weaponizes the protagonist's dead parent. The protagonist nearly breaks — ready to fight — but stops himself, because retaliation would destroy everything he's worked for. This triggers the backstory: flashback to the parent's death, a child holding a dying parent who gives two commands — never reveal the secret, become the chosen one. A sealed object is left behind that the protagonist can't open. Back in present day, the protagonist lives in squalor, studies obsessively, works menial jobs, and spends every remaining hour trying to unlock what the parent left behind. Functions to plant the central mystery (what's sealed inside?), the central constraint (the deathbed promise), and the reason the protagonist absorbs punishment instead of fighting back — all in one move. The audience now has two threads: empathy (how much more can he take?) and curiosity (what's in the object?).

**Beat 3 — Hope Arrives and Corrodes.** Someone from the powerful class takes an unexpected interest in the protagonist. She's everything he isn't — status, lineage, the trait he lacks. A relationship forms. But the same gap that makes the protagonist a target starts eroding the relationship from inside. She wants things he can't provide. He values things she's losing patience for. The cracks form not from cruelty but from incompatibility that the world's hierarchy makes unfixable. Functions as a false reprieve that the audience can already see failing — the system doesn't just attack the protagonist directly, it poisons anything good he touches.

**Beat 4 — No Sanctuary.** The bullying follows the protagonist to his job, his private spaces, the places he retreats to. The specific bully is the romantic rival — personal and persistent. The protagonist's sharp tongue earns him physical punishment. Even his workplaces become sites of humiliation. Functions to close every escape route. There is nowhere safe, and the rival's presence keeps the romantic threat active.

**Beat 5 — Breaking Point.** The protagonist goes to his dead parent's grave and breaks down. He shouts at her. Accuses her. Questions the promise. Then feels guilt. Then begs for help. The rain falls. Functions as the emotional floor — the audience watches the protagonist's faith in the mission crack. He's not stoic; he's barely holding on. The audience feels the isolation: the only person he can talk to is dead.

**Beat 6 — The Trap + Retaliation.** A powerful family offers the protagonist what he wants most — the trait everyone else has — with one condition: abandon the person he loves. He refuses. The offer was never real — it was designed to humiliate him or kill him. The powerful family retaliates through institutional channels: political connections, military authority, confiscation of savings, eviction. His poverty, which was already a burden, becomes a weapon used against him. The offer resurfaces with the same condition. He refuses again. Functions to test the protagonist's loyalty under maximum pressure AND escalate the threat from social to existential. The antagonist isn't just mocking the powerless — she can erase them.

**Beat 7 — Everything Lost.** The person he loves leaves him. Not because she's cruel — because she's exhausted. She says what the world has been saying, but in the one voice that actually wounds him: you're not enough. The next morning, he's physically thrown out of his home. He sits on the street with his belongings and the sealed object, and considers abandoning everything — the mission, the promise, the object. Functions as rock bottom. The protagonist has lost the relationship, the home, the money, and the self-belief. The audience needs to feel that he is genuinely done before the turn arrives.

**Beat 8 — The Vault.** An external trigger arrives at the last possible moment — a message about something the dead parent left behind that the protagonist didn't know existed, expiring today. He goes to the institution that holds it. Staff dismiss and disrespect him based on appearance. His identity is verified through the system, and attitudes flip instantly — a micro-status-inversion that foreshadows the macro one. The vault is nearly empty except for one object the protagonist immediately recognizes as the missing piece to the sealed object he's spent years trying to open. The parent hid the key separately from the lock. Functions as the mystery solving itself through inheritance at the last possible moment, and as the mechanical trigger for activation.

**Beat 9 — Activation.** The key requires the protagonist's blood to work. The sealed object opens. He sees a vision of his parents' final moments — one parent sacrificed themselves so the other could escape with the object. Generations of stored power flow into the protagonist's body. A voice names him the chosen one. He collapses. When he wakes, he's in the same body with a completely different presence. He knows what he is. He heads straight for the place where every bully, every authority figure, and every person who dismissed him is gathered in one room. Functions as the awakening — but it's wrapped in grief (watching his parent die), not pure triumph. The audience gets the power-up and the cost simultaneously. The transformation is internal, not visual — same clothes, same appearance, different person.

**Beat 10 — Status Inversion / Cliffhanger.** The protagonist arrives at the public event. Sees the person who left him holding hands with the rival. Gets mocked one final time by the antagonist who tells him to stay away from her powerful father. He smiles. Then he walks through the crowd, past the cameras, up the steps, straight toward the most powerful person in the room — the man whose family took his home, his money, and nearly his life. Security moves to intercept. The powerful man stops his own guards. Goes pale. Addresses the protagonist by name, with deference. The entire world is watching. Every humiliation from the first half of the promo is alive in the audience's memory as the kid everyone laughed at stands on stage and the most powerful man in the room is afraid of him. Resolution initiated — the inversion is visible, public, undeniable. Resolution withheld — the audience never sees what he does with it. Cut.

# LIVE ACTION (2.0)

## *Core hook: Underdog / Weak → Strong (or) Poor → Rich*

**Beat 0 (optional) — Powerstart.** The protagonist approaches someone out of his league and gets physically destroyed for it. No buildup, no context — just a powerless person getting blasted across a hallway. The audience knows the hierarchy instantly. Quinn tries to ask Eliza for help and she launches him into a locker with her water power. "Do you even have an ability?"

**Beat 1 — Protagonist at zero / the object.** The world's hierarchy is established through what it does to the protagonist, while a sealed object from his dead parents is established as the one thing that could change everything. The object is stolen by the top bully, and the protagonist chases it through a room full of powered students who use him as target practice. Even the teacher is hostile — tells him the enemy will kill him quickly, just like his parents. Quinn's ability book is taken by Rylie, flung into a live combat gym. Quinn charges through a battlefield of ice and lightning to get it back. Rylie smacks him in the face with his own book. The teacher tosses it back with a death prediction. The audience holds two frames: this kid is getting destroyed, but he won't stop chasing that book.

**Beat 2 — The blood key.** Alone with a ticking clock, the protagonist desperately tries everything to open the sealed object. Nothing works. Frustration breaks him — he destroys something, cuts himself. His blood lands on the object and it glows. The key was inside him the whole time. Quinn has one hour before the military picks him up. He tries fire, impact, simulation of every element. Nothing. He hurls the book at a mirror, glass cuts his arms, blood drips onto the cover. Red glow. The mechanism is blood — the first hint that whatever this power is, it's not clean.

**Beat 3 — Activation.** The sealed object opens and something uploads into the protagonist — not a power he controls, but a system that controls itself. His body transforms physically. A quest appears that's almost absurdly mundane. The tone is confusion and dread, not triumph. Quinn's book explodes open, a digital interface floods his vision, puncture wounds appear on his neck. He's taller, muscular, different. "YOU HAVE BEEN GRANTED THE VAMPIRE SYSTEM." First quest: drink three water bottles. The audience's reaction is "what just happened to him?" not "hell yes."

**Beat 4 — First cycle / first cost.** The protagonist completes the trivial quest and feels the reward — his body surges beyond his control. The progression loop is introduced: do the thing, get stronger. But immediately, the system reveals a fundamental weakness. Quinn chugs the water bottles, accidentally sprays his sergeant, and his legs carry him halfway down a hallway before he can stop. Then he steps into sunlight and everything drains. Stats halved. The power has a built-in cost the protagonist can't avoid.

**Beat 5 — First brave act.** The protagonist sees the bully harassing someone and intervenes — not with power, but with nerve. It's the first time he's stood up to anyone. Before the confrontation can escalate, an external force knocks everyone unconscious. On the bus, Rylie is cornering Layla. Quinn steps between them. "I thought you had a girlfriend?" Rylie is furious but green gas floods the bus before he can retaliate. Quinn's system tries to resist the fumes but he goes down too. He wakes up at the academy.

**Beat 6 — New world / allies formed.** The protagonist arrives at the academy where everyone's power level will be permanently visible. He meets a fellow powerless cadet and completes a stealth quest no one notices. A bully attacks the new friend; the protagonist catches the punch — first real proof the system works. A powerful stranger intervenes and offers protection. Watches are snapped on — rank displayed for all to see. Quinn meets Peter, who hates the ranking system because he's powerless too. Quinn tricks Peter into pocketing a silver necklace (vampire weakness — quest complete, strength increases). Momo attacks Peter; Quinn catches his electrified fist mid-swing. Vorden steps in with the same lightning and shuts Momo down. Three allies, formed in minutes.

**Beat 7 — Power ceiling.** Other cadets are tested against holographic enemies. The audience sees what real power looks like at this level — and learns that falling below a threshold means expulsion within thirty days. The protagonist hasn't been tested yet. Layla's telekinetic arrows hit three targets simultaneously. Erin sprouts ice wings and shreds a dozen enemies at once. Layla scores a two — needs a three in thirty days or she's out. The bar the protagonist has to clear is now visible and terrifying.

**Beat 8 — The test / dark power.** The protagonist is forced to test despite being "powerless." He needs to complete a quest mid-combat but the mechanism is absurd and humiliating. He fails, gets overwhelmed, is saved by an ally. The quest accidentally completes. Dark power floods in. He goes on a rampage and enjoys it too much. Quinn has to pull Vorden's hair during a Dalki fight. He grabs Vorden's entire scalp — nothing. Gets piled on by three Dalki. Vorden saves him with ice powers (copied from Erin — his secret). Quinn grabs Vorden's forearm to stand up, snagging a tiny hair. Quest complete. Shadow blades appear at his fingertips. He carves through Dalki laughing, splattered in holographic blood, until Vorden physically stops him. Level one. The audience saw what happened. So did every cadet in the crowd.

**Beat 9 — Party / blood trail.** The system guides the protagonist somewhere social with no explanation. The romantic interest finds him and chooses him openly. Then a new ability activates — he can see trails leading to blood. He follows one and finds his powerless friend being beaten by the bully. The system recommended the party. Layla bumps into Quinn, tells him she wants to train together. Rylie glares from across the room. Then a red trail bleeds into Quinn's vision, leading to the back — Rylie has Peter by the collar, punching him. Quinn steps into the circle.

**Beat 10 — Final fight / full unlock / cliffhanger.** The protagonist fights the bully. He's losing — the bully is stronger. But the system adapts in real-time, building immunity to the bully's signature attack. The protagonist endures the pain until the immunity kicks in, draws blood, and faces the final quest: consume it. He hesitates — every quest has been darker than the last. He looks at his friends, looks at the blood, and chooses. Full transformation. Quinn bear-hugs Rylie through his own flames until the system grants temporary fire immunity. He cracks Rylie's nose. Blood on his knuckles. "CONSUME TO COMPLETE QUEST AND FULLY UNLOCK SYSTEM." Rylie powers up with a beast weapon. Quinn looks at the blood, thinks about everything the system has asked him to do — destroy silver, steal hair, track blood. He licks it. Shadow wings erupt from his back, claws extend, fangs sharpen, eyes burn red. He rises off the ground. The kid they called Level Zero floats above the room. "Ready to find out what Level I really am." The audience never sees the fight finish. Cut.

# PHOENIX / LOVE BITE

## *Core hook: Secret Identity / Reincarnation*

**Beat 1 — Public Sentencing.** A hostile authority assembles their entire household to mock the protagonist and pronounce a "merciful" choice that is actually a death sentence. The crowd laughs because everyone in this world already knows what the choice means. The protagonist accepts — not because the option is real, but because refusing means immediate execution. Hierarchy is established through the assembled laughter, not through narration. e.g. Adrian sentences Serena to Lava Valley or hanging; the assembled house laughs at the magicless girl going to the heat that kills magicless people. The Mistress offers Tristan bite-or-die in front of forty howling werewolves who already know what conversion does to humans.

**Beat 2 — The Hidden Identity Engages.** Inside the body the world is sentencing, a different identity is already active that no one can see. The audience learns who is *actually* wearing the disguise. The promise of the show is set: every cruelty going forward is being absorbed by someone the world has fundamentally misclassified. e.g. Serena is a Thief Goddess in a thirteen-year-old's body; Tristan is the world's first Zompirewolf in the cell where they expect to find a half-dead pawn.

**Beat 3 — The Hidden Mentor Surfaces.** Alone for the first time, the protagonist is contacted by a voice bound to their body — a sentient agent, not a passive system. The mentor names what the protagonist is, sets the hard rules of the power economy (level via X, hide via Y, die if Z), and offers a deal. The audience now holds a system the world doesn't see, and a contract whose price is deferred. e.g. Lucien materializes in Serena's palm, demands thirty thousand demonic cores in exchange for breaking the seven seals; Julian's voice cuts in mid-collapse, names Tristan a Zompirewolf, sets the gene-balance rule and the hide-or-die constraint.

**Beat 4 — First Hidden Power Earned (Private).** The protagonist accesses the first fraction of their true power through a secret action — a theft, a private endurance, an unauthorized activation. The world doesn't see it. The audience does. Vindication is real but unshared, which prevents the satisfaction from leaking out of the audience and into the world. e.g. Serena steals the House Phoenix crest from her grandfather's chambers, breaks the first seal, conjures a flame in her palm and weeps. Tristan's first system reveal in the cell — "Designation: Zompirewolf. Grade F." — earned by surviving a bite that should have killed him.

**Beat 5 — The Antagonist's Larger War.** The dominant antagonist's personal agenda surfaces — through a monologue, an overheard conversation, or a forced confession. The protagonist learns the captivity isn't incidental: they're being held as a piece in someone else's war against their own world. Cover deepens. Stakes shift from "survive today" to "play along while planning." e.g. Serena overhears Adrian and her uncle arguing — her parents' magic was sealed deliberately, Lava Valley was supposed to "handle what we won't." Mistress reveals to Tristan she's a bastard daughter planning to kill the Lycan King and use Tristan as her weapon.

**Beat 6 — Second-Tier Cruelty Earns First Public Skill.** A lower-ranked antagonist tortures or tests the protagonist. The protagonist endures or counters, and the system rewards the endurance with a skill the world *can* see. The progression is small enough that the top antagonist doesn't fully reassess — but it's the protagonist's first publicly visible upgrade. e.g. Clara torches Serena's room; Serena's spark trips Clara into her own fire — first public proof of magic. Donovan grinds Tristan into glass shards in the training yard; he doesn't scream and earns Pain Tolerance Level 1.

**Beat 7 — Top Antagonist Registers an Anomaly.** The dominant antagonist witnesses something they cannot explain — survives what shouldn't be survivable, performs what shouldn't be possible. They scan, suspect, or examine. The cover holds, but they've now looked closely. The audience is handed a clock: the secret has a finite shelf life. e.g. Clara runs to tell the family Serena has magic. Mistress storms into the simulation room, finds the real ghoul Tristan killed, slaps Donovan, and looks at Tristan with new eyes that don't quite find what they're searching for.

**Beat 8 — Private Warning + Decision to Risk.** Back alone, the mentor warns the protagonist that the antagonist saw something. Leveling at the safe rate will not be fast enough. The protagonist breaks a rule — slips off the path, sneaks out, pursues a target above their level. The audience knows it's too soon. The protagonist knows too. They go anyway. e.g. Lucien senses a demon beast half a mile east; Serena slips off the Lava Valley path. Julian warns about the Mistress's all-seeing ability; Tristan decides one trait per day is too slow and goes out into Maddencreek to feed his vampire and zombie genes.

**Beat 9 — Forced Activation Against Superior Enemies.** Out in the dangerous space, the protagonist is overpowered. At the moment of imminent death, the mentor forces a desperate activation of hidden power — and the activation comes with a hard constraint shown in the same beat (time limit, last resource, single-use cost). The protagonist kills enemies above their level. The world has no record this should be possible. e.g. Serena fights the demon beast head-on, kills it with a jagged rock, claims the core. Treaty vampires pin Tristan against a wall; Julian forces all three genes active at once — Undead Rhapsody, two minutes only — and Tristan ashes both vampires.

**Beat 10 — The Fake-Friendly Betrayal.** A character who appeared to be helping is revealed as the trap-setter — or a previously hinted betrayal completes its detonation. The protagonist's trust map collapses by one major node. The kill counter visibly extends. e.g. Alaric, the cousin who gave Serena armor, walks in on her ambush and reveals he was the setup all along; the twins shatter her armor and leave her to die. The Mistress whispers mid-bite that Wyatt — Tristan's father's best friend — sold him out for a captain's rank, the same Wyatt who sold Tristan's parents four years ago.

**Beat 11 — Threshold Confrontation / Cover Holds.** The top antagonist intercepts the protagonist at the moment of empowerment. They demand proof — read the doctored display, scan the body, look the protagonist in the face. The cover holds because the mentor's hack is good, but the antagonist has now looked at close range. e.g. Mistress in the corridor reads Tristan's status plate — Werewolf Level 2, Pain Tolerance, Battle Tactics — and finds nothing else. Serena kneels in the Aerie among the cousins who just left her to die; they pale at the sight of her but the sage is already chanting.

**Beat 12 — Elevation Disguised as Trap / Cliffhanger.** The antagonist sends the protagonist into the one situation where their secret cannot survive — packaged as a reward, an honor, or an inevitable rite the protagonist cannot refuse. The mentor reveals a second unseen player whose hand the antagonist doesn't fully control. The protagonist is becoming the thing the world has never seen, walking into the place built to identify it. Resolution initiated, payoff withheld, multiple threads open. e.g. Mistress announces Tristan will attend Blackmoon Academy — "the worst place in this kingdom for what you are" — and Julian admits someone else wanted him there. The Phoenix egg cracks in front of Serena while the sage warns one of the four will discover what's hiding inside her.
