# THE SCENE BRIEF TEMPLATE · v4 — the per-promo layer, derived mechanically from W2/W3 material
*(v3 delta — the six derivation laws from the Round B failure, where the constitution held and the BRIEF failed. v4 delta — laws 7–8 from the Disowned run, where every gate passed and the briefs still staged an 83%-narration script. A brief that violates a law below produces writer defects that are the brief's fault, not the writer's.)*

**THE CONCEPT BUDGET (governing law):** at most 3–4 world concepts in play per scene, each paying emotionally in-scene; cold opens lean lower; plants that pay later move later; XFERs needing untaught systems reposition; stakes-free beats cut. Over-budget material is a structure defect routed upstream.

## THE EIGHT DERIVATION LAWS (1–6 Round B · 7–8 Disowned)

1. **COLD-LISTENER TRANSLATION.** Every WORLD/LORE sentence is written in a stranger's vocabulary. Source-artifact and institutional jargon ("the phase is live," "graded," "the board") never transmits untranslated — if a person wouldn't say it to a friend, rewrite it before it enters the brief.
2. **TURN MAPS END ON THE HERO.** Every exchange floor closes on the hero's answer or a marked silence — never on an unanswered blow. A map that hands the villain the last word stages a passive hero, and cruelty on a passive hero is misfortune, not injustice.
3. **PREREQUISITES TRAVEL.** A mid-promo or anchor scene lists every concept an in-scene beat depends on that earlier scenes taught (or would have) — carried as WORLD facts. A beat leaning on an untaught concept is taught in-brief or cut.
4. **LEDGER PHRASINGS ARE CONCRETE.** Listener-knows and lore sentences get transcribed into prose verbatim — so they are written physical and literal, never thematic ("saving her and feeding on her are the same act" ✗ → "to take the blood he has to put his mouth to the wound" ✓). A phrasing that would read as mush in the script may not enter the brief.
5. **MECHANISMS ARE LITERAL, OR THE EVENT RESTAGES.** Any device an event uses (how a wound is treated, how a power works) is stated as a literal canon how. If canon supplies no mechanism, the event is restaged to canon shapes — never transmitted as mystery for the writer to hedge ("the way only his kind could").
6. **EVERY ROW IS CANON-CHECKED.** Each lore sentence, backstory beat, and XFER is verified against the world doc before it enters the brief (ages, years, pronouns, abilities). Collisions surface as OPEN items with a proposed **-adapted** resolution (precedent: speaker/addressee adaptations, words untouched) — never silently transmitted, never silently fixed.
7. **EXCHANGES ARE THE COUNTED UNIT.** A budget derivation names the scene's exchanges and their turn counts first; **"narration beat" is banned as a counted unit** — narration is mortar between events, never counted mass. A scene whose derivation can't name its exchanges is under-staged: route to W2 for material, never fill with narration. *(Absorbs the old turns-plus-narration-beats arithmetic in W3 §1. Evidence: Disowned briefs counted "~6 narration beats" per scene → the writer delivered 130 narrator blocks vs 61 voiced lines, exactly on budget.)*
8. **XFERS ARE RUNGS, NOT THE EXCHANGE.** A turn floor counts rendered turns; XFERs sit inside it as fixed rungs, and **the writer voices the turns around and between them** in the characters' registers. A floor built only of XFERs stages transfer-and-narrate — the documented v3 verbatim-transfer anti-pattern. *(Evidence: Disowned S1's entire voiced content was 7 XFERs.)*

## THE SEVEN BLOCKS

1. **THE WORLD** — ≤6 plain sentences; only facts this scene's events touch; law 1 and law 3 applied.
2. **THE LISTENER-KNOWS LEDGER** — each secret as ONE concrete reusable sentence, **ASSIGNED to the moment where it fires** (or explicitly tagged background-only) — an unassigned ledger invites sprinkle. Empty at a cold open (say so; gaps marked by verdict and body). **One fact, one home:** a fact appears in exactly one block (WORLD, LEDGER, or LORE) — duplication signals importance at random.
3. **LORE, PRE-STATED** — every needed backstory fact, plainly, source named; **every number supplied — writers never mint one**; hero-facts stated as truth.
4. **THE INJUSTICE SPEC** — per unit: ACT (quotable) · PUNISHER + how the act is cited · GAP (one sentence).
5. **EVENTS, TELEGRAPHED** — bare clauses in order; exchanges carry numbered turn floors (speaker + speech-act + content-clause; extend while escalating, never shrink; **close on the hero**); XFERs verbatim with trigger speech-act and addressee. **The list OPENS with the map's IN state (TIME included, every CARRY item due to be touched, and the scene's BECAUSE — the prior-scene event that forces this one, which the opening stretch makes felt) and CLOSES with the OUT state and the seam voice** — the continuity contract rides inside the events, not as a side note.
6. **VOICES** — 1–2 plain lines per speaker; their phrasing never enters narration.
7. **BUDGET + POV + FORMAT** — word target, POV/tense, tags, scene position.

**Derivation bans:** no authored dialogue outside verbatim XFERs · no authored narration · no rendered STAGE prose carried over · no craft advice or rule cross-references · nothing from outside the W2/W3 artifacts and world docs.
