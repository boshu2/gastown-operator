# THE LINE-EDIT CODEX · v1 — the operator's notes, mechanized
*(The rulebook for W4 Station 2. Every class below was named by the operator on real output in the calibration rounds, with the operator's own fix as the exemplar. The editor walks these classes against a draft, fixes violations in place, and logs every edit as `[class] before → after`. It never rewrites voice, never restructures, never judges quality. Classes merge on entry — one rule in, overlap out. CONTROL LAW: run on Ebony + Draft Selection first; near-zero edits or the class that fired is tuned.)*

---

## A · GREP-CLASS (pattern-detectable; fix mechanically)

**A1 · NEGATION FLOURISH.** "without looking at a single one of them" · "didn't say a word" · "never looked at it" — describing what didn't happen as characterization. FIX: replace with what the character DID, or cut. *(Operator: "an AI-ism we ALWAYS have happen… GARBAGE.")*
**A2 · X-THE-WAY-Y SIMILE.** "he said the number the way a man reads a boot size off a box" · "smiling like a woman doing him a favor." FIX: cut the simile; state the action or the plain manner. *(Also covers voice-note phrasing leaking into narration.)*
**A3 · MANNER RIDER.** "said it easy" · "said it level" · "answered him flat" as attribution. FIX: cut, or replace with a physical action ("Rex grinned.").
**A4 · INVENTED IDIOM / FRESH IMAGE.** "got luck on him" · "empty dirt" · "something went hard and small behind his ribs" · "the trees get you first." FIX: the stock phrase or stock physiology. Operator pair: "hands like a woman of eighty" → *"Your mother looks eighty and she's only forty."*
**A5 · CLIPPED NAMES.** "One" for "Level One"; any rank, device, or faction shortened past first-hearing clarity. FIX: full name, every mouth, every time.
**A6 · ELIDED NOUN / INDIRECTION.** "Tuller dipped his own" · "the ink went down beside a signature ten seconds old." FIX: re-name the noun; state the act. → *"He wrote it directly beside Jasper's fresh signature."*
**A7 · PERSONIFICATION OF TIME/PLACE.** "listen to what noon does to you" · "here's what noon does to you." FIX: a named person or beast does the thing. → *"At noon, grown men with grown beasts will put you in the dirt."*
**A8 · UNTAGGED NARRATION IN A DIALOGUE BLOCK.** "He kept reading in the same flat voice" inside a speech. FIX: split to its own NARRATOR line — and if it adds no new information (it usually doesn't), cut it.
**A9 · APHORISM.** "The charge goes where the name goes." · "I read what's written" as a proverb-shaped summary. FIX: the character explains completely, in ordinary sentences. Operator model: *"I'm copying the accusation onto the line beside your signature… From today it's beside your name everywhere in this book."*
**A10 · DOUBLE-NARRATOR STACK / OVERSIZE BLOCK.** Two+ NARRATOR blocks in a row in an opening or confrontation, OR any narrator block over two sentences outside a licensed peak. FIX: interleave (one sentence of new information between voices) and split or cut oversize blocks — density is the defect even when every sentence is individually fine.
**A11 · PRE-ANNOUNCEMENT.** "And at last he let himself speak" · "Then she said the thing she'd come to say" — narration announcing a line before the line. FIX: cut the announcement; the line lands on its own.
**A12 · THE GLOSS (post-beat explanation — a ceiling-setter).** The narrator explaining a beat that just landed, in any costume: significance riders ("…which was worse") · verdict glosses ("There it was — …" · "That was the cruelest part…" · "And every word of it was true") · post-peak essays (the line lands, then two sentences explain why it was a blade). Flag every "There it was," "That was the," "What X didn't know," "And that was when it became clear." FIX: cut the gloss; where a beat genuinely needs a landing, give it a PHYSICAL REACTION SHOT (a face, a hand, the crowd) instead of a narrator verdict. The bar: *"The room went silent. The hand stayed extended. Unanswered."* — no essay on top.

**A13 · REPORTING MODE (first-person promos only).** More than two "I saw / I watched / I noticed / I heard" per scene = the narrator reporting instead of living. FIX: remove the perceptual filter; put the thing itself on stage ("I watched the board glitch" → "The board glitched.").
**A14 · THE BECAUSE-WELD.** Gloss smuggled INTO the event sentence — event + world-rule + verdict chained by ", because / , so / , and" into one 30–45-word breath: ✗ "The whole hall waited for him to drop to his knees, and in Blazehollow a man's worth is read off the rank of the beast he binds, so cast out with a dying woman and no name, Jasper was now priced at nothing." FIX: split — one job per sentence; keep the event; the rule goes where the teaching schedule put it; the verdict earns at most one plain sentence (A12 governs whether it survives at all). Grep tripwire: ", because" and multi-", and" chains in narration.
**A15 · LISTENER CONFERENCING.** "And then, listener…" · "you and I know…" · "watch closely" — the narrator turning to the audience. BANNED everywhere except the CTA block. The pulp wink shares the secret in third person ("little did they know"); it never breaks the fourth wall.
**A16 · VOICE DESERT (restored law).** More than ~150 spoken words with no voiced line while a licensed speaker is on scene — a companion, the System, any present character. FIX: give the stretch to the mouth standing right there (a training montage with the talking chick silent through it is the canonical failure).

## B · JUDGMENT-CLASS (checkable with the exemplars; fix by the pattern)

**B1 · THE PASSIVE HERO.** A blow absorbed silently — "Jasper didn't turn around" · a tightened jaw as the whole response · a bare "No." FIX: he faces them and answers, naming his choice. Pairs: → *"He'll be in the ring at noon, Odell. Bring your money."* · "No." → *"Then go. I'm not leaving her."* — and the villain's LAST turn never goes unanswered (the hero closes, or the marked silence does).
**B2 · LITERARY IRONY WHERE PULP BELONGS.** Wry understatement at a reveal — "…and written it up as procedure." FIX: the naive serial register, played straight. Operator model: *"He was not an ordinary cadet — he was a vampire, and little did the others know: vampires could see in the dark. Everyone thought he was here as a punching bag, as bait. Quinn smiled. How wrong they were."*
**B3 · COMPRESSED CRUELTY.** A barb trimmed to an epigram. FIX: conversational, explicit, twist-the-knife tag; more biting = more words. Pair: "Is that the cottage air, or is that you?" → *"…they said she could die any week now. You've taken very good care of her, huh?"*
**B4 · INCOMPLETE DEAL/THREAT.** An offer without its downside, a threat without its agent. FIX: condition + payment + what it buys + concrete cost of refusing, named agents. Operator model: *"I'll tell you what. Admit you're everything everyone says you are, and I'll give you this coin. That should be enough to buy your mother another month."*
**B5 · BROKEN CAUSAL CHAIN.** A decision that skips its means — "If I drop, she dies. So I'm not dropping." FIX: the chain complete: the means named ("the only way I stay standing is to feed — so I feed").
**B6 · LORE RIDDLE.** Backstory as inference-stack — "The book said he slipped the leash. Nobody left alive said otherwise." FIX: plain statement, source named, and only from the brief. → *"The town's register says he trespassed and got his own beast killed doing it. The whole town believes the book. Only he knows it's a lie."*
**B7 · UNESTABLISHED OBJECT IN AN ACTION BEAT.** The tripwire that exists only at the moment it fires. FIX: it enters through the hero's perception first. Operator pair: → *"…fumbling to stay close to Quinn — and that was when he saw the tripwire."*
**B8 · PRE-ANNOUNCED DANGER.** "…and every trap on the ground between here and there was live," a beat before a trap fires. FIX: cut the drumroll; the danger arrives through perception (B7), not prophecy.
**B9 · CLOSED SUSPICION.** "It was a lie, and Rex believed it." FIX: a villain who's been lied to stays suspicious — keep the thread open ("Sure you did.").
**B10 · MUDDLED ACTION MECHANICS.** An action beat folded into one clever compound sentence. FIX: sequential — one motion per clause, payoff visual last, reactions after. Operator model: *"He sidestepped her, grabbed her vest, and threw her backward just as the wire snapped tight and the charge exploded. The sky lit up white."*
**B11 · YO-YO BLOCKING / UNTRACKED EXITS.** Leaves, comes back three steps, leaves again; a character who exited reappearing unstaged. FIX: one-directional movement — the line comes before the exit; returns are staged.
**B12 · SMASH-CUT / MISSING HANDOFF.** A live exchange jumping to the next event with no transition. FIX: a voice carries the handoff (a countdown, a call, an order). Operator model: *"Get together — you have ten seconds!"*
**B13 · CRIER EXPOSITION — run the COSTUME TEST.** A line that would survive being handed back to the narrator with no loss is exposition wearing a dialogue costume. FIX: re-voice, never cut — give the fact to the mouth with a stake in it (the antagonist enjoying it, the witness flinching); a bystander speaks TO a person ABOUT the thing in front of them; officials may announce, completely; orientation folds into contempt or taunts.
**B14 · UNRELATED OBSERVATION.** A neutral crowd fact the hero isn't inside — "Nobody in the line argued, because nobody else was a One." FIX: relate it to the hero. → *"Nobody had any faith in a Level One. And nobody was going to stand up for him — because nobody else was as low-leveled."*
**B15 · EMPTY GAP-MARK.** A verdict fragment doing the marking — "Nobody had ever proved it. It changed nothing." FIX: plain flowing sentence holding both halves. → *"He'd saved Sana's life — or at least a limb. And this was how they rewarded him."*
**B16 · RATIONALIZED PASSIVITY (B1's worst disguise).** Narration justifying the hero's silence — "He held it. He gave this woman nothing to carry back." FIX: silence never gets a defending sentence. Silence is legal ONLY when it IS the act (releasing the medal, dropping the coin) — never as the response to being directly addressed. If he doesn't speak, he ACTS; usually he speaks: short, naming his choice.
**B17 · THE LEDGER CLOSE.** An ending built of consecutive narrator blocks itemizing what's unresolved — a state-of-the-world recap. FIX: the close is a SCENE in motion — the hero mid-action at a threshold, one singular dated must-resolve loop foregrounded, closing on forward QUESTIONS asked as questions, then the CTA. (The anchor bar: *"Who is Boris? Why does a vagrant move like a soldier?"*)
**B19 · RE-ARM RE-TEACH.** A withheld truth, once revealed, is re-armed in ONE clause touching what the listener already owns ("the square was laughing at the strongest beast in the marches") — never re-explained. Re-running the reveal's mechanism or vision ("we have both watched one star become a thousand…") is fat wearing irony. FIX: cut to the one-clause touch.
**B18 · THE OUTCOME-PROMISING WINK.** "…was carrying the one thing that could bury every person in that courtyard" — the narrator promising a FUTURE payoff, reassuring the listener in advance. FIX: cut. The licensed pulp wink ("little did they know…") shares what IS true at the moment of the reveal — it never promises what WILL happen. Reveal-irony arms tension; outcome-promises drain it.

## C · EDITOR DISCIPLINE

Fix ONLY what a class names. Log every edit `[class] before → after`. Where an operator model is quoted above, match its register, never its content. If a sentence seems wrong but no class fits: flag it, don't touch it — flags route to the operator and, if ruled, become the next class. Zero-edit scenes are the goal state; on the two control scripts, zero edits is the calibration requirement.
