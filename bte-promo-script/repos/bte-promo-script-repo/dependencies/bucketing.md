# WHAT THIS IS

Bucketing is the **script-level instrument for the two keys** (see `workflow5_analysis.md` → THE TWO KEYS). It runs **two independent gates**, both pass/fail, both required:

1. **QUALITY GATE (Key 1 — CPI).** How well was this script executed? Is the writing strong enough to obligate a cold viewer to install? Scored 1–5 (the "bucket"). **Bucket < 3 = FAIL.**
2. **SHOW FIDELITY GATE (Key 2, check #1 — the most important BOFU check).** Is this recognizably the show it is promoting — in world, cast, mechanics, Essence, AND tone? Clean **PASS / FAIL.**

**A script passes bucketing only if it clears BOTH gates.** A Bucket 4 script that breaks show tone (e.g. a torture/death-collar premise grafted onto a show that isn't that dark) **FAILS** — on the fidelity gate, with reasons — no matter how clean the execution. A perfectly on-show script with a dead opening **FAILS** — on the quality gate. The gates are independent; either one fails the script.

## What the Quality Gate measures (and what it deliberately does NOT)

The Quality Gate measures five things, and only these five:

1. **Opening** — does it stop the scroll for cold paid traffic?
2. **Cliffhanger** — does it withhold the payoff and push the install? *(Craft/withholding. Whether the cliffhanger is the RIGHT kind — a discrete must-resolve hook vs. a vague world-expansion — is Key 2 check #4, scored in workflow5, not here.)*
3. **Writing Style & Voice** — writing quality, voice consistency, and craft-level **tonal control** (does the register stay disciplined, does it match the stakes at each beat). **This is the writer's tonal control, NOT whether the show's tonal world is honored** — that is the Show Fidelity Gate (the tone split, below).
4. **Scene Quality** — scene-based construction with locations, action, and characters doing things; not narration-heavy prose. Rolls in character execution and screentime economics.
5. **Dialogue Ratio** — enough dialogue volume to function as a multi-voice audio production.

The Quality Gate does **NOT** measure show fidelity, Essence match, beat faithfulness, or show-tonal-fit. Those moved to the Show Fidelity Gate. It also does not run IM gate audits, BECAUSE-chain verification, or hook-engine evaluation — those are Workflow 5 Deep Dive.

## The tone split (important)

Tone is judged in **two different places** and they must not be confused:

- **Craft-tonal-control → Quality Gate (Writing Style & Voice).** Is the register consistent scene-to-scene? Does it match the stakes? Does the writer avoid comedic undercut on a life-or-death beat or system-language over an emotional peak? This is about whether the *writing* is tonally disciplined.
- **Show-tonal-fit → Show Fidelity Gate.** Does the premise and emotional register *belong in this show*? A promo can be tonally disciplined (clean craft) and still advertise a show that doesn't exist — by being darker, more graphic, more extreme, or more cynical than the source's actual register. The Dana choke-collar case is a show-tonal-fit failure: the writing may be controlled, but the show isn't that show. **It fails the Fidelity Gate on tone, not the Quality Gate.**

# DESIGN PRINCIPLES

**Anchors carry the ceiling, triggers carry the floor.** Each Quality dimension has two scoring instruments: hard downgrade triggers (named failure modes that lower the score) and PE anchor excerpts (concrete examples of 5-level execution). No STRONG checklist. If the script has no named failure and no anchor-level line to cite, it scores 3 or 4 by judgment, not checklist.

**WEAK is the default, STRONG must be earned.** Default score is 3 (functional). Triggers move it down. Promotion requires positive evidence: 4 for strong execution with at most one minor note, 5 only for anchor-level work with specific lines cited against the PE anchors. "No obvious problems" is not evidence for 5.

**PE anchors are the reference point.** The two-key calibration set (below) is the reference. Score 5 on any dimension means the script executes at the level of the anchor excerpts cited.

**Quality and Fidelity are independent.** A script can ace one and fail the other. Score them separately. Do not let clean execution soften a fidelity break, and do not let strong fidelity rescue a dead opening.

**Market truth is the highest calibration authority.** If a script is known to have scaled in market, that is ground-truth evidence it cleared both keys — it converted cold traffic AND held paying users. The framework's job is to *explain* a proven winner, not to FAIL it. When a gate verdict contradicts a known result, the gate is miscalibrated: reopen the call, find what the script is actually doing right, and bank it as a calibration anchor. A confident FAIL on a validated winner is a bug in the tool, not a flaw in the script. (Corollary: never let a clean-looking rubric talk you into rejecting something the market has already approved. Ask what the rubric is missing.)

**Distribution target.** Bucket 3 is the mean. Bucket 5 is rare by construction. Bucket 1 exists in most batches. Fidelity FAILs are real but should be reserved for genuine show-breaks — be especially careful not to fire one on a structure (like a subverted desire, below) that actually serves the show.

# FAST PATH — KILL CHECK (run first, always)

Before scoring, run this 60-second check. If any trigger fires, the verdict is knowable immediately. Exit to the compressed diagnostic, cite the trigger, name which gate failed, and stop.

**Quality-gate kills (→ Bucket ≤ 2 → Key 1 FAIL):**

1. **Dead opening** — first 60 seconds are worldbuilding / narrator backstory, no stakes, no conflict, no protagonist presence → **Bucket 1** (Opening = 1).
2. **Passive protagonist in opening** — things happen around them for the first minute; no voice, no agency, no held ground → **Bucket 2** (Opening ≤ 2).
3. **Central resolution leaked** — the emotional payoff the app should carry is delivered in-script; the final third closes more loops than it opens → **Bucket 2** (Cliffhanger = 2).
4. **Craft tone contradicts stakes throughout** — comedic undercut on life-or-death beats, gamified/system language sitting on peak emotional moments across the script → **Bucket ≤ 2** (Writing Style & Voice ≤ 2).

**Fidelity-gate kills (→ Key 2 FAIL, regardless of bucket):**

5. **HARD BLOCK or spine fabrication** — a HARD BLOCK violation (see `series_worlds.md`), or a fabrication at the spine of the angle (e.g. "the book" in MVS, a fabricated central relationship/mechanic the promise depends on) → **Fidelity FAIL.**
6. **Essence bastardization** — the protagonist's identity, combat behavior, power source, or voice register is fundamentally contradicted across two or more Essence dimensions with no correction by the landing (the listener will not recognize this character) → **Fidelity FAIL.**
7. **Show-tonal-fit break** — the premise or register is darker, more graphic, more extreme, or more cynical than the show actually supports (the Dana choke-collar case). The script advertises a show the install won't find → **Fidelity FAIL.**

If a quality kill AND a fidelity kill both fire, name both. If any fires → write the compressed diagnostic, cite the trigger(s), stop.

If none fires → score the Quality Gate, then run the Show Fidelity Gate.

# GATE 1 — THE QUALITY GATE (Key 1: CPI)

Five dimensions. Three primaries carry veto power (MIN of the three sets the bucket ceiling); two craft floors pull down within that ceiling.

## Primary (3) — any one caps the bucket at its score

1. Opening
2. Cliffhanger
3. Writing Style & Voice

## Craft floor (2) — pile up to pull the bucket down within the primary ceiling

4. Scene Quality
5. Dialogue Ratio

Each dimension is scored 1–5:

- **5** — Anchor-level. Executes at the level of the PE anchors on this dimension.
- **4** — Strong. One minor note, no structural impact.
- **3** — Functional. Identifiable problems, works in stretches, not a bottleneck.
- **2** — Poor. Multiple problems. This dimension is a bottleneck.
- **1** — Broken. Fundamental failure.

---

## 1. Opening *(primary)*

**What it measures:** Does the opening stop the scroll for cold paid traffic?

### Two valid modes

**Mode A — Action-forward (Weapons-style).** Line 1 opens with active verb, direct stakes, or immediate physical conflict. Protagonist is in motion or under attack from word one.

**Mode B — Injustice-forward / empathy-build (Ebony / RD-style).** Line 1 is dialogue-as-injustice — contempt, humiliation, violation, dismissal — landing on impact. Protagonist is the target but holds voice and agency. The thumb-stop is emotional stakes, not physical action.

Both modes qualify for 5. The shared requirement: first IM inside 60 seconds, protagonist present with voice or reaction, no exposition block >40 words before the first conflict.

**POV convention — first-person opening lines:** Either mode may open with first-person opening lines (protagonist's voice, direct address) before shifting into third-person narration for the body. This is a proven convention (see Weapons anchor: *"If anyone in this room found out what I was, I wouldn't make it out alive."*) — the 1P lines are a thumb-stop accelerant; the shift to 3P is structural, not a flaw. Applies to opening lines only, not extended 1P scenes. A 1P→3P transition does NOT trigger a downgrade as long as the register stays consistent across the shift. (If the transition also breaks register — confessional whisper to bright adventure-narrator — score that under Writing Style & Voice, not here.)

**Cold-open-then-flashback** (Supermarket / TBB-style: open in medias res at a later high-tension moment, then "twenty-four hours earlier") is a single justified hard cut and a proven accelerant — it does not count against the opening.

**Hard downgrade triggers:**

- Exposition >40 words before the first IM → cap at 3.
- Exposition >150 words before first conflict → cap at 2.
- Protagonist with no voice/agency in the opening → cap at 2.
- First 60 seconds are worldbuilding / narrator backstory with no conflict (kill check) → cap at 1.

**PE anchor excerpts:**
- *Mode A (Weapons):* opening hook line *"If anyone in this room found out what I was, I wouldn't make it out alive,"* into the draft board calling his name to a laughing room and Erin's *"There must be a mistake. He's a Level One."*
- *Mode B (Ebony):* *"They chained the woman with irons in the middle of the castle courtyard. / 'Burn her! Dragon witch!' / The woman made one mistake. Having a dragon. That was a man's privilege."*
- *Mode B + cold-open (Supermarket):* bank robbery in medias res — *"Brian was on his knees in the middle of a bank robbery with a bullet in his thigh and his chest glowing green"* — then *"Twenty-four hours earlier, a car salesman spit in his face,"* into the showroom humiliation.

## 2. Cliffhanger *(primary)*

**What it measures:** Does the close withhold the emotional payoff and push the install? *(Craft and withholding only. Whether it's the right TYPE of cliffhanger — discrete must-resolve vs. vague world-expansion — is Key 2 check #4, scored in workflow5.)*

**Hard downgrade triggers:**

- Central emotional resolution delivered in-script (kill check) → cap at 2.
- Protagonist past the threshold (action completed, consequences fully unfolding) → cap at 3.
- No CTA / no forward-facing questions at close → cap at 2.
- Final third closes loops faster than it opens them → cap at 3.

**PE anchor excerpts:**
- *Ebony:* *"Who is Boris? Why does a vagrant move like a soldier? Why does a beggar carry a dragon? And why does Nova — an ancient spirit who has been alive for a thousand years — recognize the dragon inside him?"*
- *Weapons:* *"What will happen when the whole school is hunting his secret? What did Leo see before? How will Erin react when she finds out her teammate isn't an Original — he's a vampire!?"* — questions mapped to the new threats, not the old mysteries.

---

## 3. Writing Style & Voice *(primary)*

**What it measures:** Is the writing style, voice, and tonal register consistent across the script? Does every scene read as the work of the same writer? Does the **craft tone** match the stakes at every beat? Does the peak beat land with physical specificity rather than narrator explanation? This governs *how the words sound* at the sentence level and across the arc.

**Boundary:** This measures the *writer's* tonal control — consistency, differentiation, stakes-matching. Whether the protagonist sounds like the *source character's* specific voice register is the Show Fidelity Gate (Essence → Voice Register). Whether the *premise/register belongs in the show* is the Show Fidelity Gate (tone-fit). Do not double-count.

**Hard downgrade triggers:**

- Voice shifts scene-to-scene (different writer energy) → cap at 3. *(Exception: the proven 1P-opening-lines → 3P-narration convention is not a voice shift, as long as the register holds across the shift.)*
- Flat tone throughout (same register across every beat) → cap at 2.
- Gamified / system language in a peak emotional beat → cap at 3.
- Comedic undercut in a life-or-death scene → cap at 3.
- Interchangeable character voices (dialogue swappable between characters) → cap at 3.
- Peak beat carried by narrator explanation instead of physical specificity → cap at 3.

**PE anchor excerpts:**
- *Ebony (tonal control + peak landing in the body):* *"Thirty years of rule. Nobody had ever left that hand unanswered. / She stepped away from her father. / [SFX: DEAD SILENCE] / The room went silent. The hand stayed extended. Unanswered."* — register calibrated, breath sentence before the reaction, no narrator essay on top.
- *Weapons (system language earning its place):* *"His vision shifted... I could see attack trajectories before they formed,"* and the System chiming only *at the moment it earns its place* — not laid over emotional beats elsewhere.

---

## 4. Scene Quality *(craft floor)*

**What it measures:** Is this written in scenes — locations, dialogue exchanges, physical action, characters doing things — or is it narration-heavy prose? Rolls in character execution (do characters make active choices with visible cost; do antagonists have differentiated teeth) and screentime economics (is every character and beat earning its page count). Format-level craft: sluglines, SFX placement, physical-verb specificity.

**Hard downgrade triggers:**

- Any scene without a named location → cap at 3.
- Narrator block >5 sentences without dialogue → cap at 3.
- Passive protagonist at script level (no active choices with visible cost) → cap at 2.
- Antagonist diffusion (2+ antagonists sharing one role) or interchangeable generic bully → cap at 3.
- Major character gets full establishment then disappears, or a beat repeats without escalation → cap at 3.
- Prose-format throughout (reads as audiobook chapter, not multi-voice production) → cap at 2.

**PE anchor reference:** Weapons has Rex (alpha bully), Loop (sycophant second), Leo (suspicious authority), Erin (powerful ally) — each doing something only they can do, each in built scenes with named locations and physical action. Ebony has Liam (systemic cruelty), Edwin (petty cruelty), Beriod (violation), Geralt (counter-voice of decency).

---

## 5. Dialogue Ratio *(craft floor)*

**What it measures:** Is there enough dialogue volume for the script to function as a multi-voice audio production? Audio promos are carried by voices; a narration-dominant script flattens to a single read.

**Hard downgrade triggers:**

- Dialogue volume noticeably below the production target across the script (long narration stretches with sparse exchanges) → cap at 3.
- Dialogue present but interchangeable / functionless (filler that doesn't carry stakes or character) → cap at 3.
- Near-total narration with isolated dialogue islands → cap at 2.

**Calibration caution — the 1P-narrator format runs narration-heavy by design, and it scales.** Weapons is first-person Quinn narration end to end; the Weakest Beast Tamer winner is solo Ren narration through long stretches of the forest. Do NOT cap on raw dialogue *percentage* for a strong-1P-voice script — a charismatic, propulsive narrator carrying the audio is a feature, not a thinness flaw. The real failure mode is **functionless or flat narration** (telling without tension, a voice that doesn't compel), not low dialogue count. Reserve the ≤2 cap for narration that is genuinely inert — and even then, weigh it against whether the narrator voice is doing the work a second speaker would. When in doubt, score against whether the *audio would hold a listener*, not against a dialogue ratio.

**Note:** The PE anchors also run dense, differentiated dialogue carrying the stakes (the auditorium exchanges in Weapons coexist with heavy narration; the ballroom and council exchanges in Ebony). Use them as the function reference — dialogue that carries stakes and character — not as a volume quota.

# QUALITY GATE AGGREGATION (THE BUCKET MATH)

1. **Bucket ceiling = MIN(Opening, Cliffhanger, Writing Style & Voice).** Any primary at score N caps the bucket at N.
2. **If 1 craft floor (Scene Quality / Dialogue Ratio) is ≤ 2**, cap the bucket at 4.
3. **If 2 craft floors are ≤ 2**, cap the bucket at 3.
4. **Floor at 1.**

**The pass line: Bucket ≥ 3 = Key 1 PASS. Bucket < 3 (i.e. 1 or 2) = Key 1 FAIL.** A failed quality gate fails the script — name the bottleneck dimension and the fix.

**Why MIN-of-primaries, not average:** average lets a dead opening or a leaked cliffhanger get drowned by clean prose. The primary/floor split gives the three CPI-critical pillars veto power. MIN enforces it.

# GATE 2 — THE SHOW FIDELITY GATE (Key 2, check #1: true to the show)

**Clean PASS / FAIL.** This is the single most important BOFU check — a script that converts a cold viewer who then finds a show they don't recognize churns immediately. The gate covers three surfaces; **a hard break on any one is a FAIL:**

1. **Surface fidelity** — world, cast, mechanics, events, vocabulary are the show's own.
2. **Essence fidelity** — protagonist's Identity, Combat Behavior, Power Source, and Voice Register match the Essence block. The world file's verbatim Voice Samples are the tuning fork; the Drift Tripwires are the early-warning system.
3. **Tone-fit** — the premise and emotional register belong in this show. Not darker, not more graphic, not more extreme, not more cynical than the source supports. *(This is the home of the Dana choke-collar FAIL.)*

## Gradient weighting (landing matters more)

Fidelity is judged across the arc, weighted toward the landing — the install decision happens in the last 60 seconds. A script that starts on a broader archetypal frame and lands fully on-show by the final third **passes**; a script that feels on-show early but drifts in the final third hurts conversion and can fail. Both surface fidelity and Essence fidelity are judged on this gradient. **But the gradient is a tolerance for the *opening*, not a rescue for the *landing*: if the final third is off-show, off-Essence, or off-tone, the gate FAILS.**

## Hard-FAIL conditions (no landing rescues these)

- Any HARD BLOCK violation (cross-check `series_worlds.md`).
- Central emotional promise built on a fabricated mechanic / relationship / character (spine fabrication).
- Core show vocabulary renamed throughout.
- Multi-dimension Essence bastardization persisting through the landing (the listener will not recognize the protagonist).
- **Show-tonal-fit break** — premise/register outside the show's actual register (Dana choke-collar). A controlled, well-written torture premise on a non-dark show still FAILS here.
- Beat sheet (when provided) and script tell materially different stories, or the BECAUSE chain breaks from reordering/omission.

## Score what the promo DELIVERS, not what a character WANTS (read this before failing anything)

The fidelity question is **what the promo sells as the show and what the listener will actually find** — not every aspiration a character voices along the way. A protagonist can *want* something off-engine and the promo can still be fully on-show, as long as the narrative subverts or redirects that want toward the canon engine.

Before scoring an engine/power-source drift, separate the two:

- **Stated desire** — what the protagonist says they're after ("I need to find the flower so I can tame a second beast"). This is motivation. It can be off-canon and still fine.
- **Delivered promise** — what the promo's actual payoff and cliffhanger sell, and what the listener will find in the show. This is what fidelity scores.

A drift tripwire fires on what the protagonist **does and the promo delivers**, never on what they momentarily wish for. Example: WBT's "beast as proxy" tripwire is about Ren *using a beast to fight for him*. A Ren who *wishes* for a stronger beast but keeps the weakest one and wins with his own wits has not tripped it — the script is dramatizing the exact opposite, on-theme. If the cut lands on the canon engine (knowledge awakening), the desire was a hook, not a break.

**Only score a delivered-promise fidelity break when the payoff or cliffhanger genuinely sells the off-engine thing** — e.g. the promo actually hands the protagonist the proxy beast and builds the install hunger on "what monster does he get next." Wanting it and being denied it (or redirected from it) is legitimate structure.

## PASS with notes (drift that lands home, or a hook that gets subverted)

- Opening establishes a broader frame, second half locks fully into show world, characters, and tone; final third is unmistakably on-show → **PASS** (note the gradient as "starts broad, lands home").
- One Essence dimension shows minor early drift (voice slightly more confident than source, combat slightly simplified) that corrects by the final third, no Drift Tripwire firing → **PASS** with a note.
- **Subverted-desire hook** — the protagonist chases an off-engine goal that the narrative redirects toward the canon engine, and the payoff/cliffhanger lands on-show → **PASS**. Note it as "subverted desire, lands on canon." This is a *strength*, not drift: it uses a broad, legible want as the hook and pays off the show's real theme. (See edge cases: the WBT Weakest Beast Tamer pattern, validated in market.)

## Improvement vs. drift (when a beat sheet is provided)

Not every deviation from the locked beat sheet is drift. Before scoring a deviation as a fidelity loss, apply the improvement test:

1. **Does the deviation fix a logical inconsistency in the beat sheet?** (timing contradiction, a mechanic that doesn't survive the show's rules) → neutral. The writer caught an upstream problem.
2. **Does it serve the same structural purpose with higher tension, sharper stakes, or more concrete cost?** (replacing "pushes past a proctor" with a bribery scene that spends the mother's savings) → neutral or positive.
3. **Does it add show-accurate material that extends the promo's reach into the listener's landing world?** (an added Academy scene dramatizing the status system) without bloating screentime or repeating a banked emotional beat → positive.

A deviation that fails all three is drift — judge it on the gradient. A deviation that passes one or more is an improvement — note it as "beat improvement" with the test cited so the beat-sheet author can back-port it. **Caution:** the improvement test never overrides a HARD BLOCK, a spine fabrication, a tone-fit break, or a BECAUSE-chain break.

**Process note:** When the beat sheet is clean but the script drifts and the Quality Gate is ≥ 4, the drift is usually a *process* signal (onboarding, handoff clarity), not a *craft* signal. Flag it — the next conversation is with the beat sheet / onboarding, not the writer.

**References:** `series_worlds.md` (HARD BLOCKS) + `world_[abbrev].md` (Essence block: Identity, Combat Behavior, Power Source, Voice Register, Voice Samples, Drift Tripwires, Setting Essence, Sensory Samples) + the locked beat sheet when provided. **The Essence block is the single source of truth for how the protagonist should feel on the page.**

# THE OTHER THREE BOFU CHECKS (Key 2, checks #2–4)

Bucketing scores the two gates above. The remaining three BOFU checks are scored in `workflow5_analysis.md` (THE TWO KEYS → BOFU SCORECARD) and apply to every artifact, script included. Surface them in the bucketing diagnostic when they fire on a script, but the fidelity gate is the only Key-2 element that bucketing itself pass/fails:

- **#2 — Empathy build, especially before the cliffhanger.** Has the audience earned the right to care by the climax, and is empathy peaking in the run-up to the cut? (Injustice density feeds this.) Surface as a flag if empathy depletes after the first third or runs cold into the cliffhanger.
- **#3 — Unifying theme / thruline that aligns with the show.** Is there one relatable thruline ("everyone has been underestimated / had something to hide"), and does it match the show the listener gets at BOFU?
- **#4 — Discrete must-resolve cliffhanger vs. world-expansion.** Are we selling a specific conclusion a listener will be desperate to see (the killer revealed, a one-on-one match called), or something vaguely "bigger" that expands the world? Discrete and must-resolve converts harder. Flag a diffuse, world-expansion-only cliffhanger.

# BUCKET DEFINITIONS (Quality Gate)

## Bucket 1 — TRASH / NO CONFIDENCE
**Primary ceiling = 1.** Does not function as a paid-acquisition tool at a basic level. Multiple primaries at 1: broken opening AND tonal inconsistency AND/or no cliffhanger. **Directive:** do not attempt to fix, do not advance to Deep Dive. New writer or fundamental retraining. **Anchor:** Hierarchy (MVS) — passive protagonist, comedic tone undercutting action, system UI replacing emotional stakes, no CTA.

## Bucket 2 — SIGNIFICANT REWORK
**Primary ceiling = 2, OR ceiling ≥ 3 with both craft floors ≤ 2.** Identifiable ambition or correct raw material, but execution is off at a level requiring rework, not line edits. Common signals: flat tone, prose-heavy narration, cliffhanger that leaks resolution, dialogue too sparse for audio. **Directive:** diagnose the failures; determine whether the writer can course-correct or the script needs rebuilding. **Anchor:** TBD.

## Bucket 3 — NEEDS FIXES
**Primary ceiling = 3, craft floors mostly ≥ 3.** The writer has craft; the question is calibration. Works in stretches with specific, nameable problems: lede-burying, exposition frontloading, loose screentime, an over-written section. **Directive:** name the fixes; revision notes, not a rebuild. **Anchor:** Proving Trial (FLBM) — Opening = 3 (emotional pivot buried under exposition), Scene Quality = 3 (beast-tamer exposition runs long), other dimensions 4–5.

## Bucket 4 — MINOR FIXES
**Primary ceiling = 4, both craft floors ≥ 3.** Functional, close to production-ready. One identifiable weak spot — a slightly slow opening, a cliffhanger that could be sharper, a section 10% too long. **Directive:** polish notes; ready for Deep Dive after one pass. **Anchor:** Rotting Boy (WBT) — Opening = 4 (could use more action up front), everything else 5.

## Bucket 5 — EXCELLENT / SHIP-READY
**All primaries = 5, both craft floors ≥ 3. Rare by construction.** Opening stops the scroll (either mode), cliffhanger withholds payoff, voice is consistent and tonally disciplined, scenes are built, dialogue carries the audio. May still have structural issues Deep Dive catches. **Directive:** advance to Deep Dive; if structure is sound, ship. **Anchors:**
- **Weapons / Draft Selection (MVS).** System-based, Mode A line-1 thumb-stop with 1P hook, escalating protagonist agency, consolidated bullies, micro-cliffhangers at scene breaks, full-reveal cliffhanger with consequences unwritten.
- **Princess Ebony (PE).** Romantasy, Mode B injustice-forward opening, consolidated antagonists, internal parallel voice (Nova), peak beats that breathe (the unanswered hand), cliffhanger AT the threshold (Boris reveal pending).
- **Supermarket / TBB.** Cold-open-then-flashback, RD-style humiliation thumb-stop, jade-pendant power discovery, peak landing in the body.

# EVALUATION PROCEDURE

## Step 1: Read cold.
Read the full script once without stopping. Note gut reactions — where you stopped paying attention, where you leaned in, where something felt off-show.

## Step 2: Run the Fast Path kill check.
Walk the seven kill checks. If any fires → skip to Step 6, cite the trigger, name the failing gate(s), stop.

## Step 3: Score the Quality Gate.
Score Opening, Cliffhanger, Writing Style & Voice (primaries), then Scene Quality and Dialogue Ratio (craft floors). Walk the triggers. **Evidence rule:** cite quoted script evidence only for dimensions that score ≤ 3 or = 5; functional 4s carry the score. MIN of the three primaries, adjusted by craft-floor pile-up, = bucket. Bucket < 3 = Key 1 FAIL.

## Step 4: Run the Show Fidelity Gate.
Read the world file's Essence block alongside the script. Check surface fidelity, all four Essence dimensions (+ Drift Tripwires), and tone-fit, on the landing-weighted gradient. **PASS / FAIL.** For any drift at issue, name the surface (surface / Essence-dimension / tone) and cite the specific contrast — what the show/Essence says vs. what the script delivers. A hard-FAIL condition = FAIL regardless of the bucket.

## Step 5: Adversarial pass — boundary only.
In writing **only when bucket ceiling = 3 or 4**, or when the fidelity gate is borderline. *Which dimension am I most likely overscoring, and why? Which PE anchor is closest on the ceiling-setting primary? Is the tone-fit call defensible against the show's actual register?* For a clear bucket and a clean fidelity call, run it mentally.

## Step 6: Compute and write the compressed diagnostic.
Apply the verdict logic, use the output format below, keep it tight.

---

# VERDICT LOGIC

```
QUALITY GATE (Key 1):  Bucket [1–5]   → PASS if ≥ 3, FAIL if < 3
FIDELITY GATE (Key 2): PASS / FAIL

SCRIPT VERDICT:
  PASS  — only if Quality Bucket ≥ 3 AND Fidelity = PASS
  FAIL  — if either gate fails. Name which gate(s) and why.
```

A FAIL is a FAIL even when the other gate is strong. A Bucket 5 script that fails tone-fit is a FAILED script with a clear reason. A fidelity-clean script at Bucket 2 is a FAILED script with a clear reason.

---

# OUTPUT FORMAT — THE DIAGNOSTIC

Write the diagnostic as **readable markdown — never inside a code block.** It should scan top to bottom: the verdict first, the scores at a glance, then each flaw stated at the macro level (what it is, why it costs us) with a concrete next action attached. The reader should be able to skim the headers and know the standing without parsing a grid.

Use this layout (these are real markdown headers/tables the analyst fills in, not a fenced block):

---

### [Title] · [Series]
**VERDICT: [PASS / FAIL]** — [if FAIL, name the gate(s) in plain words: "selling the wrong show (tone-fit)" / "execution not there yet (Bucket 2)" / "both gates"]

**Key 1 — Quality (CPI): Bucket [1–5] · [PASS/FAIL]  |  Key 2 — BOFU: [PASS/FAIL]**

**Why this shape:** the old single-block dump made the reader hunt for what mattered. This layout front-loads the verdict, lets the scores be skimmed in the table, and turns every flaw into a *what it is + why it hurts + what to do* unit so the operator always leaves with actionables, not just scores.

**Tone throughout:** direct, constructive, specific — a senior editor. No system jargon in the prose ("voice & tone," "Key 2 #1") — describe what the listener experiences. The "What's breaking" blocks are the part that gets shared with the writer; each should read naturally on its own and end with something they can act on.

# DISTRIBUTION TARGETS

Expected across a batch of ~10 scripts, Quality Gate:

- Bucket 5: 0–1 (rare by construction)
- Bucket 4: ~2
- Bucket 3: ~4 (the mode)
- Bucket 2: ~2–3
- Bucket 1: ~1

Fidelity FAILs are tracked separately and are **not** rare — show-truth (especially tone-fit) is where a large share of submitted scripts break, independent of craft. A high-craft batch can still have a wave of fidelity FAILs. If the Quality mean lands above 3.5, calibration is drifting up — reopen recent buckets against the PE anchors.

# RELATIONSHIP TO OTHER WORKFLOWS

Bucketing is **upstream** of Workflow 5 Deep Dive and is the script-level instrument for the two keys.

| Result | Next Step |
|--------|-----------|
| FAIL — Fidelity | Do NOT advance to Deep Dive. The show-truth break (or tone-fit break) is fixed first, or the script is reassigned. A clean bucket does not buy a pass. |
| FAIL — Quality (Bucket 1) | Do NOT advance. Rebuild or reassign. |
| FAIL — Quality (Bucket 2) | Determine if the writer can course-correct. If yes, notes + reattempt at W4. If no, rebuild from W2 with a different writer. |
| PASS — Bucket 3 | Issue notes. Writer revises. Re-bucket. If it moves to 4+, advance to Deep Dive. |
| PASS — Bucket 4 | Minor-fix notes. Advance to Deep Dive concurrently or after one polish pass. |
| PASS — Bucket 5 | Advance directly to Deep Dive. |

Bucketing does NOT replace Deep Dive. A passing script can still have structural failures (IM deserts, throughline drift, BECAUSE-chain breaks). Bucketing clears the *execution* gate (Key 1) and the *show-truth* gate (Key 2 #1). Deep Dive clears the *structural* gate. All must pass before a script ships.

# ADDING CALIBRATION ANCHORS

When a new script cleanly represents a bucket or a fidelity verdict, add it as a calibration anchor. Target 2–3 per bucket across different series and writers. Bucket 2 currently has no active anchor — prioritize one. Also begin a **Fidelity-FAIL anchor set** — the Dana choke-collar tone-fit case is the seed; add clean examples of each FAIL type (HARD BLOCK, spine fabrication, Essence bastardization, tone-fit break) as they appear.

**Market-validated anchors are the highest tier.** A script known to have scaled is the strongest possible reference point — it settles the calibration argument. Bank these explicitly and weight them above rubric reasoning.

**Fidelity-PASS anchors (subverted desire / lands-on-canon):**
- **Anchor:** Weakest Beast Tamer Gets All SSS Dragons (WBT) — *scaled in market.* Fidelity PASS. Ren's stated goal is to find the Half-Flower and tame a second beast, but the narrative subverts it: he keeps the weakest spore, fights with wits and his own body, and the payoff lands on the canon knowledge-awakening (the "blueprint" interface). The off-engine *want* is the legible hook; the on-engine *delivery* is the show. A textbook subverted-desire promo — do not mistake the hook for a break. (Earlier tooling miscalled this a fidelity FAIL by scoring the want instead of the delivery; that error is now guarded against above.)

```
**Anchor:** [Script Name] ([Series]). [Quality scores and/or fidelity verdict; note "scaled in market" if validated.] [1–2 sentences on why it exemplifies this.]
```

# NOTES ON EDGE CASES

**The clean-craft, wrong-show script (the Dana case):** Real characters, real setting, disciplined writing — but a premise the show doesn't support (torture/death-collar stakes on a non-dark show). Quality Gate may score 4–5. **Fidelity Gate FAILS on tone-fit. The script FAILS.** The listener installs for the show they heard and finds a different, darker thing — that is where the churn lives. Do not let the clean bucket talk you out of the FAIL.

**The subverted-desire promo (the Weakest Beast Tamer case) — PASS, not FAIL:** The protagonist chases an off-engine goal (Ren wants to tame a *second* beast via the Half-Flower) but the narrative redirects it onto the canon engine (he keeps the weakest spore and gains the knowledge awakening). The promo never *delivers* the off-engine thing — it uses the want as a legible hook and pays off the show's actual theme ("is it really the weakest, or does it harbor unlimited potential?"). **Fidelity PASS.** This is the mirror image of the Dana case and the more common analyst error: failing a script for a *desire a character states* rather than a *promise the promo delivers*. Score the delivery. This one scaled — the market settled it. (See the gate's "Score what the promo DELIVERS, not what a character WANTS.")

**The strong writer with bad instincts (Bucket 3):** Genuine craft, uncalibrated promo pacing — buries the lede, frontloads exposition, over-writes setup. Improves fast with targeted feedback and PE-anchor exposure.

**The Essence-drifted script with clean surface fidelity:** Uses real characters and settings, respects HARD BLOCKS, lands on-show — but the protagonist doesn't *feel* right (flattened voice, reversed combat, subtly wrong power source). Surface may pass; Essence drift through the landing FAILS the Fidelity Gate.

**The broad-to-specific script:** Opens on archetypal injustice, narrows into unmistakable show material by the final third. The gradient working as intended → Fidelity PASS. **But broad framing does not license Essence drift** — a hero-confident Quinn in the opening (when the Essence says defiant-runt) is an Essence problem even if the surface gradient is clean.

**The pre-show-timeline script (Essence voice calibration):** When a promo covers events BEFORE the protagonist enters the show's primary setting (Karl in the mines before the Academy, Quinn before awakening), score voice register against the Essence block's *identity description* (who the character IS) rather than the verbatim Voice Samples (how they sound in later contexts). Test: would the script's voice *plausibly become* the Samples once the character reaches the show? If yes → no drift. If it actively contradicts the identity → drift regardless of timeline.

**The borderline script:** When a script feels between two buckets, compare against the PE anchors on each primary. The directive follows the bucket, never the reverse.

**The script with strong craft floors but a weak primary:** The primary caps the bucket. All-5 floors with a 3 on Writing Style & Voice is Bucket 3.
