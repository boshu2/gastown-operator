## WHAT THE THREE PILLARS ARE

Every show is defined by three pillars: the core elements an audience is actually installing to experience. They are the show's promise, stated as content. The promo must thread all three or it advertises a show the listener won't recognize when they open the app (a "Woodward risk").

## HOW PILLARS ARE DETERMINED

Choose one pillar from each of these three categories. The categories guarantee the show is described across its full surface — the person, the place, and the engine — rather than three flavors of the same thing.

1. **Character stakes** — what the protagonist stands to lose or win as a person: the revenge, the family in danger, the rejection, the secret they must keep, the debt, the rescue.
2. **Setting** — the world the story lives inside: the academy, the patriarchal court, the apocalypse, the elite school, the monster kingdoms, the VR game world.
3. **Fantasy element / progression system / complication** — the genre engine: the power system, the System/interface, the cultivation method, the hidden legacy, the bloodline, the time-travel mechanic.

Order in the list below is the show's own ordering, not the category order — a pillar that reads first may be the fantasy element, not the character stakes. What matters is that all three categories are covered.

## RELATIONSHIP TO THE RELATABLE THRULINE (W2)

The pillars do double duty. As **content**, they are the fun the audience installs for (threaded via Promise Threading). Translated into a **universal human experience**, one of them becomes the promo's **Relatable Thruline** — the empathy nerve a cold viewer feels in the first 10 seconds. Examples: a Secret/Hidden Identity pillar → "everyone has had something they needed to keep hidden"; an underdog / class-system pillar → "everyone has been underestimated." See `workflow2_skeleton.md` → Spine → Relatable Thruline.
