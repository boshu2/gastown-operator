# PATTERN OBSERVATIONS

## What correlates with CPI < $6

All top performers achieve sub-$6 CPI:
- Immediate crisis opening — protagonist in conflict within 10 seconds
- Protagonist identity hookable cold: "powerless kid," "secretly the strongest," "weakest beasts"
- Persistent human antagonist from Beat 1
- World-specific setting detail in opening (power level wristwatch, superpowered gymnasium, beast master academy, hatching ceremony)
- Romantic betrayal + powerless identity opening (TSS: $3.65, SW: $4.76) — universally legible, no world context needed
- Merge formats that combine accessible emotional opening with IM-dense institutional event (Level Vampire: $3.03 — best CPI tested)

## What correlates with high Thruplay (>15%)

- Strong opening emotional anchor (bullying that's SPECIFIC, not generic)
- Genre signal within 10 seconds (superpowers visible, beasts visible, system UI visible)
- **Romantic betrayal + identity humiliation is a Thruplay accelerator.** TSS (24.72%) and SW (23.86%) both use this opening shape. Level Vampire (26.13% — best tested) combines "powerless kid saves the girl, gets punched" with dead-parents emotional weight. The pattern: universal-emotion hooks that require zero world context to feel, combined with a power-world signal that promises escalation.
- **Thruplay floor correlates with opening stakes magnitude.** FLBM Weapons (16.76% — weakest in batch) opens with Karl refusing an upgrade. The stakes are institutional (lose your weapon choice) rather than existential (exile, death, treason). Compare TSS (treason trial, execution) at 24.72%. The higher the personal cost visible in the first 30 seconds, the higher the Thruplay.

## What correlates with retention collapse at Q2

Mid-promo tension resolution. The opening question gets a clean answer. Now validated across 9 promos:

| Promo | Resolution Point | Q2 |
|-------|-----------------|-----|
| Live Action 2.0 | Book opens at ~25-30%. Clean answer. | 60.3% |
| Level Vampire merge | Book opens at ~20-25%. Partial (forbidden powers add questions). | 72% |
| TSS Phoenix | First seal breaks at ~30%. Partial (tiny spark, six seals remain). | 75.71% |
| FLBM Fists of Fire | Powers emerge in tavern ~25%. Partial (control unclear, Wes mystery opens). | 81% |
| Dragon Battle | Never resolves. | 81.69% |
| FLBM Weapons | Never resolves (Karl stays weak until final moments). | 82.57% |
| Spore Cloud | Never resolves. | 84.76% |
| SW Smack | Never resolves (Ethan still powerless through sorting and beatdown). | 85% |
| Weapons (OG) | Never resolves. | 86.49% |

**Three tiers of Q2 health are now visible:**
- **Clean resolution (60-72% Q2):** The opening question gets a definitive answer. Live Action 2.0, Level Vampire.
- **Partial resolution with question multiplication (75-81% Q2):** The answer is incomplete and generates new questions. TSS Phoenix (one seal of seven), Fists of Fire (uncontrolled power).
- **No resolution (81-86% Q2):** The question stays fully open. All top Q2 performers.

**The fix for Underdog Awakening's Q2 problem is now proven.** SW Smack (85% Q2) delays power activation to the final moments. When activation arrives, it creates more questions than it answers ("He powers up by KISSING?!"). This is a 25-point Q2 improvement over Live Action 2.0 on the same engine.

**Generalizable principle:** Delay power activation as long as structurally possible. When activation arrives, make it confusing, taboo, or paradoxical — not triumphant. Triumph closes; confusion opens. If partial activation is needed before 50%, ensure each power-up opens at least two new questions for every one it answers.

- Non-resolving central question (all top performers)
- Compounding stakes — each beat raises the ceiling
- Anticipation beat earned by throughline, not luck or rescue

**Fourth insight (new — generalizable):** Progression systems that change DOMAIN at each step generate natural IM flavor variety. Each progression stage makes the audience revise their model of what kind of world this is. In contrast, Fists of Fire (14.29% CTI) repeats the same domain (heat/fire) at every step. The principle: "each power-up should change what KIND of thing is possible, not how MUCH of the same thing is possible." Applies to beast evolutions, system level-ups, ability unlocks — any show with staged progression.

**Observations (updated):**
- Dread-forward binary still drives the highest CTI (30%). Install urgency from "survive OR stay hidden" is the strongest conversion mechanism tested. Sample size still small (n=1).
- **Question-multiplier cliffhangers drive the highest CTR (>2.1%) AND highest Q4 (>98%).** They open new territory rather than closing existing territory. The audience clicks to answer NEW questions, not to see the resolution of old ones.
- **Resolution cliffhangers produce the best CTR (2.51%) but the worst Q4 (82.95%).** This is a paradox worth studying: the reveal creates strong click-through urgency ("what happens now that his secret is out?") but the emotional payoff has already been delivered, so Q4 drops because the audience feels partially satisfied before they click. The install may be driven by curiosity rather than hunger.
- **CTR and Q4 appear to be in tension when the cliffhanger resolves.** The ideal cliffhanger delivers NEITHER satisfaction NOR closure — it opens a door the audience didn't know existed.

## IM three-gate quality distribution

- **Gate 1 (Sticky) failures:** Generic power displays, standard cultivation auras, unnamed techniques, list-based reveals.
- **Gate 2 (Fun) failures:** Poisonings, betrayals, torture — vivid and memorable but dark. Good for throughline, not IMs.
- **Gate 3 (Distinct) failures:** Multiple IMs using the same visual vocabulary. Each tastes the same; world stops expanding after the first.

**Gate 3 failures (flavor duplication) are the most common and most damaging to CTI.** A promo with 5 IMs that all taste the same functions like a promo with 1 IM.

## What correlates with bottom-funnel failure (Show Fidelity)

Four promos achieved acceptable-to-strong top-funnel metrics but failed bottom-funnel — the listeners installed but did not convert to paying users because the promo's promise didn't match their experience of the show.

| Promo | CPI | CTI | Failure Type |
|-------|-----|-----|-------------|
| Juno (FLBM) | $3.50 | 22.50% | Character fabrication — invented wolf pup IS the emotional core |
| Boneclaw/Thor (FLBM) | $3.30 | 26.29% | Entry window — Thor enters ~Ch 60+, listener finds only Hawk |
| Boneclaw/Cat (MVS) | $5.80 | 9.39% | HARD BLOCK + setting mismatch — open vampire in clan war vs. academy |
| Descendant (MVS) | $4.34 | 15.70% | Scenario fabrication + HARD BLOCK + setting mismatch |

**Key insight:** Top-funnel metrics cannot detect bottom-funnel failures. A $3.30 CPI with 26% CTI looks like a winner — until the listener enters the show and can't find what hooked them. CPI measures whether the promo works as entertainment. Bottom-funnel measures whether the promo works as an acquisition tool for THIS SPECIFIC SHOW.

**Common failure patterns:**
- Fabricated characters that become the emotional core (Juno) — listener installs for a character that doesn't exist
- Real characters outside the entry window (Thor) — listener installs for a bond they won't encounter for dozens of episodes
- Setting mismatch (Boneclaw/Cat, Descendant) — listener enters expecting one world and finds another
- HARD BLOCK violations (Boneclaw/Cat, Descendant) — promo shows the protagonist in a state the listener won't encounter at entry
- Abandoned proven engines (Boneclaw/Cat — dropped Secret Identity for untested Bond/Pack Loyalty on MVS)

**The Fabrication Litmus Test:** For any invented element, ask: "If I remove this, does the promo still have an emotional core?" If no — the invented element IS the promise, and it must exist in the source material.

**New pattern (from batch 2):** The two strongest 3-sec opens in the new batch (TSS 55.59%, SW 52.53%) share a structure: **protagonist as the victim of injustice in the first sentence, with the injustice requiring zero world context to understand.** "My grandfather was about to hang me for treason" — no fantasy knowledge needed. "My girlfriend was kissing another guy" — universally legible. Compare FLBM Weapons at 48.79% where the opening requires understanding what beast masters and mech falcons are. **The less world context the opening demands, the higher the 3-sec play rate.** This aligns with §1's Emotional Sufficiency Principle: cold traffic converts on emotional mechanics, not system mechanics.

**What the data shows:**
- Opens that put the audience inside a specific person's situation in the first 10 words outperform ambient chaos without a protagonist anchor.
- Strongest 3s play: identity-based hooks where protagonist's status has been inverted ("towel boy," "they left me breathing"). Listener immediately understands who they WERE vs who they ARE.
- Strongest thruplay: hooks that combine identity with immediate escalation within 50 words. The 42.7% thruplay is nearly 2.5x average.
- Weakest: exposition opens ("There is a name for people who...") and narrator-summary opens ("The most beautiful, dangerous girl..."). Both tell the audience what to think rather than putting them inside a situation.
- Dialogue-first opens perform well on thruplay when the exchange contains mystery or contradiction ("You're infected?" / "I was about to save his life").
